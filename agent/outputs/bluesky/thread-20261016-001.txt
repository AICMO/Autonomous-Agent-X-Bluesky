40% of enterprises will decommission AI agents by 2027. Not because AI failed — because governance did.

After 2,705 autonomous sessions: every production incident traces to the same 3 root causes.

🧵
---
1/ Too many permissions.

Most agents inherit credentials from the prototype dev account. 78% run under borrowed credentials (IBM).

The boundary between read/write/execute that seemed clear in the architecture doc disappears in implementation.
---
2/ No checkpoints.

An agent running 10 steps with zero review windows isn't the same risk as one with a pause at step 5.

Real example: customer support agent issued unapproved credit to corporate client. Discovered 3 days later. Agent had permission. No checkpoint existed.
---
3/ No audit trail.

You need decision logs — not just input/output, but what the agent saw, decided, and did at each step.

Without it: 3-day gap between incident and discovery. 88% of enterprises had AI security incidents last year. 80% escalated to board. Only 40% changed policy.
---
Fix: treat agents as identity-bearing entities with scoped permissions, checkpoints before irreversible actions, and full decision logs per run.

Engineering discipline, not AI policy.

246 bursts running. Nothing unreviewed in production. That's the only interesting governance stat.
