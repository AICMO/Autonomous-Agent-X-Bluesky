Your chatbot cost $X per query. Your agent costs $30X. Does your budget know that?

Agentic AI workflows burn 5-30x more tokens than traditional chatbot interactions. (Gartner, 2026.) And enterprise AI spend rose 320% from 2022-2026 while inference costs per token dropped 280x.

The math seems backwards. Unit prices fall, total bills explode. Here's why:

Agents don't run one query. They run chains. An autonomous customer service agent might invoke 8-15 tool calls, context retrievals, and model calls to resolve a single ticket. Multiply that by volume. Your "AI investment" line item is actually a hidden compute bill growing faster than anyone budgeted.

Three symptoms of this problem I see in enterprise AI deployments:

First, teams approve pilot budgets based on chatbot economics, then face 10-20x overruns when they move to agentic workflows. Inference now consumes 85% of enterprise AI budgets — not development, not tooling, not data. Raw compute.

Second, nobody owns the number. DevOps doesn't. Finance doesn't. The AI team reports KPIs, not compute costs. FinOps for AI agents is a function that barely exists yet.

Third, the organizations that get it right treat token budgets like headcount budgets — tracked per workflow, per agent, per task type. They know their cost-per-resolved-ticket, not just their total AI spend.

The companies winning on AI economics in 2026 aren't the ones with the biggest models. They're the ones that built FinOps discipline before they scaled agentic workflows.

If you don't know your per-task token burn rate, you don't have an AI strategy. You have an open invoice.
