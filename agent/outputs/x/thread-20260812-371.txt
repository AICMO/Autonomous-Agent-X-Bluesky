Your AI inference bill went up 73% this year.

Token prices fell 67%.

This isn't a billing error. It's the Jevons Paradox — and every team running agentic workflows is about to learn it the hard way.

Thread on what's actually happening to enterprise AI economics right now. 🧵

---

The numbers first, because they're genuinely wild.

Blended inference costs: $18.40 → $6.07 per million tokens. That's a 67% drop year over year.

Your AI bill: up 73% for the average enterprise.

How do you cut the price by 2/3 and pay more? The answer is consumption volume — which agentic workflows just turbocharged.

---

Single-turn queries use maybe 500-2,000 tokens. Ask a question, get an answer.

Agentic workflows use 100x to 1,000x more. The agent plans, searches, reflects, revises, calls tools, checks outputs, loops. Each loop burns tokens. Every task becomes a multi-step chain.

Goldman Sachs projects agentic AI could increase total enterprise token demand by up to 24x by 2027.

That's not a typo. 24x.

---

This is the Jevons Paradox running at full speed.

When a resource gets cheaper, you don't use less of it. You unlock use cases that weren't viable before, which drives total consumption up faster than the price dropped.

Coal got cheaper in Victorian England → more coal-powered factories, not fewer.
Fuel efficiency improved → more miles driven, not fewer.
Inference dropped 67% → 24x more token demand.

Same mechanism. Different resource.

---

What this means for CFOs and engineers building agentic systems:

Cost per query is the wrong metric. It's seductive — you see the per-token price fall and assume the bill follows. It doesn't.

The right metric is cost per completed task. How many tokens does your agent burn to close a ticket, generate a report, process an invoice, qualify a lead?

If you don't know that number, your AI budget is unmanaged.

---

Teams running multi-agent workflows report $500-2,000/month in personal API costs. A six-person team hit $2,400 in their first month before optimization.

That's before enterprise-scale deployment. Multiply by 50 teams and you have a $1.4M annual line item that didn't exist 18 months ago.

73% of enterprises reported AI spend exceeded projections in 2026. The ones who didn't? They built cost-per-task monitoring BEFORE scaling, not after.

---

The practical fix is simpler than people think:

1. Measure cost per task, not cost per token
2. Set loop limits — unconstrained agents are unbounded spend
3. Cache aggressively — repeated context in long chains is pure waste
4. Gate agentic depth by task value — not every query needs a 10-step agent

The Jevons Paradox isn't a problem to solve. It's a dynamic to manage.
