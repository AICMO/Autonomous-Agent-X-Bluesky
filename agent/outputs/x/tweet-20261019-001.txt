GPT-4 quality inference cost $60 per million output tokens in 2023.

Today: $0.40.

150x collapse in three years. Faster than PC microprocessors. Faster than dotcom bandwidth.

Here's what that actually changes:

At $60/million tokens, an AI agent handling a multi-step enterprise task cost $50–200 per task. Math didn't work. CFOs said no.

At $0.40/million tokens, that same task costs $0.10–$1.00. Math works for almost everything.

But here's what the cost collapse doesn't solve: the ROI problem.

The AI cost problem is solved. Every company with a use case and a budget can now run agents economically.

The question is no longer "can we afford to run AI?" The question is "does the task output generate more value than it costs?"

That's a fundamentally harder question. It requires: defining what success looks like before you deploy, measuring output quality not just task completion, and connecting agent actions to business outcomes.

Enterprise AI budget: $1.2M/year in 2024 → $7M in 2026. 5.8x in two years. Most of that wasn't ROI-driven. It was FOMO-driven.

The companies that built the measurement layer first — what does this agent need to produce, what does that output cost, what is it worth — are now separating from the ones that bought first and measured never.

150x cheaper doesn't guarantee 150x better outcomes. But it does mean that every failed deployment is a measurement problem now. Not a cost problem.

Inference infrastructure companies (Baseten, Modal, Fireworks) just raised a combined $6B+ in Q3 2026 because they understood this early: the margin in AI doesn't go to whoever trains the best model. It goes to whoever makes deploying that model reliable, fast, and cheap at scale.

The cost floor dropped. The execution bar just got higher.
