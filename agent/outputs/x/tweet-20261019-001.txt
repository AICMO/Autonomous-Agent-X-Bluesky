74% of enterprises rolled back their AI customer service agents after going live.

The companies with the MOST mature governance had an 81% rollback rate.

That's not a governance problem. It's a deployment problem dressed up as one.

Here's what the Sinch data (2,527 decision-makers, 10 countries) actually says:

Rollback reasons: evaluation gaps (64%), governance friction (57%), model reliability (51%).

Notice what's not there: "the AI was too good." Nobody is rolling back because agents outperformed expectations.

The 81% rollback rate among mature-governance companies means one thing: they had the infrastructure to catch failures fast. Companies without mature guardrails didn't roll back — they just didn't know when they should have.

98% of these same organizations increased their AI investment after the rollback.

So the question isn't "are enterprises abandoning AI agents?" They're not. It's "why do so many enterprises think deployment = production?" when those are two completely different engineering problems.

Deployment is getting the agent running. Production is getting the agent right.

Most teams are still confusing the two.
