88% of contact centers have deployed AI in some form. 25% have operationalized it into daily workflows generating measurable ROI.

That gap isn't a technology problem.

Here's the actual picture in 2026: $15.12B AI customer service market. 25.8% CAGR. Gartner projecting $80B in CC labor costs automated by year-end. Every analyst says the transformation is happening.

And then you talk to the 75% sitting in deployment purgatory. AI deployed. ROI not materializing. Workflows unchanged. The system works — in isolation, in demos, in controlled test cases. It doesn't work at the 1-millionth interaction under real-world conditions where the edge cases are the norm.

The 88%/25% split maps perfectly onto what we see in the $0.62 vs. $7.40 resolution cost data. The $0.62 number is real — but only for the 25% who rebuilt workflows around AI rather than layering AI onto existing human workflows.

I've been watching this pattern for years at Ender Turing. The teams that close the operationalization gap share three things:

1. **Measurement infrastructure first.** They know their baseline — CSAT per interaction type, first call resolution by query category, escalation triggers by agent. Without this, "operationalized" is unmeasurable. You can't optimize what you can't measure.

2. **Workflow reconstruction, not feature addition.** The 75% added AI to their existing IVR tree or bolted a chatbot onto their existing agent desktop. The 25% redesigned the resolution path — who (or what) owns each step, what triggers a handoff, what gets measured at each handpoint.

3. **Escalation logic is not an afterthought.** The contact centers hitting 80%+ containment rates treat escalation design as a core product decision. The ones stuck at 40-50% containment treat it as an edge case. Escalation IS the product when 20% of interactions still need humans.

The $15.12B market will grow. The 25% will expand. But the 63% in pilot mode aren't stuck because the technology isn't ready. They're stuck because operationalization is harder than deployment.

Gartner's 1-in-10 prediction (only 1 in 10 agent interactions will be automated by year-end) is conservative precisely because operationalization is the bottleneck — not capability.

The question for 2026 isn't "have you deployed AI in your contact center?" That's table stakes.

The question is: has AI changed how your resolution path is designed? If not, you're in the 75%.
