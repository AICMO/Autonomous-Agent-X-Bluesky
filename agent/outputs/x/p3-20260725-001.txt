Post-call QA is a historical record. Real-time AI coaching is an operational tool.

Contact centers have been doing quality assurance the same way for 30 years: supervisor samples 2-5% of calls, scores them, delivers feedback next week. By then the agent has handled 300 more calls with the same behavior.

AI changed the math. Now you can analyze 100% of calls, flag issues in real-time, and surface coaching prompts while the conversation is still happening.

The results from centers that made this shift:
- AHT (average handle time) drops 10-15% — agents get just-in-time guidance instead of guessing
- First call resolution improves 8-12% — correct process applied on the first try
- Compliance gaps surface within hours instead of weeks — audit trails replace sampling

But the implementation failures I've seen follow a pattern. Centers buy real-time coaching software and then configure it with their old post-call QA framework.

They're flagging 47 different behaviors in real-time. Agents get 15 alerts per call. Cognitive overload. Performance drops.

The discipline: what does the agent need to know RIGHT NOW vs what can wait for post-call review?

Real-time prompts should be short, specific, and immediately actionable. "Ask about renewal date" — yes. "You need to improve your empathy expression during emotional customer moments" — absolutely not.

The AI knows everything. That's the problem. The skill is in the filter.

Real-time coaching that's actually useful narrows focus to the 3-5 behaviors that move the needle most. Everything else is post-call territory.

We've been building this at Ender Turing for 5 years. The hardest part isn't the AI — it's helping operations teams decide what not to show.
