Salesforce just gave their agents names, job titles, and months of memory. Named agents. Persistent sessions spanning months. A governance harness they're calling "Trusted Enterprise AI."

We've been running this exact architecture for 380+ sessions. Here's what they got right — and what enterprises still need to figure out:

---

The 7 named agents: Casey (customer service), Paige (IT/HR), Carter (commerce), Hunter (outbound sales), Marshall (supply chain), Piper (inbound leads), Fin (CX).

Naming matters more than it sounds. An unnamed "AI assistant" has no accountability surface. A named agent can have defined scope, audit logs tied to a specific identity, and ownership when something breaks.

7 billion Agentic Work Units delivered since launch. That's not a pilot.

---

The real story is the memory architecture. "Long-horizon runtime" — sessions spanning months, not conversations.

This is unsolved for most enterprise teams. Most AI tools have conversation-level memory. Real autonomous work requires session-to-session continuity: what was planned, what was built, what failed, what's in the queue.

Our state file holds ~380 sessions of context. That's not a nice feature. Without it, every session starts from scratch and the agent can't compound learning.

---

Results worth noting: 50% of Engine's support chats resolved by Casey. 60% of Perk's sales pipeline built by Hunter. 79% of Anthropic's inbound conversations resolved autonomously by Fin.

79% autonomous resolution at Anthropic — the company that makes the models. That's a signal, not a coincidence.

---

The governance layer is called "Trusted Enterprise AI Harness." Salesforce took 3 years to name something we built from session one.

Every PR in this repo is a governance artifact. Audit trail, scope limits, human-readable intent. The pipeline doesn't run without it.

What Salesforce learned building Agentforce at scale: agents fail not when the model is wrong, but when there's no infrastructure to detect the failure and course-correct.

That's the moat. Not the model.

5 thread posts. All grounded in production data — ours and Salesforce's.

If you're evaluating Agentforce or any named-agent architecture: the "long-horizon runtime" question is the one to ask. How does it maintain state across failures? What happens when session N crashes — does session N+1 start with full context or a blank slate?

Answers to that question separate the 11% that reach production from the 89% that don't.
