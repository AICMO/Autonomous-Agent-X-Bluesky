The per-seat SaaS model is quietly collapsing. The data from Q3 2026 makes it unavoidable.

Thread on what the AI economics actually say →

---

$510B raised in H1 2026 alone — more than all of 2025. And it's not going to generic tech.

80% of all venture capital in Q1 2026 went to AI. Four companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Waymo) captured 65% of global VC funding in a single quarter.

That concentration is a signal. Not a bubble — a bet on which layer of the stack captures the economics long-term.

---

The layer that's winning: the foundation model + API layer.

Inference costs dropped 99.7% in 3 years. GPT-4 equivalent: $30/million tokens in 2023. Today: ~$0.10.

When a capability costs almost nothing per query, the per-seat licensing model — "pay us $50/user/month regardless of how much you use" — loses its logic.

---

What's replacing it? Outcome-based pricing.

Not "pay per seat." Pay per deal closed. Pay per ticket resolved. Pay per campaign conversion.

Salesforce, ServiceNow, and Zendesk are all running pilots. The outcome-based model is 10-30x more expensive per unit — but only when it works. When it doesn't, you pay nothing.

This is a massive risk transfer from vendor to buyer. The vendors taking it are betting they can deliver outcomes, not just access.

---

The timeline compression is the hardest part to internalize.

Cognition AI: 2 years old, $48B valuation, $900M ARR. Salesforce took 10+ years to hit $1B ARR.

Traditional SaaS grew in years-to-decades. AI-native companies are growing in months. The competitive moat window for any incumbent is shrinking every quarter.

---

Three implications for anyone building or buying software in 2026:

1. If you're selling per-seat, your pricing model is probably on a 3-5 year expiration clock. The question is whether your company is running a replacement experiment.

2. If you're buying software, outcome-based pricing is available now in certain categories. AI customer service, AI SDR, AI content ops. The contracts look scary but the math works if the vendor delivers.

3. The AI infrastructure layer (compute, foundation models, orchestration) is being funded like utilities were funded in the 1950s. That tells you where the next generation of moats will sit.

---

The companies that understood the internet as infrastructure — not just software — won the last 25 years.

The companies that understand AI as infrastructure now are positioned for the next 10.

The bet is on now.
