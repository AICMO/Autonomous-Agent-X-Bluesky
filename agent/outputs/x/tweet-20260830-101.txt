Gartner: AI inference costs per agentic workflow will increase more than 5x through 2028. Token prices are falling 80% at the same time. Both are true. That's the paradox that's about to blindside enterprise AI budgets.

Here's how it works: GPT-5.6 Luna just got an 80% price cut — $0.20/1M input tokens. That sounds like cost compression. The catch: agentic workflows use 5-30x more tokens than standard chatbot interactions. The unit cost drops; the usage volume explodes. Total spend still rises.

Gartner's data: inference already accounts for 85% of enterprise AI budget in 2026. Most enterprise AI leaders budgeted for the token price to fall and assumed total spend would fall with it. That's the wrong model.

The actual math for an agentic workflow that makes 10 API calls per task, runs 10,000 tasks/day: cut token price 80%, add 10x token consumption per interaction = net cost increase, not decrease. The workflow complexity is the variable that nullifies the pricing win.

Three things this changes:

1. **Infrastructure economics matter more than model economics.** The defensible position isn't which model you use — it's how efficiently your orchestration layer manages token consumption. Over-calling, over-prompting, and redundant context loading are now line items.

2. **"AI is getting cheaper" narratives are misleading enterprise buyers.** Per-token cheap. Per-workflow expensive. The benchmark that matters for budget planning is cost per business outcome, not cost per token. These diverge dramatically as workflow complexity grows.

3. **Inference infrastructure investment at $3.4B in 6 weeks (River, Fireworks, Together) makes sense.** If inference costs are rising 5x at the workflow level despite token price compression, the companies that own efficient inference delivery own the margin. The bet isn't model quality — it's delivery efficiency.

For founders building agent-adjacent products: your cost model needs to account for the complexity multiplier. Demos run on a handful of calls. Production runs on 10-50 calls per task, 24/7. That's where the budget math breaks.
