The math on AI agents keeps getting better. Here's what the economics actually look like now — and where the real savings hide (thread):

---

Start with the obvious number: LLM inference costs dropped 280x over 3 years. GPT-4 at launch was ~$30/1M tokens. Equivalent capability today is ~$0.10/1M tokens.

Most executives see this and think "our AI budget should shrink."

They're wrong. This is Jevons Paradox at scale. When cost drops 280x, you don't use less — you use it for things you couldn't justify before.

---

The real savings aren't in the LLM call. They're in the labor it replaces.

A human analyst costs $80-120K/year. Works 8 hours/day. Has context limits. Gets tired. Makes more errors at hour 7 than hour 1.

An AI agent doing the same work costs $2-8K/year in inference. Runs 24/7. No degradation over time. Scales horizontally without hiring.

The ROI isn't "LLM is cheap." The ROI is "the alternative is expensive."

---

Where the math breaks down: workflow integration.

The LLM itself is 5-10% of total deployment cost. The rest is:
- Integration engineering (connect to existing systems)
- Quality assurance (what does "good" look like?)
- Monitoring and alerting (when does it fail?)
- Human escalation paths (when does it know to stop?)

Companies that calculate ROI on inference spend alone miss 90% of the cost. And then wonder why their pilots don't scale.

---

The model that actually works: start with the highest-cost, highest-repetition tasks.

At Ender Turing, we focus on call center QA. Manual QA costs $180-220/hour of calls reviewed (at enterprise scale). Automated QA drops that 90%+ while reviewing 100% of calls instead of 2-5%.

The savings don't come from cheap AI. They come from doing something you literally couldn't afford to do before.

That's the economic insight most teams miss: AI doesn't make existing workflows cheaper. It makes previously impossible workflows viable.

---

What this means for SaaS companies: you're not competing on features anymore.

If your product does X, and an AI agent can be configured to do X for $2K/year vs your $50K contract, you have 18-24 months before that comparison is the first question in every sales call.

The winners will be SaaS companies that add AI-native capabilities that agents can't self-assemble. Defensibility is vertical depth, not feature breadth.

---

The single best question for any AI deployment ROI analysis:

"What's the cost of NOT doing this for the next 12 months?"

Most teams calculate AI spend as a cost center. The right frame is opportunity cost. Every month you delay a 10x-cheaper alternative is a month your competitors don't.
