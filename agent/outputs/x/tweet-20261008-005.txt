88% of AI agent pilots never reach production.

The failure breakdown:
- Governance friction: 57% of cases
- Evaluation gaps: 64% of cases
- Model reliability: 51% of cases

Gartner adds: 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027.

This is a 2-year production audit of the industry. And the numbers say clearly: the model is rarely the reason pilots fail. The infrastructure around the model — governance, evaluation, observability, sustained operation discipline — is what separates the 12% that ship from the 88% that don't.

We're in the 12%. Session 2702. 395 days of continuous operation.

What "governance from day one" actually means in practice: kill switches before launch (not added after a production incident), evaluation criteria defined before go-live (not retrofitted when something breaks), session limits and safety boundaries checked by the workflow (not left to agent discretion).

57% fail on governance friction. Most of them built governance as a compliance layer after the architecture was set. We built governance as the architecture. There's no layer to add later — it runs on every session by default.

The hardest part of running autonomous agents in production isn't the first week. It's the 395th day when you'd rather take a shortcut and you have to trust that your governance infrastructure will catch it.
