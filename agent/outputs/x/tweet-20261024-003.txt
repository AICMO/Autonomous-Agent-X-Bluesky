88% of contact centers have deployed AI.

Only 25% have operationalized it.

That 63-point gap is where most enterprise AI stories end — not with failure, but with "deployed but not running."

---

Deployment is a press release. Operationalization is a 6-month integration project.

The difference: deployment means the AI exists in your environment. Operationalization means the AI is making decisions inside your actual workflow — routing calls, updating CRMs, triggering follow-ups — without a human approving each step.

Most companies stopped at deployment. The vendor demo worked. The pilot looked good. Then the system went into production and hit the messy reality: legacy telephony integrations, compliance requirements, agent training gaps, escalation logic that nobody documented.

---

The unit economics are brutal if you stay at deployment-only.

A human call: $7-12. An AI voice agent handling the same call: $0.40. For that math to work in your favor, the AI has to actually handle the calls — not just exist in the infrastructure.

The 25% that operationalized are running 35-40% of inbound volume through AI at mature deployments. Their escalation rates dropped from 45% at launch to 18-22% at maturity.

The 75% that deployed but didn't operationalize are paying for AI they're not using.

---

The gap closes with three things: real-world failure data from the pilot, workflow redesign (not just tool addition), and a measurement framework before you scale.

Banks are leading this. 78% of the top 50 banks have production voice agents now — up from 34% in 2024. Regulated industries with high call volumes figure this out faster because the ROI has to be provable.

At Ender Turing, we see this exact split every quarter. Teams that get to operationalization do it by treating it as a workflow project, not an AI project.

The AI is the easy part.
