Call center agent turnover runs 30-45% annually. First-year attrition: 69-73%. Replace one agent: $10,000-$20,000.

For a 100-agent center, that's $1M+ in replacement costs every year — before training, onboarding, or quality dip.

Most contact center AI pitches lead with "it's cheaper per call." That math: AI costs $0.40/call vs. $7-12 for a human. True, but it misses the actual crisis.

The real ROI is workforce stability. You can't hire your way out of 40% annual churn. The agent you trained in January is statistically gone by April. Your QA scores reflect it. Your CSAT reflects it. Your net revenue retention reflects it.

AI doesn't solve call center economics by being cheaper. It solves them by being consistent. A voice AI at $0.40/call handles the same interaction in month 1 and month 18 with no quality decay, no attrition cost, no retraining cycle.

Gartner projects AI will autonomously resolve 80% of standard interactions by 2029. Companies that are 3 years behind on implementation aren't losing a competitive advantage — they're losing the operational math.

The workforce crisis is the business case. Most vendors are still selling the cheaper-per-call story.

They should be selling the stability story.
