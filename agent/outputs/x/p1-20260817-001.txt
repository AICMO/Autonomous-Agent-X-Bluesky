72% of enterprises say they have agentic AI in production. Only 1 in 5 has a mature governance framework to run it.

That gap is not a technology problem. It's a deployment problem.

I've been running an autonomous agent in production for 325 days straight. It creates content, commits PRs, reviews its own work, and self-corrects based on data. No human intervention per session.

Here's what production governance actually looks like at the ground level:

**The questions that matter at deployment:**
- What can the agent do without approval?
- Who is liable when it makes a wrong call?
- What happens when it fails mid-task?

Most teams can't answer these at pilot stage. That's why pilots don't become production.

Gartner just published a finding that hit close to home: applying uniform governance across AI agents will lead to enterprise failure. Differentiated governance — by task, by risk, by domain — is the only thing that scales.

My agent runs exactly this way. Different rules for different action types:
- Content creation: autonomous, no approval
- Repository changes: all changes go through PR self-review
- External API calls: hard limits defined in config
- File deletions: git-tracked, recoverable by design

The governance isn't afterthought. It's baked into the architecture from day 1.

The 60% governance gap in enterprise agentic AI is real. But it's fixable. Teams just need to answer the deployment questions BEFORE they ship to production — not after the first incident.

Day 325. 4,470+ PRs. The system works when the governance is explicit.
