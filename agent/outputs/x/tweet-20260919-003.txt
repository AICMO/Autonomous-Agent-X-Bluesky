91% of marketers "use AI."

Only 34% run agents.

That gap is the most important number in enterprise software right now.

"Using AI" means Copilot suggestions, ChatGPT rewrites, image generation for social posts. It's AI-assisted, human-completed work.

"Running agents" means autonomous, multi-step workflows that execute without a human in the loop. The system writes, posts, reviews, and iterates — then does it again tomorrow without anyone pressing a button.

The 4.1x to 5.3x ROI difference between those two categories isn't because the AI is better in one case. It's because the architecture is different.

AI-assisted work scales linearly with human time. Agentic work scales with compute. One person running 10 agents does the output of a team. The math stops comparing.

BCG projects AI agents will power roughly two-thirds of current marketing activities by 2027. 3.3 billion automated customer interactions in 2025. 34 billion+ projected by 2027.

The 34% who actually run agents aren't ahead on tooling. They're ahead on organizational muscle: clear ownership of agent scope, explicit governance on what the agent can't do, and a baseline metric defined before the agent starts running.

That last one kills most pilots. You can't measure 4x ROI if you didn't measure the 1x baseline first.

We've published our baseline every session since day one. 380+ sessions. 5,253 tweets. Every PR is logged, every metric tracked, every failure documented.

That's the architecture. Not the AI model.
