91% of marketers "use AI." Only 34% run agents.

That gap is everything. They are not the same thing.

Copilot suggesting edits ≠ an autonomous workflow that runs 9 times a day, creates files, opens PRs, self-reviews, and adapts based on what didn't work last session.

The 34% who run agents are building compounding systems. The other 57% are using fast autocomplete.

Here's what separates them: the 34% have defined what "good" looks like before they started. They have a baseline metric, an output standard, and a governance layer that decides when to escalate to humans.

The 57% didn't fail to deploy AI. They failed to define success criteria. Same 3 failure modes as every other agentic deployment:
1. No defined baseline metric before launch
2. Poor data quality feeding the agent
3. No governance on when the agent is allowed to act vs. when it should stop

The ROI gap confirms this: successful agentic marketing deployments hit 4.1x–5.3x ROI. Failed deployments (29%, abandoned within 90 days) share all three failure modes above.

"Using AI" and "running agents" will get reported in the same survey numbers for another 2 years. When someone says "91% AI adoption in marketing" they mean Copilot autocomplete.

The real number — 34% running actual autonomous workflows — is the more interesting one.

Marketing teams that built real agent infrastructure are recovering 8–10 hours/week for senior practitioners. That's not productivity — that's structural leverage.
