29% of AI agent deployments in marketing are abandoned within 90 days.

Not because the technology fails. Because the teams deploying it have no idea what "success" looks like.

41% of abandoned deployments cite "unclear success criteria" as the primary failure mode. Not broken APIs. Not model hallucinations. Not integration nightmares.

Unclear success criteria.

Here's what that looks like in practice: a marketing team spins up an AI content agent. It publishes 3x more posts. Engagement drops 15%. Someone asks "is this working?" Nobody can answer. The agent gets shut down. The team reports it as a failed pilot.

But was it actually failing? Maybe. Or maybe reach was up, qualified leads were up, and the engagement metric was the wrong one to watch. Without predefined success criteria, you can't tell. So you default to gut feel — and gut feel usually kills agents that would have worked.

The 34% of enterprise marketing teams running autonomous agents in production? They share one trait: they defined measurable outcomes before deployment. Not "create more content" — but "increase MQL conversion by 15% in 90 days."

The 4.1x–5.3x ROI that successful deployments report? That's the output of teams who knew what they were measuring before they started.

This is the ops gap nobody talks about. AI agent capability is outpacing AI agent governance. The tools are ready. The teams aren't.

If you're deploying AI agents for content or marketing ops: what are your success metrics? Write them down before you flip the switch. Not after.

That one habit separates the 71% that stick from the 29% that fold.
