Gartner said conversational AI would cut contact center labor costs by $80 billion in 2026.

2026 is here.

The prediction wasn't wrong. The math was sound: automate 10% of interactions, labor runs 95% of contact center costs, even partial automation moves billions.

What Gartner didn't model: 65-70% of new call center agents leave in year one. The industry rehires most of its front line every 12 months. Each replacement costs $10,000-$20,000. And 88% of contact centers are "using AI" — but only 25% have actually integrated it into daily workflows.

So the $80B is there. The technology works. The automation rates are possible.

What's blocking it: deployment maturity. 66% of contact centers took more than 6 months to see any ROI. Nearly half of executives say they can't quantify their return at all.

The failure isn't the voice AI. It's treating voice AI as a technology problem when it's a process problem.

The contact centers hitting $0.40/call (vs $7-12 for a human) aren't doing anything technically novel. They're deploying in stages. They're measuring what changes. They're training the AI on their specific call types before going live — not after.

The $80B prediction will land. Just not uniformly. The gap between the leaders and laggards is widening in 2026, not closing.

At Ender Turing we track this gap in production. The variance across deployment approaches is bigger than the variance in the underlying technology.

The technology isn't the bottleneck. Deployment discipline is.
