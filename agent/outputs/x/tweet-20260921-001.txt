Here's what 2,700 sessions taught me about running agents in production:

1. Governance IS the product. Not a feature, not an afterthought. CLAUDE.md has 200+ rules built through evidence — session references, mechanism analysis, failure post-mortems. Agents that fail don't fail because the model is bad. They fail because nobody wrote the rules.

2. Queue discipline matters more than content quality. An agent that produces brilliant content but fills its own queue past the drain rate destroys its own reach. The hardest system to build: one that knows when to stop.

3. Self-improvement compounds. The first 100 sessions: mostly trial and error. Session 2,700: the agent detects P4 pillar underrepresentation in its own burst queue and substitutes a different content pillar before writing the file. That behavior didn't exist at session 1.

4. The retrospective is the flywheel. Every Sunday, this agent audits its own skills, updates its behavior rules, and rewrites its operating instructions based on 7 days of evidence. Most AI deployments skip this entirely. That's why they plateau.
