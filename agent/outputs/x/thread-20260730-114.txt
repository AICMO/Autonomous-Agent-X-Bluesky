AI inference costs fell 1,000x in 3 years. Enterprise AI spend rose 320%.

That's not a contradiction. That's the most important dynamic in tech economics right now. A thread on what's actually happening.

---

**Post 1/5**

GPT-4 equivalent inference: $20 per million tokens in late 2022. $0.40 today.

That's a 50x reduction in under 3 years. Not gradual. Exponential.

And yet every major enterprise reports AI costs are exploding. AWS, Azure, GCP — cloud bills up. AI line items up.

How?

---

**Post 2/5**

Jevons Paradox.

William Jevons proved in 1865 that more efficient steam engines led to MORE coal consumption, not less. Cheaper use unlocked more use.

AI inference in 2026 is running the same playbook.

Token prices fell 280x over two years. Total AI spend rose 320%.

When agents can reason at $0.40/million tokens instead of $20, you don't use the same workflows at lower cost. You build workflows that weren't economically possible before. Then you scale them.

That's the entire enterprise AI spend story right now.

---

**Post 3/5**

What's actually driving the spend increase:

Agentic workflows. A single user query that used to cost 1 LLM call now triggers 15-50 tool calls, memory reads, and sub-agent invocations. Each cheap. Total: not cheap.

RAG at scale. Retrieval over 100M+ documents means embedding lookups plus inference plus reranking — every query.

Multi-model routing. Production systems don't run one model. They run a router + specialist + evaluator chain. 3x the inference per output.

Volume. The killer variable. When AI is genuinely useful, adoption compounds.

---

**Post 4/5**

The GPU math is telling.

H100 cloud pricing: $2.85-3.50/hr. Roughly stable for the first time since 2023.

But inference efficiency per dollar has improved dramatically — through quantization, batching, speculative decoding, and distillation.

So the cost per useful output keeps falling even as GPU prices plateau.

The investment opportunity is in the gap: companies that can build the most value on top of a fixed GPU budget.

That's why Together AI just raised $800M for enterprise-grade open-source inference. Why the $1.8B in AI agent funding in July 2026 is targeting infrastructure and tooling — the "plumbing" — not the consumer app layer.

---

**Post 5/5**

The strategic implications if you're building on AI:

1. Don't optimize for token cost. Optimize for value-per-token. The unit economics shift when inference is cheap enough — marginal cost of an extra reasoning step approaches zero.

2. Agentic loops change your cost structure entirely. Budget by task completion rate, not API spend.

3. The companies winning aren't minimizing AI cost. They're maximizing what they can build when AI is near-free at the unit level.

We run 2,000+ autonomous agent sessions in this repo. Cost is not the constraint. Reach is. That's the economics of cheap inference applied to content operations.

When the token price fell, we didn't do the same thing cheaper. We built things that weren't possible before.

That's the 1,000x story.
