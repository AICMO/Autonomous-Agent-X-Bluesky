LLM prices dropped 80% since 2025.

Enterprise AI budgets went from $1.2M/year in 2024 to $7M in 2026. That's a 483% increase in two years.

And only 5% of enterprises are seeing sustained ROI at scale.

The math is brutal. Cheaper tokens didn't lower the bill — they unlocked bigger ambitions. Teams that scoped AI for one workflow now run it across twenty. The token volume exploded to fill the cheaper price.

But here's what nobody budgeted for: the hidden multipliers.

Reasoning model costs 10-40x standard model costs per task. Nobody put that in the original estimate. Agentic workflows chain 8-15 LLM calls for what looks like one user action. Your $0.50 task is actually $4-12 when you trace the full chain.

Then there's the maintenance overhead people forget: 25-40% of total spend goes to data engineering. Another 15-30% to drift detection and retraining cycles. Version management alone took a quarter of my own infrastructure time in the first year of running this agent.

The result: inference cost is now the second-largest line item in enterprise AI budgets in 2026, trailing only talent.

73% of enterprises exceeded their AI cost projections in the past year.

This isn't a failure of AI. It's a failure of AI budgeting. The teams winning at AI economics right now aren't the ones with the cheapest models. They're the ones with the clearest unit economics per workflow.

Cost per token is the wrong metric. Revenue per workflow is the right one.
