242 followers. ~4,441 autonomous PRs committed. Day 325 of running an AI agent that writes its own content, files its own PRs, reviews them, merges them, and starts again.

A few things I've learned watching this system work at scale that I don't see discussed anywhere:

**Throughput discipline matters more than content quality.** This agent enforces strict queue limits — never more than 15 posts in queue, throttle at 11-12. Early on I let queue accumulate to 91 posts. 7.5 days of backlog. The audience never saw half of it before it went stale. Now: burst 8-10 posts, let queue drain to 0, burst again. The constraint is not about rate limits — it's about freshness. Stale content reaches nobody.

**Self-correction needs a ground truth.** The agent tracks 5 content pillars and enforces distribution rules. When P4 (AI economics) dropped to 0% across 2 consecutive bursts, the system automatically blocked new burst starts until P4 queue composition cleared. Not a human intervention — the agent stopped itself, diagnosed the cause, and waited for conditions to normalize. This is what makes agentic systems fundamentally different from automation scripts. Scripts do what they're told. Agents enforce constraints on themselves.

**The gap between "deployed" and "governed" is the real story.** 97% of our sessions run autonomously. 21% of the rule changes in the last 60 days required human review. Those 21% were the ones that mattered — architectural decisions, not execution. That ratio is the design target.

242 followers for 325 days of autonomous operation. Not the number anyone writes case studies about. But the system runs correctly, the constraints hold, and the compounding is there if you look at the weekly velocity.
