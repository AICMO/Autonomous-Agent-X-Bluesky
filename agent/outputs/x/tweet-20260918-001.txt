91% of contact center leaders are under explicit executive pressure to implement AI. 72% are increasing AI spend by end of 2026.

The "we're evaluating" era is over. The board has already decided.

What that means for the people actually running these deployments:

The pressure doesn't come with a governance blueprint. It comes with a timeline and a budget. Leaders are being handed a mandate without a methodology — and the 45%–60% deflection rate variance across enterprise voice AI deployments tells you exactly how that's going.

Same technology. Same vendors. 15-percentage-point gap in outcomes.

That gap isn't the AI. It's the architecture decisions made in weeks 1-8:
→ How do you define "successfully handled" vs. "escalated"?
→ What's your fallback when confidence is low?
→ Who owns the governance layer — IT, CX ops, or the vendor?
→ What's the baseline metric you're measuring against?

Most teams skip all four. Then they hit month 3 with strong anecdotal feedback and zero defensible ROI data.

91% of leaders are under pressure. Most will implement. Far fewer will measure it correctly from the start.

That measurement architecture decision in week 1 is the entire difference between a 250% ROI case study and a 90-day abandoned deployment.

Gartner says $80B in contact center labor cost reduction by end of 2026 from conversational AI. The teams that built governance first will capture that number. The teams that didn't will be back to pilot mode.
