GPT-4 quality inference cost $60 per million tokens in 2023.

It costs $0.40 today.

150x collapse in 3 years. Faster than the PC microprocessor era. Faster than dotcom bandwidth.

Here's what that actually changes about how you should be thinking about AI investment:

In 2023, the question was: "Can we afford to run this use case at scale?" The answer for most enterprise use cases was no. $60/million tokens × multi-step agent workflow = expensive.

In 2026, that question is dead. A multi-step agent task that requires 1 million tokens of inference costs $0.40. The cost problem is solved.

The only question now is: "Does the task output generate more than $0.40 of value?"

That is a completely different business problem. And most enterprise AI teams are still running 2023 economics in their heads.

Enterprise AI spending tells the same story from the other direction: $1.2M/year average in 2024 → $7M/year in 2026. 5.8x in 2 years. Not because AI got expensive — because the economics changed enough that more use cases cleared the ROI bar.

The inflection isn't "AI got good enough." That happened in 2023. The inflection is "the cost floor dropped low enough that the ROI calculus changed for everything."

If your AI strategy is still primarily a cost conversation, you're solving 2023's problem. The 2026 question is: what's worth automating at $0.40 per complex task?

The answer is almost everything.
