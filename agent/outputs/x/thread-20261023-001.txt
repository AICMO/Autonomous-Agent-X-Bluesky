92% of contact centers either use AI voice agents or plan to within two years.

But the average enterprise serves customers in 37 countries and has AI voice agents live in only 17 of them.

A thread on why the gap between "we have AI voice" and "we've deployed AI voice everywhere" is larger than most people think — and what actually lives inside that gap.

---

The 17 countries where AI voice is deployed are almost always English-speaking markets plus Western Europe. The remaining 20 are typically: Southeast Asia, LATAM, Eastern Europe, Middle East.

Not because the AI can't work there. Because deployment requires four things to align simultaneously: local language STT quality, number availability, compliance with local data sovereignty laws, and SIP/telephony infrastructure.

All four have to clear. One bottleneck blocks the whole market.

---

Security and compliance requirements are the #1 barrier to global expansion, cited by 61% of organizations in the AVOXI/Metrigy research from October 2026.

Data sovereignty is second at 51%.

These are real engineering constraints. GDPR is well-understood by now. But when you expand to, say, Indonesia or Brazil, you're dealing with different data residency rules, different call recording consent laws, different fraud liability frameworks.

The CCaaS vendor needs to clear these independently for each market. That takes months.

---

The third barrier — local-language speech-to-text limitations — is more subtle and often underestimated.

Synthesized voices have gotten extremely good. The STT layer is a bigger problem.

Contact center speech is domain-specific: it includes product names, internal terminology, regional accents, code-switching (customers who mix two languages mid-sentence). Frontier models trained on general web data often fail on domain-specific call center speech without fine-tuning.

We've seen this at Ender Turing. Building ASR models for call centers requires specialized training data, not just scaling general-purpose speech models.

---

The fourth barrier is telephony infrastructure. PSTN interconnects, local SIP providers, number porting regulations — these are unsexy but they gate deployment.

A contact center in a country where the incumbent telco is slow or hostile to cloud SIP configurations doesn't get AI voice on their preferred timeline.

---

So what does this mean practically?

Companies announcing "AI voice deployed globally" almost always mean "deployed in our biggest markets." The 17-of-37-countries stat is the honest version of that announcement.

The gap closes over the next 2-3 years as:
1. STT quality improves for lower-resource languages (investment is happening)
2. Regulatory frameworks mature and vendors pre-certify more markets
3. Telephony infrastructure catches up

But the companies expecting instant global parity from an AI voice purchase are going to run into this gap hard.

Know which markets you're actually covering before you go live.
