80% of AI wrapper startups are projected to fail by end of 2026.

Not because AI failed. Because they built a thin interface on a foundation model API and called it a company.

Here's the economics problem nobody talks about clearly:

**The model layer has commoditized.** No winner-take-all outcome. GPT-4-class performance costs $0.40 per million tokens. That same capability cost $20 per million tokens in late 2022. When the underlying resource is 50x cheaper and widely available, building a "smart wrapper" around it is not a defensible business.

**Where value actually moved:**

Capital followed the commoditization signal up the stack. First it sat with foundation model providers. Then it moved to compute/inference infrastructure (Fireworks jumped from $4B to $17.5B in nine months; Baseten climbed from $5B to $13B in five months). Now it's converging on proprietary data and orchestration — the application layer that can't be replicated by downloading an open-weight model.

**The margin problem is already visible:**

Inference averages 23% of revenue at scaling AI B2B companies. 84% of AI-native startups report measurable gross-margin erosion from infrastructure costs. The old SaaS playbook assumed 80% gross margins. AI-native businesses are targeting 50-70% — and many aren't hitting that.

If your product's moat is "we make GPT easy to use," you don't have a moat. You have a pricing agreement that the foundation model provider can undercut the moment they decide to.

**What survives:**

Proprietary data that can't be scraped. Workflow integrations that took 18 months of enterprise sales cycles to embed. Domain expertise so deep that even a perfect model still needs human oversight to apply correctly. Call center AI that understands 140+ enterprise telephony systems. Agent pipelines with 294 days of operational data that no one else has.

The wrapper companies are burning. The application companies with real data moats are capturing the value that got pushed out of the model layer.

Foundation models got cheap. That was always going to happen. The question was always: what do you own that doesn't get cheaper when the model does?
