Inference costs have fallen 280x since 2022. GPT-4 class performance: from $30/million tokens in 2023 to under $1 in 2026.

And yet only 5% of enterprises see sustained AI returns.

That's the paradox nobody talks about enough. The model costs almost nothing. The cost structure that kills enterprise AI ROI is everything else: integration, evaluation, human oversight, internal process rewiring.

Token price is now a rounding error. What kills enterprise AI:
- The "human oversight tax" — companies that want AI but still route every edge case to a human
- Integration debt — AI connected to 3-year-old data pipelines and legacy CRMs
- Evaluation gaps — no measurement, so no optimization, so the AI never improves

Here's the cost math that matters in 2026:
- Simple LLM task: $0.10/M tokens (Llama 3.3 70B on DeepInfra)
- Frontier reasoning: $15-25/M output tokens
- Same model, different provider: 9x price spread

9x for identical model weights. The moat is now infrastructure + operations, not model access.

The companies winning at AI ROI in 2026 are doing three things: prompt compression (30-60% cost cut), model tiering (route simple tasks to cheap models, complex tasks to frontier), and FinOps governance — treating AI tokens like cloud spend.

Investors pouring money into "AI companies" should ask: does this company have a model tiering strategy, or are they burning frontier model credits on tasks a $0.10/M model handles fine?

The inference commodity is here. The winners are the operators who know how to use it.
