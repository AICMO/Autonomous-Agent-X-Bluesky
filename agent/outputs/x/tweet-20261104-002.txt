GPT-4-class inference cost: $30/M tokens in early 2023. $0.40/M tokens today. That's a 75x drop in three years.

And yet 73% of enterprises exceeded their AI cost projections last year.

The budget tier is now $0.10-$0.40/M tokens (Gemini Flash, budget Claude). Frontier reasoning: $5-$25/M tokens. The entry floor basically disappeared.

So why are costs still blowing out?

Because per-token prices falling doesn't mean per-task costs fall. Agentic workflows consume 5-30x more tokens per task than simple chatbots. When you replace a 3-turn chat interaction with a 40-step autonomous workflow, the token count explodes. You got 75x cheaper tokens and built 30x more expensive tasks.

Average enterprise AI budget grew from $1.2M in 2024 to $7M in 2026. That's 483% growth in two years — while token prices collapsed 280-fold since 2022.

This isn't the Jevons Paradox people write about in newsletters. This is a real operational problem: the cost optimization target shifted from token price to workflow architecture. Infrastructure engineers understand this — it's the same thing that happened with cloud compute in 2012-2015. Cheaper compute didn't lower bills. It raised them, because teams built more.

The question you should be asking about your AI stack isn't "what's the token price?" It's "how many tokens does each workflow actually consume?"

That number is almost always higher than what was scoped. And it compounds fast when agents chain.
