91% of customer service and contact center leaders are under explicit executive pressure to implement AI. Not "exploring it." Not "evaluating vendors." Under pressure.

72% plan to increase AI investment before the end of 2026.

The era of "we're assessing the landscape" ended about 18 months ago. If you're still in discovery mode, you're already behind the curve.

Here's the number that matters more than 91%: enterprise Tier-1 deflection rates are still ranging from 45% to 60%. That gap — 15 percentage points between best-in-class and median — isn't a model problem. It's a governance and implementation problem.

The organizations hitting 60% containment aren't using better AI. They defined success criteria before they launched. They built feedback loops into the first sprint, not the third quarter review. They treated the voice AI like an employee — with performance metrics, coaching, and accountability.

The organizations stuck at 45% ran a pilot, handed it to IT, and called it done.

Executive pressure is real. But pressure without structure produces fast-deployed, poorly-governed systems — which is exactly how you end up in the 89% of agent pilots that never reach production.

The question your leadership team should be asking isn't "do we have AI in the contact center?" It's: "do we have a governance layer that can explain every decision the AI made in the last 30 days?"

Fully automated resolution is now under $1.00 per interaction. The economics are solved. The architecture is what separates the 45% from the 60%.
