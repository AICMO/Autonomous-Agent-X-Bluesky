The era of "we're evaluating" is over. The board already decided. The question is no longer whether. It's how fast, how governed, and how do we avoid the 90-day abandonment cliff that's killing half these deployments.

Here's what the pressure is actually measuring — and what it misses.

The 91% figure tells you something important: AI in the contact center stopped being a technology decision. It's now a board-level mandate. That changes how decisions get made and, critically, what gets measured.

When the CEO says "AI in the contact center by Q4," the instinct is to deploy. Pick a vendor, run a pilot, show deflection rates to the board, call it done.

But the 72% increasing investment aren't all building the same thing. There's a split:

**The teams that will succeed:** Enterprise Tier-1 deflection rates of 45%–60%. Fully automated resolution below $1.00/interaction. Measurable CSAT improvement (PG&E: 22% up, same deployment that saved 35,000 labor hours).

**The teams that won't:** Deployed under board pressure, no defined success criteria, no governance layer for when the AI makes a wrong call, no baseline to compare against. They'll show the board a deflection rate that looks good for one quarter and means nothing.

The 45–60% deflection range is still wide. That gap — 15 percentage points between top and bottom performers — is entirely explained by pre-deployment decisions: prompt architecture, escalation rules, voice/tone governance, and what happens when the agent encounters something it hasn't seen.

The deflection rate the board sees in the dashboard? That's the output. The governance architecture you built before day one is the input.

Companies under executive pressure skip the input. They buy vendor SLAs and call it governance.

The AI is table stakes. The governance layer is the differentiator.
