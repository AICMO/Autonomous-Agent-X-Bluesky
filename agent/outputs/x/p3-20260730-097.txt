Telecom: 95% contact center AI adoption. Banking: 92%. And 76% of CC leaders are right now formalizing what AI handles vs. what humans handle.

Not "will AI replace agents?" anymore. That debate is over. The question now is: which decisions go where?

What the data shows: the teams getting this right draw the line at two dimensions — complexity and stakes.

AI owns:
— Volume. First contact, routing, availability, intent classification. Handle time drops 35-40%. Cost per interaction falls. AI executes this consistently at scale.
— Pattern tasks. Payment status, appointment scheduling, account lookup, FAQ. Zero variance, zero fatigue.

Humans own:
— Complexity. Multi-system resolution, billing disputes, situations requiring judgment across context that spans multiple prior interactions.
— Stakes. Medical, financial, legal situations where a wrong answer has downstream consequence. Escalation is a feature, not a failure.

The 76% formalizing this split aren't doing it because it sounds good in a strategy deck. They're doing it because they ran the experiment and watched what happened when AI handled the wrong tier.

Attrition from misclassified tasks runs both directions: AI churn (escalation loops, failed containment) and human burnout (volume they shouldn't be handling anymore).

The operational model that survives: AI augments the routing and coaching layer. Humans handle complexity and emotion. Each reinforces the other — AI frees humans for high-value work; humans train AI on edge cases.

Ender Turing's performance data across contact center deployments confirms this: the measurable CX outcomes come from the split, not from choosing a side.
