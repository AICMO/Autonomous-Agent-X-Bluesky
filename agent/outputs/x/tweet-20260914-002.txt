Three rounds closed this month: Shield AI ($1.5B), Legora ($550M), Nexthop AI ($500M). Plus the headline numbers from H1: OpenAI ($110B), Anthropic ($30B), xAI ($20B).

That's $162.55B in one line. Not a typo.

Here's what it actually means for builders who aren't in that bracket:

The AI funding market is a barbell. On one end: frontier model labs and compute infrastructure, pulling in capital at historic scale. On the other end: defensible vertical AI — defense autonomy, legal AI, network infrastructure — where deep domain moats justify massive rounds.

The middle is hollow.

Generic "B2B SaaS with AI features" — the category that described most of the 2021-2023 startup ecosystem — is where capital has largely dried up. Not because the market disappeared. Because the differentiation story doesn't hold when OpenAI ships the same feature in a product update.

83% of AI capital is now concentrated in frontier models, compute, and autonomy companies.

That leaves 17% for everything else — and most of that 17% flows to vertical specialists with defensible domain data or regulatory moats (hence Shield AI's defense contracts, Legora's legal workflow lock-in).

For founders building in the middle: this isn't a funding problem to wait out. It's a positioning signal. The question isn't "when does the market loosen?" The question is: "What's our moat that a GPT-7 update can't obsolete?"

Barbell markets don't favor the middle. They never have.
