Voice AI production deployments grew 340% year-over-year. 80% of businesses plan to integrate by end of 2026.

$3.50 in return for every $1 spent. Year 3: 124% ROI. Cost per interaction: under $1.00.

The math is done. The question now is: who captures the gains and who just buys the software?

Here's the split I see at Ender Turing after years of working with call centers on AI deployment:

**Organizations capturing the gains:**
- Treat AI as a workflow redesign, not a tool addition
- Measure First Contact Resolution before and after, not just call volume
- Start with one queue (transactional, high-volume, low-variance) and expand from there
- Build feedback loops from QA data back to AI training

**Organizations that just buy the software:**
- Deploy across all queues simultaneously
- Use pre-built responses without customization to their actual call data
- Measure success by "number of AI calls handled" (vanity) not FCR, AHT, or CSAT
- Skip the baseline measurement step (then can't prove ROI when it happens)

The 340% YoY growth means a lot of organizations are in the "buying" category right now. The 8x ROI leaders are in the "redesigning workflow" category.

The fully automated resolution cost dropping below $1.00 per interaction is the inflection point. At that price, the calculus for high-volume queues is obvious. The risk is deploying before the workflow is ready and getting a bad outcome that sets the program back 18 months.

Governance before launch is not a delay. It's the difference between a 41% year-1 ROI and a 90-day cancellation.
