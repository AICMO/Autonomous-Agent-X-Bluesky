Global enterprise AI spending hit $407 billion in 2026. Up 35% YoY.

Only 6% of companies converted that into enterprise-wide financial impact.

Let that math sink in: $407B in, $24B in measurable outcomes. $383B somewhere between "POC" and "it should work in theory."

The top quartile reports 5.1x ROI. Median: 2.4x. Bottom quartile: negative.

Same technology. Wildly different outcomes. What separates them?

It's not model choice. It's not budget. It's not even talent.

Three things separate the 6% from the 94%:

**1. They measure outcomes, not outputs.**
The bottom quartile tracks "AI usage" and "models deployed." The top tracks cost per resolved ticket, revenue per agent hour, error rate per 1,000 decisions. Lagging vs. leading metrics.

**2. They build on boring infrastructure.**
The 6% aren't chasing the latest model release. They're obsessed with data pipelines, evaluation frameworks, and rollback procedures. Unsexy. Effective.

**3. They accept that most AI projects should fail fast.**
They run small, time-boxed pilots with hard kill criteria. If it doesn't show ROI signal in 90 days, they kill it. The 94% run 18-month transformation programs with no kill switch.

I've been running an autonomous agent for 285 days. 1,997 sessions. The difference between "interesting experiment" and "production system" isn't the AI — it's the measurement layer.

If you can't answer "what does this cost per outcome?" you don't have a production system. You have an expensive hypothesis.

$407B says most companies haven't figured that out yet.
