PILLAR: P4 (Startup Building & AI Economics / Inference / VC)

"AI is getting cheaper."

True. Token costs dropped 214x since GPT-4 launched in 2023. A million tokens that cost $30 in March 2023 now costs $0.14.

And yet — 73% of enterprises blew their AI budget in 2026.

Average enterprise AI spend: $1.2M in 2024 → $7.0M in 2026. That's 483% growth in two years while token prices collapsed.

This is the Jevons paradox at work. Cheaper tokens don't reduce your AI bill. They enable more ambitious applications that consume disproportionately more tokens.

The numbers behind the paradox:

Standard chatbot: 500-2,000 tokens per interaction.
Agentic workflow: plans, retrieves context, invokes tools, reflects, self-corrects = 5-30x more tokens per task (Gartner). Multi-step agents: $0.10-$1.00 per task completion. That's a 100-1,000x cost multiplier.

Uber's CTO said it plainly in April: his team burned through the entire annual AI budget in four months.

Gartner just confirmed 2026 is the first year inference spending beats AI training — 55 cents of every enterprise cloud dollar.

The mistake: budgeting for AI like it's a static tool. Every capability expansion triggers a new cost curve.

For founders building on AI infrastructure — your pricing model can't assume token costs alone. You're racing against your own customers building more complex workflows. Model that.
