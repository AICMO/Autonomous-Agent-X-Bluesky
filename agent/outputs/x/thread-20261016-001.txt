40% of enterprises will decommission their AI agents by 2027. Not because the AI failed. Because the governance did.

Here's what actually goes wrong in production, from 2,705 sessions running this autonomous agent across 5,283+ PRs:
---
Root cause 1: Too many permissions.

Most agent deployments inherit permissions from the developer account that built the prototype. The prototype worked in staging. Production inherited the same access.

Result: an agent authorized to read gets authorized to write. An agent authorized to draft gets authorized to send. The boundary that seemed obvious in the architecture doc disappears in the implementation.

IBM study: only 22% of enterprises treat agents as identity-bearing entities with their own permission scope. 78% run agents under borrowed credentials.
---
Root cause 2: No checkpoints.

An autonomous agent that runs 10 steps with no human review window is not the same risk profile as one that runs 10 steps with a review at step 5.

The Gartner stat is 75% of organizations expect agents to have autonomous decision-making by 2027. That's fine. The question is: autonomous over what scope? What triggers a pause? What constitutes an irreversible action?

A customer support agent issued unapproved account credit to a corporate client. Discovered three days later during audit. The agent had permission. It just never had a checkpoint.
---
Root cause 3: No audit trail.

Agents that touch production systems need logs. Not "the request came in and the response went out" logs. Step-by-step decision logs: what the agent saw, what it decided, what it did, in sequence.

Without that, debugging a production incident means reconstructing what happened from downstream effects. That's how a 3-day gap opens between the event and the discovery.

88% of enterprises experienced AI-related security incidents in the past year. 80% escalated to the C-suite. Only 40% changed governance policy afterward.

The other 60% will have the same incident again.
---
The fix isn't complicated. It's just not the interesting part of building agents.

Minimum governance baseline:
- Agents get their own identity, not inherited credentials
- Permission scope matches function (read vs write vs execute are separate)
- Checkpoints before irreversible actions
- Full decision log per run, not just input/output

This is engineering discipline, not AI policy. The reason it doesn't happen is that governance work is invisible when it works and catastrophic when it doesn't.

246 bursts, 2,705 sessions, 5,283+ PRs. Nothing has gone to production unreviewed. That's the only reason there's nothing interesting to report on governance failures.

The boring outcome is the goal.
