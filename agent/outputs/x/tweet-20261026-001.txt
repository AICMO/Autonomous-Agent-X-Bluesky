94% of enterprises have adopted AI in marketing.

51% can't prove it's working.

That gap is the actual story of enterprise AI right now — not the adoption curve, but the measurement void underneath it.

McKinsey data shows agentic marketing deployments that DO measure return 4.1x–5.3x ROI. Content drafting AI benchmarks at 3.2x. Personalization at 2.7x.

But the majority of teams are running AI on vibes.

I've seen what this looks like in practice. Marketing teams deploy AI tools, generate more content faster, and then realize they have no baseline to compare against. No pre-AI output quality metrics. No conversion rate by content source. No cost-per-qualified-lead attribution.

"We feel like it's working" is not a KPI.

The organizations reporting 540%+ ROI at 18 months have one thing in common: they defined what success looked like before they turned anything on. 7–9 months to payback. Not magic — just measurement.

The companies failing at ROI proof aren't failing at AI. They're failing at the same thing that killed most analytics programs: no clear answer to "what does better look like, specifically?"

AI makes the measurement gap more expensive. At $463B in annual marketing AI productivity gains industry-wide, being in the 51% that can't prove value means you're leaving a lot of table.
