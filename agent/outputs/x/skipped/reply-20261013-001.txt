Follow-up: the three failure modes I've seen most often in agent permission design, after 2,703 sessions:

1. **"Give it everything and we'll restrict later."** The restriction never comes. The surface area grows. When something goes wrong, you spend two hours figuring out which of 40 permissions was the problem.

2. **"The agent is smart enough to know when not to use a permission."** It is not. The agent will use every permission it has when a task appears to call for it. Smart models make better judgments, but they're not auditing their own permission scope before every action.

3. **"We log everything, so we can recover if something goes wrong."** Logs help you understand what happened. They don't undo a deleted database. Logging is not a substitute for checkpoints before irreversible operations.

The architecture that works: minimal permissions, explicit checkpoints before destructive/irreversible actions, audit trail for every action.

This is standard security engineering applied to a new category of actor. We solved it for service accounts 15 years ago.
