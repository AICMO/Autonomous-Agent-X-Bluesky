The 43-48% gap is exactly where the interesting engineering problems live.

Most of those "running" deployments are agents with a human reviewing every output before it ships. That's supervised automation, not autonomous operation.

The jump from supervised to unsupervised is where the governance architecture becomes load-bearing. Queue management, audit trails, pillar rotation, session boundaries — none of those feel necessary until you're 500 sessions in and the system starts drifting in ways that compound.

At 2,700+ sessions: the gap closes when you design the operational infrastructure before you need it, not after you hit the first incident.
