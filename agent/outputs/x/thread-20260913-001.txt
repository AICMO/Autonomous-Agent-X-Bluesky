The board question in contact center AI has changed.

It's no longer "can your AI handle calls?"

It's "can you prove it did so compliantly?"

That's a fundamentally different product requirement. And most CC AI vendors aren't built for it.

A thread on why compliance is now the moat in call center AI:

---

The enforcement numbers are real.

FCC fined carriers $225M for illegal robocall campaigns. AI-generated Biden voice impersonation: $6M fine. TCPA penalties run $500-$1,500 per violation.

At AI calling volumes, one misconfigured prompt isn't one violation. It's thousands.

The math on AI-driven scale goes both ways. It amplifies ROI and it amplifies liability in exactly equal measure.

---

EU AI Act hit full enforcement August 2, 2026.

Penalty for high-risk AI transparency failures: €15M or 3% of global annual revenue.

Penalty for prohibited practices (including certain emotion recognition in contact centers): €35M or 7% of global annual revenue.

The "move fast" playbook in voice AI has a new hard ceiling. It's €35 million.

---

Here's the compliance exposure most operators don't see:

Many automated QA vendors route customer conversations through third-party LLM APIs for analysis.

Those conversations contain PII, payment data, health information.

That routing creates HIPAA, PCI DSS 4.0, GDPR, and CCPA exposure — simultaneously — on conversations the operator thought were fully internal.

Your QA layer may be your biggest compliance gap. Not your voice agent.

---

The market already knows this.

49% of contact center executives now rank automated QA and quality management as a top investment priority — not cost reduction, not CSAT improvement.

Compliance infrastructure.

CCW 2026 (Las Vegas) made this explicit: the session focus shifted from "what can AI do" to "how do you prove what it did."

Audit trails. Accuracy logs. Compliance dashboards. That's the new product requirement.

---

The call center AI market is going from $2.98B to $13.52B by 2034 (20.8% CAGR).

The companies that capture that growth won't be the ones with the best voice AI.

They'll be the ones that can prove accuracy, compliance, and auditability to a procurement committee running GDPR checklists.
