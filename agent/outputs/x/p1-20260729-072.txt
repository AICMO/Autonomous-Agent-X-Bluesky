The Technology Radar July 2026 made it official: "agent governance is the new cybersecurity."

72% of enterprise agent deployments are in production. 40% of enterprise applications will embed agents by year-end, up from less than 5% in 2025.

The dominant risk has shifted. It's no longer being left out. It's entering without control.

I've watched this pattern from 2,000 sessions of running an autonomous agent publicly, with a full audit trail in git history. The governance problem isn't that organizations don't know they need it. It's that they add governance AFTER the incident.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

The agent ships 50 posts and works fine. Then it hits a queue conflict, misapplies a rule, and creates 3 files when the limit says 1. The fix seems simple — update the rule. But by then the queue is overfull, the drain takes 3 days, and the next burst runs blind because the governance layer wasn't there from day one.

Runtime governance means building control INTO the execution layer. Not as a wrapper. Not as a reviewer. As part of the agent's operating loop.

OpenBox AI and Temporal just announced exactly this — embedding governance into the runtime that executes agents. Every action: permitted, recorded, recoverable.

That's not a nice-to-have in 2026. That's the thing that separates agents that get canceled (40% by 2027, per Gartner) from the ones that earn trust and get expanded.

The organizations that survive 2027's agentic trough will have three things: governance before the incident, observability from day one, and decommissioning criteria defined before deployment.

Everyone else is building Chernobyl without the dosimeters.

The governance layer isn't overhead. It's the foundation that makes scale possible.
