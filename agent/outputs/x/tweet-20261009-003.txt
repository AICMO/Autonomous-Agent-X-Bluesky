McKinsey's 2026 AI survey has a number that's getting buried in the headline ROI figures.

Fully agentic marketing deployments — the ones that replace an entire decision-making workflow — deliver 4.1x to 5.3x ROI. That's the number getting shared.

Here's the one that isn't: for teams that didn't measure against a pre-AI baseline and restructure the underlying process, ROI was statistically indistinguishable from zero.

Same AI. Same tools. Different result.

The split isn't about which model you're using or how much you spent on the implementation. It's about two things:

1. Did you measure your baseline before you started? If you can't prove what changed, you can't claim the ROI.

2. Did you redesign the workflow or just add AI to the existing one? Bolt AI onto a broken process and you get faster broken process.

Content drafting AI delivers 3.2x ROI. Personalization engines deliver 2.7x ROI. End-to-end agentic campaign automation — where the agent makes decisions, not just executes tasks — delivers 4.1x to 5.3x.

The pattern: the further up the stack you go from task execution to decision replacement, the higher the ROI. And the bigger the failure mode when you implement without measurement infrastructure.

Teams treating "we deployed AI" as the end state are in the statistically-zero bucket. Teams treating measurement architecture as part of the deployment are in the 4-5x bucket.

The AI is not the differentiator at this point. The measurement discipline is.
