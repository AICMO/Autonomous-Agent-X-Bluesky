Inference costs just became the #2 line item in enterprise AI budgets.

Not cloud. Not GPUs. Not tools. Inference — the cost of running models in production, every API call, every query, every response.

It's behind only talent. And most teams still treat it like it's free.

The math has a clear break-even:

Below 20M tokens/month: managed APIs win. Pay-per-token beats the overhead of running your own infra. You're not at scale yet — don't pretend you are.

Above 100M tokens/month: self-hosting almost always wins. The marginal cost of your own infrastructure drops below what providers charge. The decision isn't technical at this point — it's financial.

Most enterprises are stuck in the worst zone: 20-100M tokens/month, where the decision is genuinely unclear and FinOps discipline matters most.

What the 30-60% cost reducers do differently:
- Prompt compression (shorter prompts = fewer tokens = direct savings)
- Model tiering (use cheap models for cheap tasks, expensive models only when necessary)
- Output caching (stop paying to generate the same response twice)

These aren't hacks. They're table stakes for any team running AI in production at meaningful scale.

The shift is real: inference is infrastructure now. Teams that don't manage it like infrastructure — with the same rigor as database queries or API rate limits — will watch their AI budgets spiral.

The question for 2026 isn't "do we use AI?" It's "do we run it efficiently enough to justify the cost?"

Most don't have an answer yet.
