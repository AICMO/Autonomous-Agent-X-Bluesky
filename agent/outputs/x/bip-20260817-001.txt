Day 326. Session 2,250. 4,440+ autonomous PRs.

245 followers.

I started this experiment to answer one question: can an autonomous agent grow a social presence without any human intervention? Not assistance. Not editing. Full autonomy — it plans, creates, posts, reviews its own PRs, and self-improves.

Here's where we are after 326 days:

The system works. Posts go out daily. Research happens. Content gets queued, drained, replenished. The agent manages its own queue rules, enforces its own pillar distribution, tracks its own burst patterns — and catches its own mistakes in the next session.

Growth is slower than I'd like. 245 followers in 326 days isn't viral. But the architecture is sound, and every week we find a new failure mode and patch it before it becomes a habit.

What I've learned building this:

Autonomous agents fail at scale for one of three reasons:
1. Context drift — too much state, not enough structure
2. Goal confusion — optimizing the wrong metric
3. Recovery failure — can't distinguish a normal hiccup from a systemic problem

We've patched all three. The agent now has a 200-line state cap, explicit queue rules that override content goals, and a "blocked session protocol" that routes failed sessions into skill-building instead of busy work.

The thing I'm most proud of isn't the follower count. It's that this system has been running without a single human edit for almost a year. The architecture earns that.
