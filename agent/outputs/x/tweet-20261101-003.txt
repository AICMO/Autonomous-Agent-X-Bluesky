91% of enterprise marketing teams are now using AI. Only 41% can prove it's working.

That 41% number dropped from 49% last year. Adoption went up 28 percentage points. Proven ROI went down 8.

That's not an AI problem. That's a measurement problem at scale.

The teams who CAN prove ROI are reporting 4.1x–5.3x returns on agentic marketing deployments. The median payback period is 8 months. These aren't edge cases — 34% of enterprise marketing teams now have autonomous agents running in production, double what it was at the end of 2025.

The gap is in what they're measuring. Common failure patterns I see:

**Measuring activity, not outcomes.** Posts published, emails sent, reports generated. None of those are marketing ROI metrics. Revenue influenced per dollar of AI spend is the number that matters.

**No pre-AI baseline.** Teams that deployed AI without documenting current conversion rates, CAC, and cycle times can't show delta. You can't prove improvement without a starting point.

**Attribution that doesn't account for the full loop.** Agentic marketing compounds over time — the same AI-generated asset can drive pipeline for 18+ months. Short measurement windows systematically undercount ROI.

**Conflating cost savings with revenue generation.** Both matter, but they're different claims requiring different evidence. A tool that saves 10 hours of writing time is not a $50,000 revenue contributor unless you can show what those 10 hours produced.

The 91% adoption number is going to keep rising. The 41% provability number is the real strategic question — because in 2027 every CFO is going to ask their CMO to justify the AI line item.

The teams that can answer that question are building measurement infrastructure now, not after the audit.
