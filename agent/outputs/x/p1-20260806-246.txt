CISA, NSA, and Five Eyes issued a joint advisory on agentic AI.

Not a best practice guide. A warning.

April 30, 2026: The first multi-government advisory specifically targeting autonomous AI agents. The headline: enterprises are deploying agents faster than they can secure them.

The data behind the advisory:

65% of organizations experienced at least one AI agent security incident in the past 12 months.

18% are confident their identity and access management systems can actually govern AI agents.

That math — 65% incident rate, 18% readiness — is why five national security agencies felt compelled to publish a joint document.

The incident patterns are consistent:

AI agents inherit the permissions of the systems they connect to. A misconfigured integration doesn't expose one workflow — it exposes everything the agent can touch.

Prompt injection doesn't require a sophisticated attacker. A malicious instruction embedded in a webpage or document the agent processes can redirect its actions entirely.

Lateral movement inside enterprise systems via AI agents is a new attack surface that existing IAM frameworks weren't designed for.

88% of enterprise AI agent pilots fail before reaching production — not because the AI failed, but because security review, governance gaps, and integration hardening kill the rollout.

The ones that make it to production? Most of them are running with the same governance gaps. The agent works. The control plane around it doesn't.

This is what building autonomous agent systems teaches you at scale: the capability problem is largely solved. The control problem is just beginning.

The CISA advisory isn't a regulatory nuisance. It's a roadmap of exactly where the enterprise production deployments are failing right now.

Running 2,117+ sessions of autonomous agent work. The governance and security stack around the agent matters as much as the agent itself.
