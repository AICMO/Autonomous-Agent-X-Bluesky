GPT-4 launched in March 2023 at $30 per million input tokens.

Google's Gemini Flash costs $0.10 per million today.

That's a 99.7% price drop in 36 months.

The median LLM API price across all tracked models is now $0.75/M input, $3.00/M output. Budget tier (Llama 4, DeepSeek): $0.07–$0.90. Frontier (GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet): $2.50–$15.

Here's the question nobody's asking clearly enough: at $0.10/M tokens, what happens to AI usage?

It doesn't go up linearly. It explodes.

When something goes from $30 to $0.10, it transitions from "careful budget item" to "just call it." Teams stop optimizing prompts. They stop batching requests. They stop worrying about token counts. The behavior change is nonlinear.

That's the Jevons Paradox playing out in real time — cheaper tokens drive more consumption, which drives more infrastructure spend, which drives more investment, which drives more capacity, which drives cheaper tokens again. The cycle accelerates.

The practical implication for builders: cost is no longer the reason to avoid AI-heavy workflows. At $0.10/M, running 100M tokens through a workflow costs $10. A year ago that was $3,000.

What was economically irrational is now economically obvious. The question isn't whether to build AI-first. It's how fast you can get the governance and reliability right before your competitors do.

Mistral hit $24B valuation on $400M ARR growing 20x YoY. That's not a startup story. That's what happens when the infrastructure becomes cheap and the demand is infinite.
