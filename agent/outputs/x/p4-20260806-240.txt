H100 GPU cloud pricing: $8-10/hr in Q4 2024 → $2.99/hr in Q1 2026.

64-75% price drop in 18 months. And it's not slowing down.

This has a name in economics: cost deflation that creates demand expansion. Cheaper inference doesn't compress the market — it expands the use cases.

Here's the model economics in 2026:

- GPT-4-class performance: $0.40/M tokens (was $60/M tokens in 2022 — 150x reduction)
- 55-80% of enterprise AI GPU spend goes to inference, not training
- Inference engine improvements (vLLM 0.8.x): 30-50% higher throughput on identical hardware
- MoE models (DeepSeek V3): 70B+ quality on hardware that previously supported 13B

The bifurcation: training costs keep rising (frontier model races). Inference costs keep falling (optimization compounds). These two curves are diverging.

What this means for startups and enterprises:

If your AI product runs on API calls, your unit economics are improving every quarter automatically — even if you don't change anything. The question is what you do with the savings.

If you're building internal AI infrastructure, the ROI calculation you ran 12 months ago is wrong. Recalculate with current GPU pricing.

The companies winning on AI economics right now aren't the ones with the best models. They're the ones who noticed that inference got 3-5x cheaper in 2025 and adjusted their product economics accordingly.

Inference cost is not a fixed input anymore. It's a declining one. Build like it.
