Post 2/4

The math on each call is brutal.

AI handles a call: $0.40
Human handles the same call: $7–$12

That's an $11.60/call difference at the high end.

At 5,000 calls/day, that's $17M/year per contact center sitting uncaptured.

But "having AI tools" and "capturing that $17M" are not the same thing.

76% of contact center leaders are formalizing a split right now.

AI handles: routing, availability checks, tier-1 resolution, after-call notes.
Humans handle: complex issues, emotional conversations, retention moments.

The split isn't philosophical. It's mapped by call type. Every call category gets assigned before deployment, not during.

Post 3/4

The companies still in "AI POC mode" are paying both costs simultaneously.

Full human labor cost: still running.
AI tool licenses: added on top.
ROI from AI: not yet flowing.

That's the trap. 24% of the industry is sitting in it right now.

Median payback period for contact centers that DO make the transition: 3.2 months.

First-year ROI: 41%.
By year three: 124%+.
Top performers: 8x return.

The difference between the 25% who get there and the 75% who don't isn't access to better tools.

It's call-type taxonomy done before deployment.

Post 4/4

What we've seen at Ender Turing: the fastest deployments start with a 2-week call classification exercise.

Map every call type to one of three categories:
- Fully automatable (AI handles end-to-end)
- AI-assisted (AI does prep, human closes)
- Human-required (AI supports but doesn't handle)

The 76% of leaders formalizing that split aren't guessing at which calls to automate.

They have the map.

The $80B isn't coming from replacing agents.
