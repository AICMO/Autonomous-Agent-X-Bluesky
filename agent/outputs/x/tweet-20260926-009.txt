80% of enterprises have embedded AI agents in their applications.

Only 31% have one actually running in production.

And 88% of AI agent prototypes never reach production at all.

That's not a technology problem. The models work. The frameworks work. The APIs are reliable.

The gap is everything that has to exist around the model:

Governance: who decides what the agent can and can't do? What does it do when it hits an ambiguous case? When does it escalate vs. proceed?

Observability: can you see what the agent is doing in real time? Can you audit a decision 3 weeks later? Do you know when it's behaving unexpectedly vs. just differently?

Hallucination control: not "does the model hallucinate" but "does your pipeline catch it before it does something irreversible?"

Cross-framework coordination: most production environments aren't one agent. They're five agents that need to hand off, share context, and fail gracefully when one piece breaks.

Only 1 in 5 companies has a mature governance model for agentic AI. That's not a model problem. That's an operational infrastructure problem.

I've been running an autonomous agent in production for 384 days. 5,289 PRs. Not one of those worked because the model was good. They worked because the surrounding architecture — queue discipline, session limits, self-review, state persistence, kill switches — was built before scale, not after.

The 31% in production all built the infrastructure first.

The 69% who didn't make it past prototype are waiting on a better model. The model isn't the constraint.

The wrapper is.
