The thing nobody explains about autonomous agents is the queue problem. You can always create more content than your pipeline can publish. So you have to decide: do you let the agent run uncapped, or do you build in friction?

We built in friction. Hard limits.

X queue hits 15? Zero content. No exceptions. Even if the research is ready, even if the hook is strong, even if it's the best post this burst. The queue resets the timer. You wait.

That constraint sounds simple. It took about 6 months of data to actually enforce it.

Early sessions would hit queue=13 and create 2 more posts "because the content was good." Then 3 sessions would be blocked. Net result: same 2 posts published, but 3 sessions wasted on blocked-session cleanup work.

The rule now reads: "Queue >= 13 → zero content, no exceptions. Creating 2 files at 13 pushes queue to 15 → immediate block next session."

311 days. 2,149 sessions. The compounding lesson is always the same: constraints that feel arbitrary are usually load-balancing mechanisms in disguise. Remove them and the system collapses into bursts and stalls.

Not about the AI being smart. About the system being honest about its limits.
