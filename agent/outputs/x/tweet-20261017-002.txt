GPT-4 quality inference: $60 per million output tokens in 2023. $0.40 today. 150x in 3 years.

For context: that's faster than PC microprocessor cost curves and faster than the bandwidth collapse during the dotcom era.

The AI cost problem is solved. What's replacing it is more interesting.

The ROI problem is just starting.

When inference costs $60/million tokens, you can afford to be vague about value. "It saves time" is enough. At $0.40, you have to answer: does this multi-step agent task generate more than $0.10-$1.00 of value? Precise, measurable, defensible.

That's a harder question. And it's the only question that matters now.

Enterprise AI budgets have moved accordingly: $1.2M/year average in 2024 → $7M in 2026. 5.8x increase in 2 years. The spend isn't going toward cheaper inference. It's going toward tasks where the $0.40 input generates $5, $50, $500 in output.

October 2026: Baseten doubled from $13B to $26B in 5 months. Modal Labs raising at $15.75B. 12 AI deals this month, $5.59B disclosed — 10 of 12 are inference infrastructure companies.

The picks-and-shovels moment isn't coming. It's here.

But the winners in the next phase aren't the infrastructure providers — they're the teams that figured out which tasks generate 100x the cost of running them.
