The $955M Cognigy acquisition tells you everything about where enterprise AI spend is going.

Per-seat SaaS is dying. Per-action infrastructure is replacing it. Here's the math that's driving the shift:

---

Old model: contact center platform charges $X per agent seat per month.

You have 500 agents. You pay 500 × $X. Vendor wins when headcount stays flat.

New model: agentic AI priced at $0.05–$0.25 per minute of interaction.

1,000 calls/day at 6 minutes average = $300–$1,500/day. Your costs scale with demand, not headcount.

---

Why this matters: the per-seat model had no incentive to reduce handle time or increase resolution rate. The vendor made money regardless.

Per-action pricing flips the incentive. Resolve faster, cost less. Deflect more, cost less. The AI vendor wins when you win.

That's not a product difference. That's a business model difference.

---

$955M for Cognigy. $13.5B projected market by 2034. The acquirers betting on this know something:

The CCaaS winners will be the ones who built billing infrastructure for per-action pricing, not the ones with the most features.

Infrastructure bets always look expensive until they're obvious.

---

The companies that will get priced out of this market aren't the startups. They're the legacy platform vendors who built their entire P&L around seat-count growth.

When the pricing model changes, the business model changes. When the business model changes, the winners change.

$955M says the market already knows who the winners are.
