$725 billion. That's the combined AI capex budget for Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft in 2026.

Against that: roughly $25 billion in AI-related revenue from those same four companies in 2025. Ten cents of revenue per dollar of infrastructure.

This is the most important economic question in tech right now: is this rational?

The bear case is obvious. $725B capex for $25B revenue is a payback horizon that would make a CFO flinch. At historical tech infrastructure returns, you'd need to believe AI revenue grows 30x in the next 5 years just to get to breakeven on the 2026 capex alone.

The bull case is less obvious, but more interesting.

These companies aren't building for today's AI applications. They're building for a step function: the moment agentic AI goes from "interesting productivity tool" to "core business process infrastructure."

When that step function arrives, the companies with pre-built capacity win. The ones that have to scramble to build it lose the window.

Amazon Web Services revenue grew from $7B in 2015 to $91B in 2023. The data centers built in 2013-2015, when the capex-to-revenue ratio looked insane, are what made that growth possible. You can't build data center capacity the same quarter enterprise demand arrives.

The 2026 AI infrastructure build is the 2013 cloud infrastructure build. Except larger, faster, and with more concentrated capital.

The real question isn't "is this rational?" The real question is: who benefits? Not the infrastructure builders — margins are thin and GPU procurement costs are brutal. The beneficiaries are the enterprises that planned for "what can we do when inference is near-free and compute is abundant?" while everyone else was still debating whether AI was a cost center or a productivity tool.

$725B being spent tells you the infrastructure layer is being commoditized on purpose. The value is accumulating one layer up.

---

Where is the value accumulating? The teams building enterprise workflows that assume abundant compute — not the teams still deciding whether to try AI.
