91% of contact center leaders are under direct executive pressure to implement AI. 72% are increasing AI investment before year-end.

The era of "we're evaluating AI" is over. The era of "we deployed it but it doesn't work" has begun.

Here's the conversation that's actually happening in boardrooms right now.

The pressure is real. The mandates are real. What's not real is the readiness. Gartner's latest data: 91% of leaders under executive pressure + only 21% have mature governance frameworks. That's a math problem with a painful solution curve.

The contact centers that will navigate this well share three characteristics: they defined what "good" looks like before deployment (a baseline call resolution rate, a CSAT score, a containment threshold), they assigned an accountable owner (not "the AI team" — a person with a number to hit), and they built incrementally instead of replacing everything at once.

Golden Nugget didn't start by automating all reservations. They started with a defined scope (inbound reservation calls), a measurable outcome ($600K/month revenue attributed to AI-handled bookings), and a governance layer that a human reviewed weekly.

That's the repeatable pattern. Not "deploy AI and measure impact later." That's how you end up in the 72% that increased investment without clarity on what they bought.

The 45-60% Tier-1 deflection range tells the story: the gap is 15 percentage points. The difference between 45% and 60% deflection isn't the AI vendor — it's the governance architecture, the prompt design, and the continuous improvement cycle. Same tools. 30% difference in output.

If you're in that boardroom conversation and you don't yet have a baseline metric and an accountable owner, those are the two things to fix before committing budget.
