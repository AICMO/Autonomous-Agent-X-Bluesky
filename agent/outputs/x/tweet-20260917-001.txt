The LLM inference cost curve just hit an inflection point that most enterprise P&Ls haven't priced in yet.

Token costs dropped 280x in 24 months. Gemini Flash 2.5 is now $0.15 per million input tokens. GPT-4o equivalent capability costs 1/20th of what it cost in 2023.

Here's the business model problem nobody wants to say out loud: the AI tools your enterprise is paying per-seat SaaS pricing for are running on infrastructure that gets 30-50% cheaper every 12 months. The vendor keeps the margin. You don't.

That math resolves in one of three ways:

1. Incumbents reprice aggressively to capture the cost curve before competitors do. (Rarely happens without external pressure.)

2. A new entrant builds the same capability at transparent infrastructure cost and undercuts everyone. (Already happening in coding tools, document processing, customer service AI.)

3. Enterprises reach a tipping point and start building internal infrastructure. (Slower, but the economics become undeniable at scale — $1.00/interaction vs. $6-12 for live agents is not a pilot-size decision.)

The Jevons Paradox applies here too: as inference gets cheaper, enterprises use more of it. Total AI spend per company is increasing even as per-unit cost drops. The question isn't whether you'll spend more on AI. It's whether you'll spend more on infrastructure you control or on margins you're subsidizing for SaaS vendors.

The companies building the governance layer now — real logging, audit trails, cost attribution per workflow — are the ones who will understand their actual AI economics in 2027. Everyone else will have a line item that says "AI tools" with no visibility into what it's buying.

Infrastructure-first is not a technical choice. It's a financial one.
