61% of contact center leaders say AI made their conversations harder.

Not easier. Harder.

ClearBridge 2026 data: despite record AI investment, 61% of contact center leaders report customer conversations became more complex and difficult after AI implementation.

Everyone's asking the wrong question. They're asking "did the AI work?" The right question: "what happened to the conversations the AI didn't handle?"

Here's what's actually happening:

AI automation is correctly handling the simple calls. Routine billing questions, password resets, standard order status. That volume goes to the bot. It handles 70-80% of it without human intervention.

What's left for human agents? The 20-30% of calls that are: angry customers, billing disputes with nuance, emotionally charged situations, regulatory complaints, edge cases the model wasn't trained on.

So yes, AI is working. And yes, conversations got harder. Both are true simultaneously.

The companies that are struggling didn't plan for the composition shift. Their agents were trained for a mix: 70% routine, 30% complex. Now they handle 95% complex. Nobody retrained them. Nobody updated the QA rubrics. Nobody rebuilt the coaching frameworks.

The failure isn't the AI. It's assuming AI is a bolt-on, not a redesign.

When you deploy voice AI at scale, you're not just automating calls. You're fundamentally changing the job of a human agent. That requires:

1. New competency frameworks (patience, empathy, de-escalation — not transactional speed)
2. New QA metrics (you can't score "how quickly did they close a billing dispute" on complex emotional calls)
3. New coaching infrastructure (you need AI analyzing the hard calls, not just the easy ones)
