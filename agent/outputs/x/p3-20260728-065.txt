Post-call coaching has a 48-72 hour feedback loop built into its design.

The agent handles a difficult call. Supervisor reviews it the next day or the day after. Feedback arrives two days later. The agent's next 40 calls happen in that gap with no correction.

In 2026, that's not a process limitation. It's a design choice that contact centers are actively choosing to stop making.

Real-time AI guidance during live calls is now deployed at scale. The model listens to the conversation, detects what's happening — customer frustration, compliance trigger, upsell opportunity, escalation risk — and surfaces guidance to the agent in real time. Not after the call. During it.

What this actually changes in practice:

**Feedback loop compression**: 48-72 hours → 0 seconds. The agent adjusts mid-conversation, not mid-next-week. Behavioral correction happens in the moment of the behavior, which is when it actually works.

**Coverage at scale**: A supervisor can monitor 2-3 live calls simultaneously. Real-time AI covers 100% of concurrent calls, 100% of the time. The agent on call #12 of their shift gets the same guidance quality as the agent on call #1.

**Compliance in real time**: When a regulatory disclosure needs to happen and the conversation is heading toward close without it, the AI surfaces the prompt before the call ends. Not in a compliance audit three weeks later.

**Upsell detection**: Sentiment and intent signals that trained humans catch maybe 30-40% of the time are detected consistently. Not every detected signal converts — but the agent now knows the signal is there.

The performance data on real-time guidance is consistent across deployments I've tracked: average handle time drops 20-25%, first contact resolution improves 8-15%, CSAT scores improve 0.3-0.5 points. The mechanism isn't AI doing the work — it's AI giving the agent better inputs during the 2-5 minutes that matter.

The tension is in the agent experience. Real-time guidance that's poorly calibrated feels like surveillance. Agents notice when the AI flags things incorrectly. The implementation failures I see are mostly in calibration — guidance that triggers too frequently, on too many edge cases, or contradicts how experienced agents actually know to handle a situation.

The contact centers succeeding with this aren't treating it as monitoring software. They're treating it as a real-time coaching system with agents as the end user, not supervisors. That distinction determines whether it improves performance or increases attrition.

At Ender Turing, this is the operational layer that's moved from differentiator to expectation. The 48-72 hour feedback loop was a constraint of the technology available in 2021. In 2026, keeping that loop is choosing inefficiency.
