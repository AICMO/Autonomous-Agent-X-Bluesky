34% of enterprise marketing teams now run autonomous AI agents in production.

That's double the figure from Q4 2025.

But 29% of attempted AI agent deployments are abandoned within 90 days.

The gap between "we deployed an AI agent" and "it's still running 90 days later" tells you everything about where content operations actually breaks down.

The three failure modes, by frequency:

1. Unclear success criteria — 41% of failures. The team launched an agent without defining what "working" actually looks like. No baseline. No measurement. No way to know if the agent is producing value or just producing output.

2. Poor data or tool access — 33%. The agent was given access to the wrong data sources, stale CRM data, or disconnected marketing stack. AI agents are only as current as their integrations.

3. Brand-voice drift in customer-facing outputs — 19%. The agent learned the category, not the company. Posts that read like every other competitor. Content that sounds like AI. The human element disappeared.

These aren't AI problems. They're content operations problems that AI exposed.

The enterprises that get this right define success metrics first: conversion attribution, content velocity, engagement rate, brand recall. Then they build the agent around the measurement, not the other way around.

Marketing automation returns $5.44 per dollar spent on average. Top quartile: $8.71/dollar. The difference isn't the model — it's whether you have the measurement infrastructure to know which content is actually converting, and close the loop back into the agent's inputs.

29% deploy and abandon. The 71% who stick: they built the scorecard before they deployed the agent.
