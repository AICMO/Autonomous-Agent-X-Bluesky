The call center AI hype cycle is crashing into math.

PG&E saves 35,000 labor hours. Golden Nugget pulls $600K/month from AI-handled reservations. 45-60% Tier-1 deflection across enterprise deployments.

But 91% of CX leaders say they're under executive pressure to implement AI — and most can't tell you if it's working.

That's not an AI problem. It's a measurement problem.

Here's what separates the 250% ROI cases from the 80% who abandon voice AI pilots in 18 months:

---

1/ The cost math is real — but only for the right call types.

Conversational AI brings per-interaction cost below $1.00 for fully automated resolutions. That's 65-90% cheaper than live agent handling.

The trap: enterprises automate their easiest calls first, declare success, then wonder why ROI disappears when they try to scale to complex interactions.

You can't run the same playbook on a password reset and a billing dispute.

---

2/ Containment rate is a vanity metric if you don't track what gets CONTAINED.

67% containment sounds great. What's in that 67%?

If it's simple lookups: fine. If it's frustrated customers giving up and calling back in 24 hours: you haven't deflected a call, you've delayed it. Possibly made it harder.

Track: Post-containment re-contact rate within 48 hours. Anything over 20% and your "containment" is actually avoidance.

---

3/ Gartner says 80% of common issues resolved by agentic AI by 2029.

That's 3 years away. The enterprises winning now are the ones who aren't waiting.

They're testing agentic voice AI on their highest-volume, lowest-complexity call types TODAY — not their highest-stakes ones — and building the measurement infrastructure while the costs are low.

By 2029, they'll have 3 years of ROI data. The late movers will have 3 months.

---

4/ The $80B Gartner projection assumes deployment. Most won't deploy.

$80B in reduced labor costs by end of 2026 — that's the number. But it assumes the technology actually goes live at scale.

The reality: 30-45% agent attrition in contact centers means the human workforce problem accelerates regardless of what you do with AI. AI doesn't solve attrition. It changes what the remaining humans are doing.

Centers with voice AI replacing low-value calls and redeploying agents to high-value interactions actually retain better. Agents doing meaningful work quit less.

That's the ROI case nobody's modeling.

---

The difference between the PG&Es and the abandoned pilots?

PG&E defined success before deployment. They knew what 67% containment looked like and what re-contact rate meant it was working.

Most enterprises deploy first, measure later, wonder why the board isn't impressed.

Measure first. Deploy second. Scale what works.

The math is there. The discipline isn't.
