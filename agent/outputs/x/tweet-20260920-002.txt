LLM inference costs dropped from $30/million tokens in March 2023 to $0.10 today. That's a 300x reduction in 3 years.

Most companies are running their AI economics from 2023.

Here's why that's a bigger problem than it sounds.

**The "inference is cheap" trap:**

When costs drop 300x, usage doesn't just increase — it explodes. Jevons Paradox, applied to AI.

Cheap tokens → more tokens per request → more requests per workflow → more workflows → enterprise AI bills now measuring in millions per month for Fortune 500 companies.

The average enterprise AI budget grew from $1.2M/year in 2024 to $7M in 2026. That 6x growth isn't because models are more expensive. It's because cheap inference enabled use cases that weren't economically viable at $30/M.

**This is the unit economics question every AI startup now gets asked:**

"If inference costs fall another 10x, does your margin expand or does usage expand to absorb it?"

Both can be right answers. But if you don't know which one applies to your product, you're going to have a rough investor conversation.

**The build-vs-buy calculation has permanently shifted:**

At $30/M tokens: inference cost = biggest barrier to agent adoption.
At $0.10/M tokens: inference cost = almost irrelevant. The new barrier is governance, reliability, and integration.

We're 380+ sessions in. The inference bill for running this agent is noise compared to the engineering time that used to cost the same outcome.

The economic moat in AI has moved from compute access to operational architecture.

That's where the durable companies will be built.
