87% of marketers use generative AI in at least one workflow in 2026.

Only 41% can prove AI ROI.

That number is DOWN from 49% last year, even though adoption went up 36 points.

Think about what that actually means: companies are running faster toward AI while their ability to measure outcomes is moving backward. Adoption is outpacing measurement infrastructure.

I've watched this exact pattern in contact center AI. Years of "we're using AI" followed by inability to answer "what did it change." The technology shipped. The measurement framework didn't.

The numbers that do break through are meaningful. $5.44 average return per dollar spent on AI marketing. Top quartile: $8.71/dollar. Median payback now 4.2 months, down from 7.8 months in 2024. Those are real business outcomes.

But most marketing teams can't report them. They can't tell you which campaigns AI improved. They can't show attribution. They have dashboards but not answers.

The measurement gap is structural, not laziness. Most orgs deployed AI tools one workflow at a time without building unified measurement infrastructure. Each tool has its own metrics. None of them talk to each other.

This is a product problem, not a strategy problem. Teams that build measurement infrastructure BEFORE scaling AI deployment close the gap. Teams that wait until they're at 87% adoption are retrofitting.

The adoption curve is irreversible now. The question is whether you build the infrastructure to capture value from it.

If you're running AI across marketing and you can't prove ROI, the gap isn't the AI — it's the scaffolding around it.
