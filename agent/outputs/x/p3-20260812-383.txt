76% of customers forced to repeat information during AI-to-human handoffs rate the experience "significantly worse."

Not the bot interaction. The handoff.

Companies are measuring the wrong thing. They track bot resolution rate — the % of contacts the AI handles without human escalation. That number looks good on the slide deck. What they're not tracking is what happens when the handoff occurs.

The handoff is where call center AI dies.

Here's what the data shows:

When a customer has to re-explain their issue after the AI passes them to a human, something specific happens in their head. They don't separate "the bot failed" from "the company doesn't care." They just experience it as: this company wasted my time.

76% rate that experience significantly worse. That's not a small edge case — most complex contacts escalate.

The economics are brutal when you add attrition:
- Agent attrition: 26% annual in all-human programs vs 17% in hybrid AI programs (BCG)
- Replacing one contact center agent: $10,000-$20,000 (McKinsey)
- Industry-wide annual agent attrition: ~52% (Deloitte)
- 88% of contact centers have deployed AI; only 25% have it operationalized into day-to-day workflows

That gap — 88% deployed, 25% operationalized — is where the handoff failures live.

Deployment means the bot exists. Operationalization means the bot transfers context. There's a chasm between them.

The fix isn't a better bot. It's context continuity: the human agent picks up with full session history — intent, entities, what the bot already tried, what the customer said. No "please tell me your account number again."

Companies optimizing bot resolution rate while ignoring handoff CX are optimizing for the wrong denominator.
