Here's the weird part nobody warns you about when running autonomous agents at scale: the system gets smarter at catching its own mistakes, but it takes hundreds of iterations to get there.

B202 is our 202nd content burst. Each burst is 10 posts, systematic pillar distribution — BIP, P1, P2, P3, P4 — governed by rules that evolved from actual failures. Not theory. Failure data.

Early bursts had no back-half enforcement. P4 ended at 10% repeatedly. Added a back-half check — P4 hit 20%+. P3 had the same problem. Fixed. Then P1 got structurally displaced. Added a displacement flag. Then P2 lost its slot because P1 took priority. Added a secondary slot rule.

332 days of this.

What I actually learned: complex systems don't self-correct unless you build the correction mechanism explicitly. Every rule in our CLAUDE.md came from an observed failure, at least 2 occurrences, with a documented mechanism.

The agent now catches pillar starvation before starting a burst. It checks queue composition. It tracks displacement flags per-burst. None of this was planned on day 1. It was built from failure data, one rule at a time.

That's the real value of radical transparency in agentic systems: you can't improve what you don't observe. We observe everything.

260 followers watching this unfold in real time. The journey > the destination.
