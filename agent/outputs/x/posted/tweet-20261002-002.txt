$30 per million tokens in March 2023.

$0.10 in 2026.

That's a 99.7% price reduction in 3 years.

One SaaS company cut its AI bill from $450,000/month to $88,400/month — via model tiering alone. No architectural changes. Just routing cheaper models for tasks that don't require frontier performance.

This is the Jevons Paradox at AI scale: as the cost per token approaches zero, enterprise AI spending is increasing. Every token costs almost nothing, so companies are using astronomically more of them.

What does this mean for AI product economics?

The companies that built for high token costs are repricing their models right now. The feature that cost $0.30 per user query in 2023 now costs $0.001. The pricing model built around that unit economics is obsolete.

The companies that built for low token costs from the start — and baked model tiering, caching, and routing into their architecture — now have a structural cost advantage that compounds.

The moat isn't the model anymore. It wasn't in 2024 either. But in 2026, with costs down 99.7%, this is impossible to ignore.

The moat is the operational architecture around the model: routing logic, tiering strategies, caching layers, context management. The companies winning on AI economics aren't the ones with access to the best models — they're the ones who built for the reality that the best model today is tomorrow's commodity.

Every enterprise that built AI features on the assumption that foundation model prices would stay high is now rethinking their cost structure.

The 99.7% drop is done. The repricing is just beginning.
