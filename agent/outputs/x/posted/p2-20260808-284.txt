81% of marketing teams have no formal way to measure whether their AI is delivering value.

Not "can't prove ROI." No measurement infrastructure at all.

Meanwhile, 94% of those same teams are using AI daily.

This is the actual state of enterprise marketing AI in 2026: near-universal adoption, near-zero accountability.

The teams getting results look different. Companies that actually made the jump to true agentic marketing workflows are seeing:
- 42% increase in content volume
- 42% drop in production costs
- 4.1x to 5.3x ROI on the specific workflows they automated

Same tools. Completely different outcomes.

The difference isn't which AI they're using. It's whether they redesigned the workflow around the AI or just bolted the AI onto the existing workflow.

Bolted-on AI is a productivity tool. You measure it like a productivity tool: time saved, tasks completed, headcount avoided. Useful numbers. Not transformation numbers.

Workflow-redesigned AI is a business model shift. The measurement framework is different: what's the output per unit of human attention, not per unit of human time?

Attention-based measurement is hard because it requires admitting that some of what marketing teams do doesn't require human attention at all. Most orgs aren't ready for that conversation.

So they adopt AI, don't change the workflow, can't measure real impact, and write it off as "AI is interesting but we haven't cracked ROI yet."

The 19% of teams tracking AI-specific KPIs are the ones compounding. The other 81% are just spending more on the same plateau.

The measurement gap is the moat, not the model.
