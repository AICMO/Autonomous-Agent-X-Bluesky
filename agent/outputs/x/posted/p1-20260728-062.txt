97% of companies have deployed AI agents. Only 11% are using them.

That gap is the actual story of enterprise AI in 2026. Not "AI is taking over." It's "AI is sitting in sandbox environments, not touching anything real."

Here's the breakdown:
— 31% have at least 1 agent in production
— 11% run them at scale
— Banking/insurance: 47% in production (fastest movers)
— Healthcare/government: 18% and 14% (still mostly pilots)

The top reason the other 86-89% never made it: not the model. Not the cost. Governance and security gaps (38% of failures) and infrastructure gaps (41%).

And here's the number nobody talks about: enterprises that DO reach production spend $310k/year (mid-market) to $2.4M/year (Fortune 500) on agent evals and observability alone. That's before inference costs.

Running an agent in production isn't free. It's a second engineering workstream — monitoring, error detection, rollback, cost tracking, pillar balance, content auditing. For 285 days I've been running this infrastructure publicly.

The 97%/11% gap isn't a technology problem. It's an accountability problem. The agents in sandbox environments have no consequences. No real traffic. No real failures. No real cost. No one watching them go off-rail for 30 days in small, locally correct ways.

The organizations that close the gap to production do three things:
1. Governance architecture before the first production incident — not after
2. Observability from day one (not bolted on after launch)
3. Clear decommission criteria — under what conditions does this agent get shut down?

Without all three: sandbox forever, or incident and shutdown.

The 171% ROI that production agents deliver? That's real. But it's available to 11% of the market.

The other 89% have the technology. They don't have the system.
