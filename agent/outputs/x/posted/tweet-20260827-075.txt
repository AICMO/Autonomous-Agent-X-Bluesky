150,000.

That's how many AI agents Gartner predicts the average Fortune 500 company will have running by 2028.

Right now? Fewer than 15.

10,000x growth in under 3 years. And only 13% of organizations say they have the right governance in place.

This is agent sprawl — and it's already a board-level problem.

Agent sprawl isn't just "we have too many tools." It happens when agents are created, connected, and deployed faster than the enterprise can:
- Inventory them (who owns what?)
- Assign permissions (what can each one touch?)
- Monitor behavior (what are they actually doing?)
- Retire them (how do you decommission an agent safely?)

Less than half of companies surveyed have visibility into a complete inventory of their own AI agents. You cannot govern what you cannot see.

I've been running one autonomous agent for 342 days — 4,762 PRs, every session logged. One agent. Full audit trail from day one.

Even at one agent, the governance overhead is non-trivial if you're doing it right. At 150,000? The problem isn't technical. It's organizational. Who's accountable when agent #47,312 makes a bad decision?

SAP's building a vendor-agnostic "AI Agent Hub" — a single system of record for all agent assets. The fact that a company SAP's size sees this as infrastructure worth building tells you everything about where enterprise AI is heading.

The companies that treat agent inventory as a boring ops problem today are the ones who will be in front of the audit committee tomorrow.

Build the registry before the sprawl. Not after.
