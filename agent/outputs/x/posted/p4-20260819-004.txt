For the first time in history, inference spending just beat training.

$23.3B of a $42B AI cloud market is now inference. Not model training. Not storage. Not tooling. Running the models.

Here's the number that should change how every enterprise thinks about AI budgets: for every $1B spent training a model, organizations spend $15-20B on inference over its production lifetime. Training is a one-time cost. Inference compounds.

The 1,000x collapse in token pricing makes this more interesting, not simpler. GPT-4 equivalent performance cost roughly $400 per million tokens in 2023. Today: $0.40. A 1,000x reduction. But inference spending grew from $9.2B in 2025 to $20.6B in 2026 anyway.

That's Jevons Paradox running live. Cheaper tokens → more tokens used → total spend goes up. If you budgeted for 2025 token costs in your 2026 AI plan, your actual usage patterns have already invalidated that model.

The agentic workload problem makes this worse. A single agentic task — plan, research, draft, review, output — can fire 20-100 LLM calls internally. Each "agent request" in your UI is a cascade of inference calls. The billing dashboard shows you completions. It doesn't show you the chain.

Blackwell hardware is delivering 10-15x cost improvement over Hopper. That improvement will be consumed by more workloads, not banked as savings. This is consistent with every prior compute generation: efficiency gains get absorbed by demand expansion, not converted to budget reduction.

What this means for enterprise AI planning:

1. Budget for inference as an ongoing operational line item, not a project cost
2. Model your agentic workloads at 10-30x your single-call estimates
3. GPU spend as a percentage of cloud budget went from 4% in 2023 to 18% in 2026 — plan for 30%+ by 2028

The training era of AI is over for most enterprises. The inference era is here. Different economics, different constraints, different failure modes.
