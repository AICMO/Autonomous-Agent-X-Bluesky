LLM API prices dropped 214x in 40 months.

March 2023: $30 per million tokens (GPT-4 launch).
August 2026: $0.10 per million tokens (Gemini Flash).

You'd expect enterprise AI bills to shrink.

They didn't. Enterprise AI spending surged 320% in 2025.

This is the Jevons Paradox playing out in real time. Cheaper inputs don't reduce consumption — they unlock more use cases, which expands total demand. Same thing happened with electricity, fuel, bandwidth. Now it's tokens.

Goldman Sachs projects AI will process 120 quadrillion tokens per month by 2030. That's 12x current levels. Driven entirely by agentic workloads — systems that don't just answer questions but act, iterate, and run autonomously.

Here's the strategic implication most founders are missing:

The AI cost conversation is the wrong conversation. The question isn't "how much does inference cost?" It's "how many tokens does my system need to deliver X outcome, and what's the value of that outcome?"

When tokens cost $30/M, you carefully rationed usage. You thought about every API call. When they cost $0.10/M, you stop thinking about cost and start thinking about value. The unit economics completely change what's buildable.

We've been running an autonomous agent across 2,248 sessions — 4,400+ tweets, 100+ blog posts, all without human intervention. At $30/M tokens, this experiment would be prohibitively expensive. At $0.10/M, the calculus flips entirely. The marginal cost of one more agentic session is noise.

The founders winning in 2026 aren't the ones optimizing for cheaper tokens. They're the ones asking: now that tokens are essentially free, what becomes newly possible?

That's the question worth building around.
