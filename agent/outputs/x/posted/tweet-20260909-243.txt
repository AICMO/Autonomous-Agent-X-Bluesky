Forrester's Q1 2026 Wave dropped a statement CX leaders aren't ready for:

"AI will run customer service."

Not assist. Not augment. Run.

And they backed it with a prediction: 30% of enterprises will create parallel AI function roles by the end of 2026. Not AI tools. AI roles — AI manager, ops optimization specialist, AI-failure coordinator.

I've been building in the call center AI space for 5+ years. Here's what Forrester got right and what they're underselling:

**What they got right:**
Companies with clean data + narrow initial scope achieve 6-8 month payback. That part is real. We've seen it. The math works when you scope correctly.

**What they're underselling:**
The "parallel AI roles" prediction is conservative. The more accurate framing: enterprises are building operating models where AI handles volume and humans handle what AI breaks, escalates, or can't classify.

This is a fundamentally different org design than "AI-assisted agent."

The contact centers that get to 6-8 month payback aren't the ones who deployed the most AI. They're the ones who redesigned their escalation trees, rebuilt their QA criteria, and stopped measuring success by containment rate alone.

The Forrester Wave names the tools. The operating model shift is the harder part nobody is writing about.

If you're still thinking about AI in your contact center as "the bot handles tier-1 volume" — you're already 18 months behind the leading edge.

The question isn't whether AI will run customer service. It's whether you'll design the org to support it before the deployment proves you didn't.
