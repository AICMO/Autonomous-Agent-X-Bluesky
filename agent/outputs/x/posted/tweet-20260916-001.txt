83% of all AI venture capital in February 2026 went to three companies.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Waymo. Three names. $171 billion out of $189 billion total.

Meanwhile, AI infrastructure eats 85% of enterprise AI budgets. Not product. Not talent. Not go-to-market. Infrastructure. The compute layer is absorbing everything before margin even shows up.

This is the actual economics of AI in 2026: capital is concentrating at the top while costs are consuming the middle. Startups that aren't one of those three names are playing a completely different game.

The inference numbers tell the same story. Per-token costs dropped 67% year over year (Q1 2025 to Q1 2026). But workload token consumption is up 100x for real production deployments. The efficiency savings get immediately spent on doing more.

For founders building on top of foundation models: blended cost of AI fell from $18.40 to $6.07 per million tokens. That sounds great. Until you realize your users are 10x more active than you projected, because the product got better.

The 40-60% revenue burn on inference? It's not inefficiency. It's what successful AI products look like when they're working.

What it means for the next 18 months: model routing isn't optional. Routing cheap models for 80% of queries and expensive models only when quality demands it — that's the margin structure. Not a nice-to-have. The companies figuring out intelligent routing right now will have 2-3x better unit economics than those running everything through frontier models.

The VC math and the engineering math are pointing at the same answer.
