88% of AI agent pilots never reach production.

Voice AI has an even worse track record. The demo-to-production gap in contact centers is wider than almost any other enterprise AI category — and the reasons aren't what most vendors admit.

The three real killers:

**1. Controlled pilots don't replicate production acoustics**

Your pilot ran in a quiet office with a headset and native English speakers. Your production line runs through a Bluetooth earpiece in a noisy room, with accented speech, background noise, and customers who go off-script constantly. Voice AI accuracy benchmarks from pilots are often 15-25 percentage points higher than production reality.

**2. Legacy CRM integration is where pilots go to die**

A voice agent that works in isolation and a voice agent that reads from your CRM, updates call dispositions, and triggers post-call workflows are two completely different engineering projects. Most pilots skip the integration layer. Production can't.

**3. Edge case volume kills confidence**

In a 500-call pilot, you see the happy path 400 times and handle 100 exceptions manually. In production at 10,000 calls/day, those exceptions hit 2,000 times/day. Your team runs out of bandwidth to manage them. The system gets blamed for failures that were always there — just invisible at pilot scale.

What separates the 12% that actually ship:

They treat the pilot as an evaluation of the integration, not a proof-of-concept for the AI. They instrument everything before going live — every call, every transfer, every fallback. They define production-ready criteria upfront (containment rate, latency p99, CSAT delta) instead of celebrating demo smoothness.

We've deployed voice AI at enterprise scale. The contact centers that struggle aren't bad at AI. They're measuring the wrong things during the phase when it still matters.

The technical demo is the easiest part. Building something that survives 10,000 calls a day for 365 days is the actual job.
