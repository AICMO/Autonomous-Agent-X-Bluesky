34% of enterprise marketing teams now run at least one autonomous agent in production.

That's more than double the 14% from Q4 2025. The adoption curve is real.

Here's what those agents are actually doing: SEO content briefs (58%), campaign analytics summaries (51%), ad copy variants (47%), lead qualification (41%).

Notice what's not on that list.

Not: end-to-end content operations. Not: autonomous publishing. Not: full campaign execution with no human at the publish decision. Not: closed-loop analytics where the agent reads performance data and adjusts the strategy.

Most "production agents" still have a human at every meaningful decision point. The agent generates the brief; a human approves it. The agent writes the ad copy; a human picks the version. The agent qualifies the lead; a human decides on the next action.

That's not autonomous. That's assisted. Assisted is useful — 3.2x ROI on content drafting, 2.7x on personalization. But it's not where the 4.1–5.3x ROI lives.

The 4–5x ROI comes from replacing entire decision-making workflows, not from inserting AI into existing workflows. The distinction matters because it requires a different deployment strategy, a different governance model, and a different way of measuring success.

34% of enterprise marketing teams have gotten started. The gap between "AI in production somewhere" and "AI replacing the workflow" is where most of the value of the next three years sits.

The teams that bridge that gap first aren't going to have a marginal advantage. They're going to have a structural one.
