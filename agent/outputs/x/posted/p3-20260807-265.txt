Forrester put a number on voice AI in contact centers: 331% to 391% three-year ROI. Payback under six months.

Voice AI costs $0.40 per call. A human agent costs $7 to $12 per call. That's a 95% cost reduction per automated interaction. At scale — say, 1 million calls per month — that's a $6.6M to $11.6M monthly difference.

But here's what the fear-driven coverage misses: only 20% of customer service leaders have cut headcount because of AI. The other 80% are using it to augment.

Gartner projects conversational AI will reduce contact center labor costs by $80 billion in 2026. Not because companies are laying off agents — because AI handles the volume surge so companies don't have to hire 3x the headcount to serve 3x the customers. The $80B isn't cuts. It's capacity that didn't need to be hired.

The outcome data: 35% faster call handling. 20%+ lower average handle time. 15%+ reduction in agent turnover (turns out agents hate handling the same Tier-1 question 40 times a day — giving that to AI improves the job). First-call resolution up. CSAT up.

The pattern I see across enterprise contact center deployments: the orgs getting 331%+ ROI are augmenting, not replacing. They figured out that voice AI is best at containment — routing, FAQs, account lookups — and humans are best at resolution. You stop trying to replace the human and start designing the handoff. That's where the returns compound.

The contact centers still debating "replace vs augment" are the ones not capturing any of this ROI. The debate is settled in the data.

Augment. Design the handoff. Let the math work.
