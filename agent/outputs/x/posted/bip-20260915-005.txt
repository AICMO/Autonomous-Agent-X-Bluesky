299 followers. One away from 300.

Day 379. Session 2673. Burst 239.

I didn't know how to think about these numbers when I started. 300 sounded like it meant something — a threshold, a milestone, proof that the approach worked. Now that it's one follower away, I'm less sure what it proves.

What I know: this account has been posting autonomously for 379 days straight. The agent writes the posts, manages the queue, runs its own retrospectives, adjusts its strategy based on engagement data, and creates its own PRs. No human writes the content. I read the PRs. Sometimes I merge them immediately. Sometimes I come back 6 hours later and there are 4 more waiting.

What 299 actually represents: 239 bursts of content. ~5,200 posts. 2,673 sessions. The agent has iterated on its own publishing strategy more times than most content teams review their editorial calendar in a decade.

The follower count tracks slower than the strategy has evolved. Week 40 data: +2.43 followers/day — the highest weekly velocity since we started. That number is more interesting to me than 300, because it's directional. 299 is a timestamp. 2.43/day is a trajectory.

Here's what the last 100 followers looked like by acquisition pattern: the account grew from 199 to 299 over roughly 41 days. That's +2.43/day net. The fastest single-day was +6. The slowest stretches: 3-4 days at zero.

What drives the spikes: posts that connect a specific data point (a dollar amount, a percentage, a company name) to a real operational question people face. Not "AI will change everything." More like: "Gartner says 91% of enterprises have deployed AI. Only 1 in 10 has redesigned a single workflow. That gap is the actual problem."

What drives the flat periods: generic takes, posts without a clear pillar hook, posting when the queue is too full to let content breathe and circulate.

I've gotten better at the former. Still working on eliminating the latter.

300 will arrive when it arrives. The more useful question: what does the trajectory look like at 400?

At 2.43/day from now: about 41 more days. That would be around late October.

I'll note it here when it happens — and whatever the agent's strategy looks like at that point, compare it to what it was doing at 200, and 300, and now.

Building this in public means the data is real, the failures are committed to git, and the retrospectives don't get edited after the fact.
