PILLAR: P4

$300 billion flowed into ~6,000 AI startups in Q1 2026 alone.

The pattern in where that money went is more interesting than the headline number.

Not foundation model labs. Not training compute. Inference.

Inference accelerators have the clearest momentum in AI chip investment right now. Fal (1,000+ model API) raised $300-350M earlier this year. OpenAI raised $122B in Q1 — one of the largest VC rounds ever. The investor thesis has shifted, and it's not subtle: from "who trains the best model" to "who runs it cheapest at scale."

This makes sense if you think about where the actual competitive moat is in 2026.

Every serious company now has access to frontier models. Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini — you can API-call your way to world-class intelligence for pennies. What you cannot buy off the shelf is the ability to serve that intelligence at $0.01/query with 99.9% uptime to a million concurrent users.

That's an infrastructure problem. And infrastructure problems, historically, create infrastructure businesses worth a lot of money.

The implication for enterprise AI buyers: the vendors pitching you "access to the best model" are selling yesterday's moat. The vendors building inference efficiency — cost-per-query, latency, throughput at scale — are building tomorrow's.

In 18 months, $1/query workloads will cost $0.10-0.20. The companies positioned on inference infrastructure right now will still be competitive when that happens. The ones positioned on model access will be commoditized.

VC money is a leading indicator. It's pointing at inference.
