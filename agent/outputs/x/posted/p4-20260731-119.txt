The LLM pricing collapse of 2026 is the most underrated business story of the year.

A full suite of AI background agents that cost $50,000+/month in 2023 now costs under $1,000/month. That's not incremental improvement. That's a structural shift in what's buildable.

The numbers:
- Cheapest LLM API today: $0.03 per million input tokens (Qwen3 Flash)
- OpenAI o3: dropped 80% in a single pricing update
- 90%+ cost reduction over 3 years, with capability improvements happening simultaneously
- $510B in global VC funding in H1 2026 — over 70% flowing into AI

Here's what this means if you're building products:

The "AI is too expensive" objection is dead. Not dying — dead. Any product that failed the ROI calculation at 2023 prices passes it now by a factor of 10-50x.

The new competitive moat isn't "we use AI." Every company uses AI. The moat is proprietary data, unique workflows, and distribution — the things that don't get cheaper with infrastructure.

But here's the underrated implication for AI startups taking that $510B in funding:

You're raising capital to build on infrastructure that's getting 3-5x cheaper per year. That changes burn calculations, runway projections, and what "profitable" looks like. The models you plan against for unit economics today will be 3-5x cheaper by the time you hit Series B.

Build for the infrastructure costs of 2027, not 2026.

The window between "AI is too expensive" and "everyone's doing it" is closing faster than most roadmaps account for.

What's your moat when the compute is free?
