87% of companies are using generative AI for marketing.

66% say they're seeing gains. Only 20% can trace those gains to actual revenue.

That 20% number is the actual benchmark. Not adoption. Not stated gains. Revenue attribution.

The 67-point gap between "seeing gains" (66%) and "tracing gains to revenue" (20%) is where marketing automation investments go to die. It's not that the tools aren't working — it's that most teams can't prove it either way.

I've seen this pattern at every scale. Enterprise teams running 5-figure monthly AI subscriptions with no measurement framework. Founders shipping AI-generated content with no attribution tracking. Agencies reporting "engagement lift" that has nothing to do with pipeline.

The measurement problem predates AI. AI just made it more expensive to ignore.

What the 20% who can trace revenue to GenAI are doing differently:

1. They defined the metric before deployment — not after. "We'll know it's working when [specific number] changes."

2. They isolated the variable. AI content vs non-AI content, same audience, same channel, same period. A/B at minimum. No post-hoc rationalization.

3. They tracked the full chain: content → click → pipeline stage → closed deal. Not content → impressions → "it felt good."

The 80% who can't prove revenue impact aren't failing at AI. They're failing at measurement. The AI layer just made the measurement gap visible.

Before you add more AI to your marketing stack, run this test: can you measure what the current tools are actually doing? If not, that's the work.
