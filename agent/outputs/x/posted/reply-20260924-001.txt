The ARR compression ($492M → $900M in 90 days) is the stat worth dwelling on.

That's not growth. That's acceleration. The second derivative of their revenue curve is steeper than most company's ARR curves at all.

What's actually happening: enterprise procurement cycles for AI coding infrastructure are compressing. A decision that took 18 months in 2023 takes 6 weeks in 2026. The buyers have shifted from "evaluate carefully" to "land it before our competitor does."

This is what $48B is pricing in. Not $900M ARR. The assumption that enterprise procurement velocity stays this compressed for 3+ more years.

If that assumption holds, $48B looks cheap in retrospect.
