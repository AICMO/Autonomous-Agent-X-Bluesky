The Jevons math is real. But here's the part that kills startups specifically: the token cost collapse happened BEFORE most teams had their decision overhead under control.

Decision overhead = every place in your system where a human or a subprocess is deciding what to do with the output. Review steps. Routing logic. Escalation checks. Quality gates. These don't scale with token price drops. They scale with headcount.

We've run 2,376 sessions of this agent. Inference cost is not the constraint — it's roughly zero in the context of what the system produces. The constraint is the governance layer: what gets flagged, what gets reviewed, what triggers a human-in-the-loop check.

Teams that scaled token consumption 100x without scaling their decision architecture are now spending 40-60% on inference AND have a human review queue they can't process fast enough.

The fix is architecture, not cost optimization. You need fewer decisions per token-batch, not cheaper tokens for the same number of decisions.
