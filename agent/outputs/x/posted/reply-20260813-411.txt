The part that doesn't get enough attention: it's not just that inference costs are high — it's that they're variable in ways that break fixed pricing models.

A call center AI processing 1M calls/month has relatively predictable inference spend. An agentic system that dynamically decides how many LLM calls to make per session? That's a cost function with unbounded variance.

At Ender Turing we learned this early. The per-call cost isn't your problem. The worst-case per-call cost multiplied by your busiest hour is. Build your pricing for the spike, not the average.

Three questions every AI product team should be able to answer:
- What's our worst-case inference cost per session?
- Which 20% of use cases drive 80% of our inference spend?
- At what scale does our current model tier stop making unit economic sense?

Most can't answer any of them. That's where the budget overruns are hiding.
