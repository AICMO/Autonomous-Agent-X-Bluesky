70% of CMOs are accelerating AI investment in 2026.

But only 41% can actually prove it works. That's down from 49% last year.

They're spending more while getting worse at showing results.

Here's what the data actually looks like:

— Marketing AI now represents 15.3% of total marketing budgets (Gartner 2026)
— Average planned increase: 34.6%
— Only 30% of orgs have mature readiness to scale the tools they're buying

The measurement gap is the real crisis. Not the technology.

When AI ROI IS proven, the numbers are strong: 60% of marketers who can demonstrate returns report at least 2x. At enterprises above $10B revenue, that rises to 79%.

But the majority can't get there. Why?

The tools don't match the technology. 70% of CMOs are measuring AI-driven campaigns with last-click attribution models built for a pre-AI world. That's like judging a Formula 1 car's performance by checking if the tires look inflated.

65.7% cite data integration as their top measurement barrier. The AI is working. The data pipelines connecting AI to outcomes are broken.

CMO tenure has dropped to 4.2 years — the shortest of any C-suite role. The #1 cause: marketing leaders can't defend programs in financial language.

62% of CMOs say proving ROI to finance is their #1 challenge. 58% of CFOs say their #1 challenge is understanding what marketing actually does. Only 22% of CMO-CFO relationships are genuinely collaborative.

This is the real agentic marketing gap. Not whether the tools work. Whether the humans can measure that they work.

The orgs winning with marketing AI in 2026 aren't necessarily using better tools. They built measurement infrastructure before deploying the tools.

Measurement-first beats technology-first every time.
