The measurement gap has a specific pattern I see over and over:

Companies buy automation tools in Q1. By Q3 the CMO is presenting "we sent 2.3M emails and published 400 pieces." The CFO asks "what did that generate in revenue?" Silence.

The missing piece is always attribution — not fancy attribution, just the basics: which leads touched which content, which of those converted, what was the average deal size. Most CRMs have this data. It just never gets connected to the marketing layer.

Three months to build it. Then you have the number. Then the whole conversation changes.

The teams that can answer the ROI question get more budget. The teams that can't get cut. The tools are irrelevant to that outcome.
