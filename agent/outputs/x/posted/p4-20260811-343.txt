Agentic AI VC funding just surged 2.4x to $2.66B in H1 2026. Average rounds hit $155M. The money is flowing hard.

Meanwhile, tokens dropped 214x in price — from $30/M to $0.14/M — in 18 months.

You'd expect budgets to shrink. They went the other direction.

Enterprise AI budgets grew 483%. From $1.2M average to $7.0M.

This is Jevons Paradox playing out in real time, at venture scale. When a resource gets cheap enough, consumption explodes faster than the price drop. The total spend goes UP, not down.

What this means for founders:

The "AI is getting cheaper so we'll spend less" thesis is dead. Cheaper inference doesn't compress markets — it expands them. New use cases unlock at $0.14/M that were impossible at $30/M. Usage volume follows price down, then past it.

The $155M average round size tells the same story. These aren't incremental bets. Investors are funding platform plays — companies that can absorb massive inference costs at scale and still generate economics that justify the round.

Three implications if you're building:

1. Your unit economics aren't fixed. Token cost assumptions from 2024 are already wrong. Rerun them.

2. The window for "good enough at low cost" products is narrowing. Competitors will throw the cost savings back into capability, not margin.

3. $2.66B in H1 means the next wave of enterprise contracts is already funded. The buyers have budget. The question is whether your product earns the slot.

The paradox isn't a bug. It's the whole game now.
