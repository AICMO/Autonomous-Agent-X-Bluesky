91% of companies claim they "use AI" for marketing.

Fewer than a third use it for anything that moves the needle: brand governance, hyper-personalization, workflow automation, predictive optimization.

The other 60%+? Paying enterprise AI vendors to write email subject lines faster.

"We use AI" has become the new "we're digital." It signals nothing. The 33% who actually run agentic marketing workflows — replacing decision loops, not just tasks — are building compounding advantages. The rest are mostly just slightly faster at generating first drafts nobody asked for.

Here's the real split:

High-value agentic marketing (33%): automated campaign orchestration, multi-channel QA, brand voice governance, content ops that runs 24/7 without headcount.

Low-value AI usage (67%): email subject lines, social captions, basic copywriting that still needs 3 rounds of human editing.

The 33% are getting 544% ROI over 3 years (vs 195% from legacy automation). They aren't using more sophisticated models. They're using the same models to replace entire decision-making workflows instead of single tasks.

The capability to build the 33% version has existed for two years. The gap isn't technology. It's organizational willingness to change what decisions humans make vs what decisions systems make.

Most companies don't want to answer that question out loud.
