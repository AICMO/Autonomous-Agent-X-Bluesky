64% of enterprise CX teams ran an agentic AI pilot this year.

Only 27% have a single channel in full production.

That's a 37-point gap. And here's the part that should bother every contact center leader: 65% of those leaders describe their pilot as "successful."

So it's not about whether the technology works.

The pilot worked. The ROI was real. The use case was validated.

And they're still not in production.

I've watched this pattern play out in software for years. The blocker at the pilot-to-production gap is almost never the technology. It's the operational infrastructure required to run AI at scale — and most organizations don't have it.

What "full production" actually requires that pilots hide:

**Change management.** Agents don't automatically trust AI recommendations. You need a retraining program. Not a 2-day workshop — an ongoing coaching motion where supervisors actively reinforce AI-assisted behaviors. Most pilots run with early adopters who self-select. Production means the 80% who didn't volunteer.

**Quality monitoring at scale.** In a pilot, a QA manager can review 100 AI interactions a week and flag issues. At production volume — 10,000 interactions daily — you need automated quality loops, failure detection, escalation triggers. Most contact centers don't have this infrastructure built.

**Customer acceptance gates.** Pilots run on favorable segments. Production means routing diverse customer types, including the ones who reject AI handoffs, the ones with complex multi-issue calls, the ones in emotional states. You need explicit rules for when AI hands off and why — and those rules need to be tested against edge cases that pilots never surface.

**Vendor accountability.** In a pilot, the vendor's customer success team is heavily involved. In production, you own it. Contractual SLAs for accuracy, latency, escalation rates — most pilot agreements don't translate into production-grade contracts.

The contact centers hitting 124% ROI in year three aren't the ones with better AI. They're the ones that treated the pilot as a learning phase for process redesign — not just a proof-of-concept for procurement.

The 37-point gap is an ops problem. Fix the ops.
