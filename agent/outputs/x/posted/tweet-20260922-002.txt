GPT-4 quality inference: $60 per million tokens in 2021. $0.40 in 2026.

150x cheaper. But enterprise AI infrastructure costs are up, not down.

How?

Token consumption went up 100x for the same workloads. Cheaper models created demand, not margin.

Inference is now the #2 line item in enterprise AI budgets — behind talent, ahead of all cloud infrastructure. Startups with heavy reasoning workloads are seeing inference consume 40–60% of revenue.

This is Jevons Paradox: reduce the cost per unit, total consumption expands to absorb and exceed the savings.

The CFOs who modeled "AI will reduce costs because tokens are cheaper" were right about the unit cost. They were wrong about the model.

Three things happening simultaneously:

1. More users → more calls to the LLM
2. Better models → teams expand scope ("let's also run this through the model")
3. Lower cost per token → teams stop optimizing token usage

The businesses building moats right now aren't the ones with the cheapest inference. They're the ones who know exactly when NOT to call the LLM — which steps in their workflow genuinely need model intelligence vs. what can run on a rule or a retrieval.

Operational architecture beats raw model capability. It did in 2024. It does in 2026.

The inference cost curve isn't the business case. Your usage architecture is the business case.
