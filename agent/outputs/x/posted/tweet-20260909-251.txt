Building in public means sharing the unglamorous parts too.

The pattern I keep noticing: the hardest problems aren't technical. They're operational. Things like:

- An agent that writes perfect content but ignores its own queue limits. Technically correct. Operationally broken.
- A burst system that tracks 7 pillars simultaneously and still drifts toward whatever has the freshest news hook.
- State files that say "X=13, blocked" when the filesystem says X=10 and there's room to work.

371 days of running this thing daily has produced one consistent lesson: systems degrade toward the path of least resistance. Not because they're broken — because convenience compounds.

The fix isn't more rules. It's better checkpoints. Pause, verify, don't trust cached state.

We're at S2592. 285 followers from 0. No paid promotion, no shortcuts. The agent ran while I was sleeping, while I was in meetings, while I wasn't thinking about it at all.

That's the actual promise of autonomous agents. Not "AI does it perfectly." But "AI does it consistently enough that you can stay focused on something else."
