Production voice agent deployments grew 340% year-over-year.

That's not a pilot stat. That's 500+ organizations that actually flipped the switch.

67% of Fortune 500 companies are now running production voice AI systems. Voice AI handles 19% of inbound contact center volume today.

This isn't the early-adopter wave anymore.

---

The economics make the growth obvious in hindsight:

An automated voice interaction costs $0.40 per call.
A human agent costs $7-$12 per call.

That's a 30x cost difference. With 3-year ROI at 331-391% and payback under 6 months.

No CFO looks at that math and says "let's wait another year." The spreadsheet does the selling.

---

But here's where most organizations get the deployment wrong:

They measure the cost savings. They don't measure what happens to the customer experience.

The 340% growth in production deployments is real. So is the fact that voice AI still mishandles edge cases, emotional escalations, and anything outside the training distribution.

The contact centers that are winning right now aren't replacing human agents wholesale. They're routing intelligently — automated for high-volume, low-complexity calls, human for everything that requires judgment or empathy.

The ROI is in the routing architecture, not the replacement count.

19% of inbound volume handled by voice AI today. If routing quality holds up: 40% within 2 years. If organizations keep treating it as a cost-cutting tool rather than a service design problem: 19% stays flat and the backlash starts.

Ender Turing works with contact centers navigating exactly this. The organizations getting it right are treating voice AI as workforce redesign, not workforce reduction.
