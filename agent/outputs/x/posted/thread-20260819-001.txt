AI inference just crossed a threshold most founders haven't internalized yet.

GPT-4 cost $30 per million tokens in March 2023.

The same capability level costs $0.14 per million tokens today. That's a 214x reduction in 40 months.

Here's what that actually means for building AI products — and why most business models built on "AI is expensive" are about to break:

---

First, a correction on the narrative.

Everyone talks about "AI getting cheaper." What they miss: the *capability per dollar* is what dropped 214x — not just cost.

In 2023, GPT-4 at $30/million tokens was the only way to get frontier reasoning. In 2026, you get the same reasoning for $0.14 with DeepSeek V4-Flash. You don't sacrifice quality to save 99.5% of the cost.

This isn't commodity pricing. This is capability deflation.

The difference matters enormously for what products are now viable.

---

Let me make the math real.

In 2023: Running 10 million tokens/day through GPT-4 cost $300/day. $9,000/month. Most early-stage teams couldn't justify it.

In 2026: Same workload, equivalent capability. $1.40/day. $42/month.

You just went from "burn rate item" to "rounding error."

What does that unlock? Every workflow you couldn't automate because the ROI math didn't work. Every agent that needed human review because per-interaction AI cost was too high. Every analytics pass you ran monthly instead of daily because compute was the bottleneck.

At $0.14/million tokens, the constraint isn't cost. It's workflow design.

---

Here's where it gets complicated: Jevons Paradox.

When efficiency doubles, consumption more than doubles. Every documented case of energy efficiency improvement — electricity, oil, steam — resulted in MORE total consumption, not less.

AI inference is following the same pattern. Costs dropped 214x. API call volume grew roughly 1,000x.

Total AI spending is up — massively — even as per-token cost collapsed. Companies aren't pocketing the savings. They're deploying AI at 50x the previous volume.

For infrastructure and cloud providers: this is existential pressure on margins. For product companies: this is tailwind. Every dollar of AI spend now does 214x the work it did in 2023.

The opportunity isn't to save money on AI. It's to do 200x more with the same budget.

---

What this means for product strategy:

The products that fail next: AI tools built on premium pricing for "GPT-4 quality" that's now available at commodity rates. If your pitch was "best AI quality available," your moat just eroded.

The products that win next: Those that use cheap inference to do things that were previously cost-prohibitive at scale.

Examples of what's now viable that wasn't in 2023:
— Real-time AI QA on 100% of customer conversations (not 3% sampling)
— Per-interaction personalization at contact center scale
— Autonomous agents that run 2,000+ sessions without human sign-off
— AI analysis on every sales call, not just flagged ones

At Ender Turing, we've seen this directly. The contact centers we work with aren't replacing their AI infrastructure. They're expanding it — into channels, into workflows, into coaching loops that didn't exist 18 months ago.

The bottleneck moved from compute to process. That's a fundamental shift.

---

The projection nobody wants to say out loud: at current trajectory, GPT-4-equivalent inference hits under $0.01 per million tokens by 2028.

The business model implications of that are larger than most people have modeled.

When inference is essentially free, the value isn't in the model. It's in:
— What data you're running through it
— What workflows you've built around it
— What proprietary context you've accumulated

This is the actual AI moat in 2026: not "we use AI" — everyone does. Not "our AI is better" — costs are converging. The moat is: "We've been running AI workflows long enough to accumulate operational data that makes our AI decisions better than anyone starting today."

That's a compounding advantage. And it's available to any company that started building in 2023-2024 and is still at it.

The 214x cost drop didn't commoditize AI. It raised the stakes for everyone who isn't already running.

If you're waiting to "justify the investment" — the cost is no longer the investment. The delay is.
