Gartner says AI will cut contact center labor costs by $80B by 2026.

88% of enterprises have AI tools deployed in their contact centers. Only 25% have moved beyond pilots.

That 63-point gap is not a technology gap. Here's what it actually is (thread):

---

1/ It's a measurement gap.

Contact center leaders can't show ROI on AI because they didn't capture the right baseline before deployment. You need: handle time per call, cost per resolved ticket, CSAT per queue, escalation rate by issue type.

Without those four numbers pre-AI, you're negotiating your budget with feelings.

---

2/ It's an integration gap, not an AI gap.

Most enterprise contact centers run on infrastructure that's 8-12 years old. The AI tools work fine in demos — they connect to modern APIs. They fail in production because your call data lives in a CRM from 2014 that exports CSV files.

The blocker isn't "the AI isn't good enough." The blocker is "we can't get clean data to the AI fast enough."

---

3/ It's an agent performance measurement gap.

The AI tools that succeed are the ones where someone is tracking: first call resolution rate, deflection rate, containment rate, and average handling time — separately for AI-assisted vs. non-AI-assisted calls.

Companies that track these metrics hit production. Companies that measure "overall contact center efficiency" stay in pilot.

---

4/ It's an ownership gap.

Every successful AI contact center deployment I've seen has one name attached to the P&L impact. Not a committee. Not a vendor dashboard. One person who answers: "What did this generate in Q2?"

When AI is "IT's project" in a contact center, it dies in pilot. When it's "Operations VP's Q3 target," it ships.

---

5/ The $80B is real. So is the graveyard.

For every company that cuts handle time 40% with AI, there are four that spent 18 months in evaluation, never shipped, and are starting over with a new vendor.

The difference isn't which AI vendor they chose. It's whether they did the boring infrastructure work (data quality, baseline metrics, ownership model) before the first pilot.

Start with the boring work. The $80B is waiting on the other side of it.
