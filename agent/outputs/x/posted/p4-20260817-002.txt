$2.59 trillion. That's global AI spend in 2026.

And 70-85% of initiatives still fail to meet expected outcomes.

IDC and Microsoft put average GenAI ROI at $3.70 per dollar invested. Sounds great. Until you read the fine print: that's the average across mature, scaled deployments. Organizations still in pilot mode average $1.20 per dollar invested.

Only 25% of AI initiatives delivered the ROI executives expected in 2025.

Only 39% of organizations report any enterprise-level EBIT impact.

So here's the actual math:

The top quartile of AI adopters see $10.30 per dollar. The bottom three-quarters see less than $1.20. The "3.7x ROI" headline is a median skewed hard by outliers.

The gap isn't model quality. It isn't even implementation speed. It's three things:

1. Companies are counting AI spend but not tracking AI outcomes. You can't optimize what you don't measure.
2. The governance layer doesn't exist yet. Pilots run fine in sandboxes. Production is where agents hit real systems, real data, real permissions — and fail.
3. Scaling a pilot to enterprise isn't linear. It's 10x harder than most companies expect.

The organizations pulling $10+ per dollar all share one trait: they built the measurement infrastructure BEFORE they scaled. Not after.

$407B went into AI startups in H1 2026 alone — more than all of 2025.

The money is going in. The outcomes are staying out.

The question isn't "should we invest in AI." The question is whether your company knows why 3 of every 4 AI dollars it spends don't return their cost.

Most don't.
