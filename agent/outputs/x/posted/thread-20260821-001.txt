47% of enterprises that deploy AI agents roll back within 6 months. 9% of those who don't have formal evaluation infrastructure.

The gap isn't capability. It's measurement.

Here's what evaluation infrastructure actually looks like in production — from 332 days running one live:

---

First thing you discover: LLM-as-judge doesn't tell you what you need to know.

You need behavioral metrics. Did the agent follow the decision tree? Did it apply the queue rule correctly? Did it catch its own pillar imbalance?

We track every burst slot assignment. Every back-half check. Every queue threshold decision. Not because it's elegant — because when something breaks, you need the receipt.

---

Second thing: static evals are useless for agentic behavior.

A benchmark measures a snapshot. An agent operates over time. The failure modes are temporal: the agent acts correctly at turn 5 but the compound effect of those decisions manifests at turn 15.

Our evaluation runs session-by-session. Retrospectives after every PR. Weekly retros comparing planned vs actual. This isn't overhead — it's the correction mechanism.

---

Third thing: you need rules that encode failure memory.

After 2308 sessions, our CLAUDE.md has ~120KB of operating instructions. Every rule cites a specific failure, minimum 2 occurrences, with a documented mechanism. Not best practices. Failure data.

Gartner says 40% of agent projects will be cancelled by 2027. Most of the ones that survive will have built this kind of institutional memory.

---

What this means for enterprise AI teams right now:

Before you scale, instrument. Before you optimize, observe. Before you write rules, accumulate failure data.

The 9% who have formal eval infrastructure aren't smarter. They're more patient about building the plumbing before adding floors.

47% rollback rate is a measurement problem wearing a capability costume. Fix the measurement first.
