Together AI just raised $800M at $8B+ valuation. Inference-only startup.

Zero models. Zero training. Just serving other companies' models at scale.

That valuation tells you everything about where the money is moving in AI in 2026.

Here's the math that's reshaping startup economics right now:

GPT-4-class inference: $30/M tokens in 2023. $0.50/M tokens today. That's a 98% cost drop in 3 years.

You'd think cheaper inference = worse business for inference providers. Wrong.

Jevons Paradox in action: as inference gets cheaper, usage explodes faster than prices fall. Inference Accelerator startups pulled in $4.03B in disclosed funding over 24 months. Groq, Baseten, Together AI — all racing to own the serving layer as the model layer commoditizes.

**The model is the cost center. Inference is the margin.**

What this means for enterprise buyers:

Inference costs are moving from fixed to variable — which means AI costs scale with success, not with investment. Every production AI agent you run, every agentic workflow, every AI call you make goes on the inference tab.

This is the shift that kills per-seat SaaS pricing models. Not because they're bad products — but because value delivery is no longer tied to seats. It's tied to compute cycles.

At Ender Turing, our inference spend scales directly with conversation volume. No conversation, no cost. More conversations, more value delivered, more cost. That's a fundamentally different economic model than a $75/seat/month license.

And it's why the inference layer is worth $8B+ before it's even built to scale.

The businesses that figure out inference cost management in 2026 will have a structural cost advantage for the next decade.

Those that don't will wonder why their AI costs grow faster than their revenue.
