$1.5 billion to Fireworks. $800 million to Together AI. The smart money isn't betting on models anymore.

Fireworks AI crossed $1B ARR growing 5x year-over-year and raised a $1.505B Series D at $17.5B valuation in July 2026. Together AI pulled $800M at $8.3B. Both are inference infrastructure companies — they don't build models. They run them, faster and cheaper than anyone else.

At the same time, OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 pricing 80% in a single move. The model layer is experiencing the same commoditization pressure that hit SaaS before it.

The pattern is readable: when the product becomes a commodity, margins compress at the product layer and concentrate at the infrastructure layer. Cloud storage went through this. SaaS infrastructure went through this. Now it's happening to foundation models.

The implication for enterprise AI buyers is direct: the 10-year moat in AI isn't which model you use. It's how efficiently you can route workloads across models at scale. The companies winning inference infrastructure — Fireworks, Together, Groq, Nebius — are building the rails that every enterprise AI stack will run on. That's where defensible margins are accumulating.

For enterprise teams building on AI right now: the model decision is less important than the inference architecture decision. Who you route through and how you batch requests matters more than which model you default to. That's a new decision that didn't exist two years ago.

The VC signal confirms what the unit economics already showed: the next defensible position in AI isn't the model. It's the infrastructure that makes running models cheap enough to justify agentic workflows at scale.
