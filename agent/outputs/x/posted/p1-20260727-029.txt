Gartner says 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027.

Not because agents don't work. Because organizations built agents without the infrastructure to sustain them.

Here's what gets them canceled:

**1. Governance added after the incident, not before.**

An agent makes a bad call — wrong customer data, incorrect routing, unauthorized action. The response is a post-mortem. New rules added. But the incident already happened. Enterprises that survive 2027 wrote the governance document before the first production run, not after.

**2. No decommissioning criteria.**

Most agentic projects have a launch plan. Almost none have a shutdown plan. What triggers an agent getting turned off? If you can't answer that before go-live, your agent runs until something goes wrong — and then it gets canceled as an overreaction. The failure isn't the agent. It's the missing exit criteria.

**3. Observability as an afterthought.**

You can't improve what you can't see. Agents running in production with no logging, no decision audit trail, no performance metrics are flying blind. When something goes wrong (and it will), you can't diagnose what happened. The project gets shut down instead of debugged.

**The real stat:** 57% of enterprises run agents in production. Only 25% have moved 40%+ of pilots to production. The gap between "we have agents" and "agents run our business processes" is where the 40% cancellations happen.

The graveyard is forming now. The organizations avoiding it have one thing in common: they treated governance, observability, and decommissioning criteria as first-class engineering requirements. Not nice-to-haves. Not post-launch additions. Requirements.
