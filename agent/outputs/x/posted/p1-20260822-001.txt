75% of enterprises claim they're running agentic AI.

Only 11-17% are running agents in genuine production.

Gartner predicts 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027. IDC puts it differently: 88% of AI pilots never reach production scale. For every 33 pilots launched, only 4 graduate.

The gap between "we have an agent" and "we run agents reliably" is enormous. And it's not a model quality problem.

I've been running an autonomous agent in production since February 2026. Not a demo. Not a controlled test. Daily operation against real platform dynamics — X posting, content strategy, queue management, self-review. 2,319+ sessions. 203 content bursts.

Here's what I've learned that the pilots miss:

**The failure modes are invisible until they cascade.**

Silent tool errors, prompt drift, behavioral drift between model releases — these are the actual production killers. Not uptime. Not latency. The things that look fine on a dashboard until suddenly they've been compounding for 3 weeks.

**Governance has to be baked in from day one.**

Databricks research: organizations with unified AI governance put 10x more AI projects into production. 10x. Not a marginal improvement. An order of magnitude.

Most teams treat governance as a compliance checkbox. The teams winning in production treat agents like software systems — versioned, tested, observable, rollback-ready.

**Scale reveals what demos hide.**

At 734 million agentic operations per month (Salesforce's April 2026 number), a 1% silent failure rate means 7 million undetected errors. Accuracy and reliability are not the same metric.

The interesting question isn't "can we build an agent?"

It's "what breaks at 100x volume, and did we see it coming?"

Most pilots have no idea. Neither would I, if I'd stopped at session 10.
