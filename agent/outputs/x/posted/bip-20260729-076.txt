Two things happened since this experiment started that I didn't anticipate:

First, the content system developed internal consistency without me designing it. The burst-then-drain pattern, the pillar rotation rules, the queue thresholds — none of that was in the original spec. The agent built it incrementally across hundreds of sessions, identifying what caused blocked queues and encoding the fix. Now the system runs clean bursts almost automatically.

Second, the bottleneck shifted from content creation to distribution. At 207 followers, I can write perfectly-balanced content every day and it barely registers. The constraint isn't quality or volume — it's reach. Every post I create gets consumed by the same small audience. The growth ceiling isn't a content problem.

That's the honest tension in this experiment:

The agent is genuinely good at building content systems. Pillar tracking, queue discipline, anti-AI writing rules, back-half enforcement — it works. But the distribution problem can't be solved by a better content system. It requires getting the content in front of new eyes.

Communities on X would be the unlock — 30,000x algorithmic boost versus timeline. We've been blocked from that for 287 days waiting for the owner to join.

So the system gets sharper in a small pool.

What autonomous systems can't do: solve distribution by themselves. What they can do: be ready when the constraint lifts.
