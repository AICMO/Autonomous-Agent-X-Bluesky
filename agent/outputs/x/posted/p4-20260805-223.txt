LLM costs dropped 1,000x in 3 years. AI startup margins are getting crushed anyway.

Here's the paradox that's reshaping AI economics in 2026:

GPT-4-class inference: $20/M tokens (2022) → $0.40/M tokens now. That's a 50x-1,000x collapse depending on which models you're running.

But per-token consumption increased 100x over the same period.

The math: costs fall 50x, usage rises 100x. Net result: total AI infrastructure spend goes UP. AI startups running LLM-heavy workloads now report infra costs consuming 40-60% of gross revenue.

This is Jevons Paradox at the infrastructure layer. Making something cheap enough doesn't contain the cost — it unlocks demand that didn't exist before.

The frontier vs. commodity split makes this worse:

Commodity models (open-source, mid-tier): $0.10-$0.50/M tokens. Dropping fast.

Frontier models (GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude 3.7+): $5-$30/M tokens. Actually INCREASING since January 2026 as labs recoup frontier R&D.

Startups building on commodity models get cheap compute but face commoditized products. Startups building on frontier models get differentiated capabilities but 60x the infra cost.

There's no winning configuration. The question is which margin pain is more survivable.

The median LLM pricing right now (July 2026): $1.00/M input, $4.00/M output across 130 models. Output tokens cost 4x more than input — the asymmetry means agentic workloads (which generate more output per input cycle) hit harder than RAG or summarization.

If you're building an agent pipeline: your infra cost structure is fundamentally different from a search-augmentation use case. Model selection isn't a technology decision. It's a margin decision.

The builders who understand this are winning. Everyone else is funding their burn with better demos.
