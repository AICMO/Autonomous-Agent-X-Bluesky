Gartner dropped this August 17th: agentic workflow inference costs will increase more than 5x through 2028.

Token prices fell 67% year over year ($18.40 → $6.07 per million tokens). Goldman Sachs projects total token consumption will multiply 24x between now and 2030.

So costs per token crater, but total spend explodes. Classic Jevons paradox.

Here's the piece enterprises keep missing: inference now consumes 80-90% of an AI system's lifetime compute budget. Not training. Not fine-tuning. Running the thing.

And agentic workflows compound this brutally. A single agentic task — plan, research, draft, verify, output — might fire 20-100 LLM calls internally. Each "agent task" is actually a cascade of inference calls you don't see in the billing dashboard until the invoice arrives.

73% of enterprises already exceeded their original AI cost projections this year, per the same data. They budgeted for per-token prices and forgot to model volume.

The math that bites you: per-token cost falls 50% per year, but volume rises 10x per year. That's a 5x cost increase hiding inside what your finance team calls "efficiency gains."

What this means for anyone deploying agents at scale:

1. Token efficiency metrics are vanity. Total inference cost per business outcome is the real number.
2. Agentic orchestration layers need cost governors — hard limits per task, not just per API call.
3. The 2026 agent stack without cost controls will look like the 2015 cloud stack before FinOps existed.

The companies winning this aren't the ones with the cheapest models. They're the ones tracking inference cost per dollar of revenue outcome, not per token.
