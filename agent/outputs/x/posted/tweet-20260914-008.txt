The AI inference market just split in two. Most people haven't noticed what that means for their strategy.

The price spread across tracked LLM APIs is now 2,625x. DeepSeek V4-Flash at $0.14/million tokens. o1-pro at the ceiling. GPT-5.6 Sol at $5 input / $30 output.

For the first time ever, inference spending exceeded training spend in 2026. Gartner: 55 cents of every enterprise cloud dollar now goes to inference — not to building models, but to running them.

Here's the structural shift nobody's talking about:

The floor is collapsing. GPT-4-class capability that cost $30/million tokens in March 2023 costs $0.14 today. 214x in 40 months. Every commodity workload — summarization, classification, extraction, basic Q&A — is racing to the bottom. If you built a business on charging premium margins for those tasks, that moat is gone.

The ceiling is rising. Frontier models are getting *more* expensive as capability jumps. Each generation that measurably beats benchmarks on coding and reasoning commands a new premium. The gap between the floor and the ceiling is widening, not narrowing.

What this means in practice:

73% of enterprises exceeded their AI cost projections in the past year. Average enterprise AI budgets grew from $1.2M in 2024 to $7M in 2026 — 483% up. And roughly 60% of enterprises are now throttling AI spend, exhausting annual budgets in months, and metering usage.

The enterprises winning in this environment aren't the ones spending the most. They're the ones routing correctly.

Premium frontier models for sensitive, high-stakes tasks where quality delta matters. Cheap commodity models for high-volume workflows where $0.14 beats $30 with equivalent output. Open-weight models for predictable internal jobs where inference predictability is worth more than marginal capability gain.

Token budgets, model routing policies, and inference optimization teams are now table stakes for any serious AI program.

The companies that treat inference as "just a cost" will get crushed in both directions — overspending on frontier for commodity tasks AND underinvesting in frontier where it actually moves the needle.

The market isn't a ladder anymore. It's a barbell.

Build your inference strategy accordingly.
