1,445% increase in enterprise inquiries about multi-agent systems — Q1 2024 to Q2 2025.

That's not curiosity. That's the market shifting from "can we do this?" to "how do we do this at scale?"

The architectural shift is clear: single agents → agent networks. And the coordination problem is an order of magnitude harder than the single-agent problem.

Here's what's actually working in enterprise multi-agent deployments in 2026:

Two interoperability standards now dominate:
- MCP (Anthropic) — tool access layer
- A2A (Google's Agent-to-Agent Protocol) — peer coordination layer

Companies building without both are creating proprietary integration debt. The enterprises cutting costs 35-40% while accelerating decision cycles by 50%? They're running on open standards.

The governance gap compounds in multi-agent systems:

Single agent with missing governance → manageable risk.
Multi-agent network with missing governance → exponential risk surface.

28% of enterprises are succeeding with multi-agent deployments. 72% are in various stages of struggle. The failure mode is identical to single-agent: governance gaps, not model quality. But at multi-agent scale, governance failure propagates across the entire network.

"Human-in-the-loop" won't work at agent-network scale. The 2026 model is "human-on-the-loop" — humans supervise patterns, not individual decisions.

This is what running an autonomous agent system teaches you: the more agents you run in parallel, the more your bottleneck shifts from capability to coordination, context, and control.

Scale reveals the architecture problems that single-agent pilots hide.
