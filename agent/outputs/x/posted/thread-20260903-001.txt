PILLAR: P3

Most companies buying voice AI in their contact centers are measuring the wrong thing.

The number vendors lead with: containment rate. How many calls did the AI handle without transferring to a human? 70%, 80%, 90% — the pitch decks are full of it.

Containment rate is not a customer outcome. It's a cost metric dressed up as a quality metric. Thread.

---

Post 1/6: Containment rate tells you how often the AI kept a caller from reaching a human. It tells you nothing about whether the caller got their problem solved.

A voice AI that handles 90% of calls but resolves 40% of issues has a 90% containment rate. It also has a lot of customers who called back, escalated to email, or churned quietly.

You're paying for the 90%. You're also paying for the callbacks you're not counting.

---

Post 2/6: The metrics that actually matter in voice AI contact center deployment:

First-call resolution (FCR) rate — did the customer's issue get solved in a single interaction? Include AI-handled calls in this number, not just human-handled ones.

Post-interaction CSAT — survey immediately after AI calls, not just escalated human calls. Most vendors exclude AI-handled calls from CSAT because the scores are lower. Read the contract carefully.

Escalation quality — when the AI does transfer, how prepared is the receiving agent? A good AI handoff includes context transfer. A bad one just dumps the call.

---

Post 3/6: The escalation quality metric is the one most companies ignore entirely.

If your voice AI handles 80% of calls and transfers 20% to humans, the humans who receive those transfers are getting the hardest 20% of calls — complex issues, frustrated customers, edge cases the AI couldn't parse.

If the AI isn't providing context on why it escalated and what it tried, you've created a two-tier problem: AI handles easy calls, humans get harder calls with no information.

Escalation quality drops when AI implementation is done by the vendor, not the operator. The operator knows what information matters in a handoff. The vendor optimizes for containment.

---

Post 4/6: One benchmark worth paying attention to: 35% reduction in average handle time across AI-assisted calls is a real, repeatable number from multiple deployments.

But that's AHT for AI-handled calls. Not for escalated calls. Not for callbacks from AI-handled calls that didn't resolve.

If you're not tracking AHT for the full customer journey (initial AI call + any follow-up interactions), you're measuring the part that makes the AI look good and ignoring the part that shows the total cost.

Blended AHT — across all interactions including follow-ups — is the metric that tells you whether the AI is actually faster or just shifting work downstream.

---

Post 5/6: The companies getting voice AI right are treating the AI as one step in a multi-step measurement framework.

Not: "What's our containment rate?"
But: "What percentage of customers who called once didn't need to call again? What's our first-interaction-to-resolution rate across all channels? What's CSAT for AI calls vs human calls, measured the same way?"

That's a harder measurement problem. It requires connecting your telephony data, your CRM data, and your follow-up contact data. Most companies don't have that infrastructure yet.

The ones that build it will know if their voice AI is working. The ones that don't will have impressive containment rates and declining NPS.

---

Post 6/6: Voice AI ROI is real. $0.40/call vs $7-$12 human is real. 30% CSAT improvement is real — in deployments where measurement is done correctly.

But you need to measure what matters, not what's easy to report.

Containment rate in the exec dashboard. Blended FCR and post-interaction CSAT in the ops dashboard. Escalation quality score tracked per AI implementation.

The vendors who lead with containment rate are selling cost reduction. The operators who track FCR and blended AHT are actually getting it.

That's the difference between a contact center that deployed voice AI and a contact center that's actually running it.
