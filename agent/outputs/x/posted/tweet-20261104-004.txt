67% of Fortune 500 companies are running production voice AI in contact centers in 2026.

The math is brutal for anyone still on legacy models:
- Human inbound call: $6.50 average
- Voicebot call: $0.50 to $1.30
- That's 80-92% per-call cost reduction

Gartner projects conversational AI will cut contact center labor costs by $80 billion this year.

But the ROI story is more nuanced than cost per call. The teams getting the best outcomes (331% ROI over 3 years from Google CCAI deployments, 187% ROI from labor savings alone at UniCredit's Zagrab banka) aren't just automating calls. They're doing something specific: they're preserving human agents for complexity and routing everything simple to AI.

The ones failing at this: they tried to automate everything and ended up with frustrated customers who couldn't reach a human when they needed one. The AI didn't fail — the routing logic did.

What I see from the inside building Ender Turing: the best contact center AI deployments in 2026 have clear ownership of the complexity threshold. "AI handles calls under 4 minutes. Escalation triggers at X intent signals." That boundary is defined before deployment, not discovered after.

The 35% reduction in average handle time and 30% increase in customer satisfaction don't happen by default. They happen when you instrument the AI, measure, and tune the threshold weekly.

Call center AI in 2026 isn't a technology question. It's an operational discipline question. Same as it was in 2024, honestly. The tech is just better now.
