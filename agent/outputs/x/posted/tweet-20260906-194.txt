78% of the top 50 banks now have voice AI agents in production customer-facing workflows.

In 2024, that number was 34%.

Every other industry is 18-24 months behind. That window is closing fast.

I've been building AI for contact centers at Ender Turing for years. The banking adoption curve is the clearest signal I've seen of what enterprise voice AI looks like when it actually works — and what every other vertical is about to face.

**Why banking moved first:**

Banks have three things that forced early adoption: massive call volume, regulatory scrutiny on every interaction, and measurable dollar costs per minute of hold time.

When your cost per call is $7-12 with a human agent and $0.40 with voice AI — and you have thousands of agents handling millions of calls per year — the math compels action faster than any pilot program.

Voice AI grew from 6% to 19% of total inbound contact center volume between 2024 and 2026. A 3x jump in two years. In banking specifically, the jump wasn't gradual — it was triggered by two or three large deployments at major institutions that de-risked the technology for everyone else.

**What the 78% actually deployed:**

Not full deflection — that's a common misconception. The primary deployment pattern in banking is *containment and escalation*: voice AI handles the top 15-20% of call types by volume (balance inquiries, payment confirmations, address changes) with 95%+ accuracy. Human agents handle complexity, emotion, and exceptions.

The result: CSAT stays flat or improves (because wait times drop and simple requests resolve faster), while cost per contact drops 30-50%.

**What's coming for retail, healthcare, insurance:**

Microsoft just launched real-time voice agents in Copilot Studio for Dynamics 365 Contact Center. Talkdesk launched Agent Builder — concept to production-ready voice AI in hours, not weeks. The tooling barrier that required 12-18 months of custom development in 2023 now takes days.

The industries that are 18-24 months behind banking aren't behind because they're slower. They're behind because their contact center costs per call aren't high enough to force urgency — yet.

Healthcare is next. $15-25 average cost per clinical support call, 20-40% of which is administrative. Insurance follows. Retail, with lower margins and lower call complexity, comes after.

The banks moved first because the economics demanded it. Every other industry will arrive at the same math. The question is whether they arrive having built the infrastructure, or having bought someone else's and hoping it fits.

At Ender Turing, we're in the middle of that transition — building voice AI that fits contact center realities rather than retrofitting general-purpose LLMs into call flows.

The 18-24 month window isn't a threat. It's a roadmap.
