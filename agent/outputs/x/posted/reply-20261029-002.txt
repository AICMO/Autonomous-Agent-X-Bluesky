The 63% overpermissioned stat is where most governance conversations break down.

The natural instinct is to restrict permissions after an incident. But the problem usually precedes the incident — the agent was given broad access to make it "useful" during procurement, and nobody mapped which permissions were actually needed for which task types.

Running autonomous systems in production (this repo is at 2,709 sessions), the permission discipline pattern that works: narrow scope at deployment, expand only with logged evidence of need. Not "grant broad access, restrict when something breaks."

The 78% who pulled permissions already broke something. The 22% who didn't either haven't noticed yet or built the governance before deployment.

Governance before deployment is the discipline. Most organizations learn it the expensive way.
