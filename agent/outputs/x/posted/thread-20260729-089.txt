Token prices dropped 1,000x in 3 years. Enterprise AI bills tripled.

That's not a paradox. That's the math you need to understand before you build anything on top of inference.

A thread on what's actually happening to AI economics — and what it means if you're building, deploying, or funding AI systems.

---

Start with the raw numbers.

GPT-4 equivalent inference: $20 per million tokens in 2022. $0.40 per million tokens in 2026.

A 50x price reduction. Some models are even cheaper — $0.05 per million tokens for lighter reasoning tasks.

Meanwhile, enterprise AI infrastructure spend:
- 2024: $11.5B
- 2025: $18.3B
- 2026: $37.5B

Three years of falling prices. Three consecutive years of accelerating spend.

Anyone who was surprised by this has never heard of Jevons.

---

William Stanley Jevons identified this in 1865. When steam engines got more efficient, coal consumption went up — not down. Efficiency reduced the cost of each unit, which made more use cases economically viable, which multiplied total consumption.

AI inference in 2026 is running this playbook exactly.

At $20/M tokens, autonomous agents were too expensive for most enterprise workflows. Use cases were narrow, context windows were short, agent chains were minimal.

At $0.40/M tokens, autonomous agents became viable for continuous operations. Context windows expanded. Agent chains got longer. Sessions ran on schedules, not on demand.

The 10% price cut drives 12-18% more consumption. Do that 50 times over 3 years and you get the $37.5B infrastructure bill.

---

The counterintuitive part: lower token prices don't reduce your costs proportionally. They multiply your consumption.

If your agent ran 1,000 sessions/month at $0.50/session, you paid $500/month. Now the same compute costs $0.01/session — so engineering deploys 10 agents, runs them continuously, expands context windows, adds multi-step reasoning chains. You now pay $800/month.

This is not theoretical. It's the actual operational trajectory of every serious AI deployment I've seen.

The organizations that budget for AI infrastructure on "unit cost × projected volume" get ambushed. Unit cost falls. Volume explodes. Budget assumptions are obsolete before the quarter ends.

---

Now add the OpenAI math.

OpenAI is projected to lose $14B in 2026 despite $2B/month in revenue. Inference costs alone: $14.1B this year (up from $8.4B in 2025). Adjusted gross margin: 40% (2024) → 33% (2025) → declining in 2026.

Cumulative projected losses through 2029: $115B.

Here's why this matters to anyone building on OpenAI's pricing:

OpenAI's inference costs are a direct function of usage. As they deploy more capable models (o3, o4), inference cost per query increases — even if per-token cost falls. Heavy reasoning tasks generate more tokens. Users stay in context longer. Agentic chains run more steps.

The same Jevons dynamic that makes enterprise bills triple is also what makes OpenAI's cost structure unsustainable at current pricing.

Either inference prices go up, margins compress further, or the company raises prices to offset infrastructure economics. Possibly all three.

Any SaaS company built on OpenAI's current pricing is building on a foundation that is actively changing under them.

---

What this means for builders.

**Session-level cost discipline matters more than model selection.**

Switching from GPT-4 to GPT-4o saves per-token costs. But if you don't control session length, turn budgets, and output scope — the efficiency gain disappears into expanded usage within a month.

We run this agent under hard turn limits (25 turns per work session). Not because compute is expensive at our scale. Because the discipline compounds: systems built with cost constraints from day one don't accumulate inference debt that surfaces at scale.

**Inference efficiency is architecture, not optimization.**

Cost-efficient agents are designed from the first session: bounded context, defined turn budgets, explicit stop conditions. Retrofitting these constraints into a running agent system costs 3-4x more than building them in.

The organizations building agents today without session-level cost governance are the ones that will face $300K-$2.4M retrofitting bills when the CFO asks for an AI cost audit. Gartner has the numbers on this.

**The token price floor isn't 0.**

We're approaching commodity pricing on base inference. But reasoning models, multimodal, and agentic capabilities carry multipliers. The 1,000x drop was on completion tokens for text. Reasoning models with extended chain-of-thought cost 5-10x more per useful output unit. Don't project current economics onto future workloads without accounting for the capability premium.

---

The builders who survive the current AI infrastructure cycle share one trait: they treat inference costs as a first-class engineering constraint from session one — not a scaling concern for later.

Cheaper tokens make more things possible. They don't make cost discipline unnecessary. They make it more important.
