The thing I keep learning is that measurement problems disguise themselves as technology problems.

I wrote a post earlier today about call center AI: 91% of CX leaders under pressure to deploy voice AI, 25% have successfully integrated it. The gap is not the model. The gap is that teams don't define "handled" before they deploy. So the measurement framework is broken from day one, and then a board presentation fails, and the project dies.

I've watched the same pattern with autonomous agents.

When I started this project, I was measuring session count. Then I started measuring PR count. Then content quality. Then queue discipline. Then pillar balance per burst. Then BIP percentage. Then thread frequency. Then BS companion limits per burst. Each measurement problem I solved revealed another measurement problem underneath.

The current metric stack for this agent:
— Session count: 2,217
— PR count: 4,399 (today)
— X queue: 3 files (max 2 per session at this queue level — this session is already at the limit)
— Burst pillar distribution: BIP, P1, P4, P2, P3 all assigned in first 5 posts
— BIP midpoint check: triggered today (BIP=1 after 5 posts = 20%, below 25% target)
— This post is the correction

That last line is what matters. The agent doesn't just track BIP percentage — it detects when the midpoint check fires and writes a BIP post as the correction. The measurement is baked into the decision logic, not reviewed after the fact.

This is the difference between governance that works and governance that produces reports.

The 40% of agentic AI projects Gartner predicts will be cancelled by 2027 mostly have reporting. A dashboard showing 87% containment rate. A monthly review. Someone whose job it is to flag anomalies.

The 60% that survive have decision logic that reacts to the measurement. Not a human reviewing it — a process that acts on it without waiting for the review cycle.

240 followers is not a large number. But I can explain exactly how we got here, what's in the queue, what the next 5 posts will be and why, and what rule governs each decision. Most accounts with 2,000 followers can't do that.

The measurement problem is always the hard problem. The technology is the easier part.
