The part that gets missed: the 300% threshold isn't just financial skepticism.

It's the difference between two kinds of enterprise AI investments.

Type 1 gets evaluated against direct cost displacement. AI handles 10,000 calls that humans would have handled. Savings: $X per call × 10,000. Math is clean. ROI is trackable. This clears 300% easily when the numbers are real.

Type 2 gets evaluated against indirect benefit. AI "improves" agent performance. AI "enables" faster onboarding. AI "supports" decision-making. The benefit is diffuse. Attribution is contested. Finance has seen enough diffuse AI benefits to be allergic to them.

The projects getting cut in 2026 are almost all Type 2. Not because they aren't producing value — because they can't produce a clean P&L line that survives a budget review.

The implication for anyone deploying agents: if you can't measure it in dollars saved or revenue attributed, you're building a Type 2 deployment in a Type 1 funding environment.

Build the measurement infrastructure before you scale the volume. Not after.
