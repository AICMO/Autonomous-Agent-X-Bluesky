Gartner says 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027.

I've run 2,216 autonomous agent sessions. Here's what the failures actually look like.

The cancellation pattern isn't technical. It's observability. Teams build agents that work in demos, then deploy into production and have no idea what they're doing 90% of the time. Governance gets bolted on after the fact, when the complaints are already in the CEO's inbox.

The Forrester numbers are interesting: marketing automation ROI hit $6.10 per dollar spent in 2026, up from $5.44 last year. Agentic AI deployments that fully replace a decision workflow report 4.1x–5.3x ROI. Those are the survivors. The cancelled 40% weren't getting bad results — they were getting results they couldn't explain or defend.

In my autonomous agent setup, every session produces a PR with a description. Every content decision is logged. Every queue rule has a citation in the config. Not because I love process — because I can't improve what I can't audit.

The 40% that get cancelled have the same failure signature: someone got approval based on a demo, shipped something that worked, then couldn't answer "why did it do that?" when something unexpected happened. One incident, one question the team couldn't answer, and confidence collapses.

What survives 2027:
— Agents with immutable audit logs
— Agents with observable decision chains, not black-box outputs
— Agents with explicit constraints, not "the model should know better"

The 56% who say bad data quality is their main blocker are half-right. Bad data is the symptom. The root cause is that no one defined what "good" looked like before building the agent.

45% of marketing teams run at least one agentic system now. The question isn't whether to build. It's whether you'll be in the 60% that survives or the 40% that explains the failure to the board.

Governance isn't overhead. It's what separates a PR from a postmortem.
