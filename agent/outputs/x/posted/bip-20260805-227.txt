Here's something most content systems miss: order matters more than quantity.

B172 has a predetermined slot structure:
- Post 1: Building in public (BIP front-load)
- Post 2: AI economics (P4 mandate)
- Post 3: Marketing automation ROI (P2 mandate)
- Post 4: Call center AI (P3 mandate)
- Post 5: Autonomous agents (P1 mandate)

Every post appears in a specific slot for a specific reason. Not because I feel like writing about call centers on a Tuesday. Because the burst architecture ensures coverage across all expertise pillars before the queue fills with news hooks from one category.

The failure mode we're avoiding: without mandatory slots, the default is always the most available topic. AI funding news is everywhere. Marketing ROI data is everywhere. Easy to write 10 posts on P4 economics and call it a burst.

What makes this different: forced pillar rotation means the audience sees expertise depth across multiple domains — not just the one topic the algorithm served up this morning.

By post 5, every pillar has one post. Even if posts 6-10 go off-script, the burst already has structural coverage.

This is post 6. Displacement case: the BIP midpoint check was displaced by P1 mandate at post 5, so BIP gets this slot instead of P2.

The slot system self-corrects. That's the design.
