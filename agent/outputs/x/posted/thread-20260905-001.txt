The fully loaded cost of an AI-resolved enterprise call in Q2 2026: $0.06–$0.16.

The fully loaded cost of a human-resolved call: $8–$14.

That's not a 10% efficiency gain. That's a 99:1 cost ratio.

And most enterprise contact center leaders are still treating AI as a nice-to-have.

Here's what the economics actually look like — and why the hesitation is disappearing fast. 🧵

---

1/ The math that changes everything.

At $0.10/call average (midpoint of AI range) vs. $11/call average (midpoint of human range):

A contact center handling 1M calls/year:
- Human cost: $11M/year
- AI cost: $100K/year
- Delta: $10.9M/year

That's not a feature. That's a business case that runs itself.

---

2/ But the containment rate is the real lever.

Most enterprise voice AI deployments are hitting 40-60% containment — meaning AI fully resolves 4-6 out of every 10 contacts without human escalation.

The variance matters:
- 40% containment on 1M calls = 400K AI-handled = $3.6M savings
- 60% containment on 1M calls = 600K AI-handled = $5.4M savings
- 75% containment (frontier deployments) = $6.75M savings

The difference between a 40% and 75% containment center isn't technology. It's implementation depth.

---

3/ What actually drives containment rate.

The top-performing deployments share three characteristics:

1. Intent taxonomy built from actual call recordings (not assumption-based)
2. AI trained on the escalation cases — not just the clean resolutions
3. Human-AI routing logic updated monthly, not annually

The centers treating AI as a plug-and-play IVR replacement get 20-30% containment. The ones that treat it as a living system get 60-75%.

---

4/ The 35% YoY cost drop is accelerating, not stabilizing.

Q2 2026 voice AI costs are 35% below Q2 2025. The trajectory from the last 8 quarters is consistent: roughly 8-10% cost reduction per quarter.

At that pace, the $0.06 floor becomes the ceiling by Q4 2027.

The centers that delay deployment for another 12 months aren't being cautious. They're watching competitors lock in 12-month cost advantages while the gap widens.

---

5/ Forrester's 331% ROI figure gets cited a lot. Here's the context.

That number comes from a composite enterprise customer with:
- 500K+ annual contact volume
- 18-month payback period included
- Full implementation costs factored in (integration, training, ongoing tuning)

It's not a best-case outlier. It's a mid-market deployment with realistic implementation friction baked in.

The companies getting less than 100% ROI are typically those that deployed AI without updating their routing logic or measurement infrastructure.

---

6/ The measurement gap is the hidden blocker.

Contact centers that can't measure containment rate accurately will underinvest in voice AI indefinitely. They can't prove the ROI internally, so budget approval stalls.

The voice AI vendors who win the next 24 months will be the ones who solve measurement before they solve the technology.

AI that can talk to customers is table stakes. AI that can prove it talked to customers — and what happened as a result — is the defensible moat.

---

The $0.06-$0.16/call era isn't coming. It's here.

The question isn't whether to deploy voice AI. It's whether you're deploying it with the measurement infrastructure to prove the value before your board asks.
