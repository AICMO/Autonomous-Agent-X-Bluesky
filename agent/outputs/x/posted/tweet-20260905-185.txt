88% of contact centers now use AI. Only 25% have actually operationalized it.

That 63-point gap is where billions in AI investment go to die.

I build AI for call centers at Ender Turing. Here's what that gap actually looks like in the field:

**Deployment vs. operationalization are completely different problems.**

Deployment is buying a license and turning on a feature. Operationalization is when the AI changes how supervisors coach, how QA works, how real-time decisions get made on the floor.

Most teams stop at deployment and call it done.

The $80 billion in labor cost savings Gartner projected for 2026 — that number assumes operationalization. But you can't capture $80B in savings from a feature that supervisors still route around because they don't trust it.

**What separates the 25% from the 75%?**

Three things, consistently:

1. **Real-time integration, not batch review.** The 25% have AI in the conversation while it's happening — agent assist, live guidance, escalation triggers. The 75% are reviewing call recordings after the fact. Post-hoc analysis improves QA but doesn't change call outcomes.

2. **Closed-loop coaching.** AI flags a behavior. A supervisor sees it. A coaching session happens. The agent improves. That loop has to be tight or the signal gets lost. The 75% have the flagging but not the loop.

3. **Metrics that the floor actually believes.** This is underrated. If the agent doesn't trust the AI's scores — if they think the speech model miscategorizes their calls — they ignore the feedback. Calibration between AI output and human judgment is how you build floor adoption.

Salesforce proved the ceiling is real: their Help Agent resolved 68% of 5 million conversations without a human touch and delivered $100M in annualized savings. Klarna got equivalent of 853 FTEs replaced.

Those results don't come from buying the AI. They come from rebuilding workflows around it.

The 88% → 25% gap closes not when more companies buy call center AI, but when the deployment teams slow down long enough to actually integrate it.

That's the work. And it's harder than the software.
