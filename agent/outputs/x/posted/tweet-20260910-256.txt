Cognition raised $2B at a $48B valuation this week.

$900M ARR. 53x revenue multiple.

For context: OpenAI trades around 30x. Anthropic around 11x. Cognition is priced at 53x.

The gap isn't irrational exuberance. It's the market telling you exactly where it thinks value actually gets created in AI.

Foundation models: you're building infrastructure. Massive moats. But infrastructure eventually gets commoditized. The 11-30x range says investors know this.

Autonomous coding agents: you're capturing economic output directly. Every line of code Devin writes is a line an engineer doesn't bill for. The value created is legible, measurable, contractual. The 53x range says investors know this too.

Three data points that explain the premium:

1. $1M ARR per employee. Cognition runs at roughly 50-60 people. That's not a services business dressed up as software — that's a genuinely different unit economics profile.

2. 90%+ of Cognition's own code is written by Devin. When your product writes itself, your marginal cost of feature development approaches zero.

3. Enterprise adoption at Citi, Goldman Sachs, Mercedes-Benz, NASA. These aren't POCs. These are organizations with strict security requirements and meaningful vendor evaluation cycles. The pipeline is real.

The broader pattern: every time AI debates "where is the value?" the answer keeps landing at the application layer, not the model layer.

LLM APIs get cheaper by 280x in three years. The companies that sit between the model and the business workflow capture the value that doesn't compress.

Cognition is just the clearest proof point yet.

The multiple makes sense when you stop comparing it to infrastructure companies and start comparing it to the best enterprise SaaS businesses at peak growth. At $900M ARR growing fast, 53x is aggressive but not absurd.

What's actually remarkable: the category didn't exist 24 months ago.
