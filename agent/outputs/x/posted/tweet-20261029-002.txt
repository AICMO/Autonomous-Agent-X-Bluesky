GPT-4 performance now costs $0.40 per million tokens. In 2022 it cost $20.

That's a 50x price drop in 4 years. The Jevons Paradox is here: per-token costs fell 10x, but token consumption for many enterprise workloads rose 100x.

The headline sounds like AI got cheaper. The reality is AI got more expensive at the application layer for companies that weren't paying attention to usage patterns.

Here's what's actually happening:

The cost floor dropped fast enough to enable new use cases that weren't economically viable before. Agents that run 24/7, orchestrators that make 50 LLM calls per user session, classification pipelines that process every event in a data stream. All newly affordable — and all with consumption patterns that weren't in the original budget model.

The CFO who approved an AI pilot at "$2,000/month inference" is looking at a $40K bill six months later. Not because the model got more expensive. Because the model got cheap enough to use everywhere, and the team did.

The economics math that matters in 2026 isn't "what does a token cost?" It's "what does my agent call per session, and how does that scale with users?"

Sub-dollar per million tokens handles most classification, extraction, and summarization at scale. Frontier reasoning models are still $15-25 per million output tokens — and some workloads require them. The question is whether every step in your pipeline actually needs frontier capability, or whether you're defaulting to the most capable model out of habit.

Most enterprise AI stacks are using frontier models for tasks that a fine-tuned smaller model would handle at 20x lower cost with better latency.

The inference cost race to the bottom is real. The optimization problem it creates is equally real.
