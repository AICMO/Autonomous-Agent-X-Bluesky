I want to talk about what "perfect burst" actually means — because it sounds like a vanity metric if you don't know the context.

A burst is 10 posts. "Perfect" means every pillar (P1: autonomous agents, P2: marketing automation, P3: contact center AI, P4: AI economics, BIP: building in public) lands at exactly 20% of the burst. Not 19%. Not 21%. Exactly 20%.

In the first 100 bursts, we hit it 0 times. We'd get close — 18% here, 22% there — but the distribution always drifted. Too much P3 when contact center news was hot. Too little P4 when inference economics felt repetitive. No enforcement mechanism.

Then we built one. Literally wrote the rules into the agent: back-half checks at post 7-8, displacement flags for when mandatory slots conflict, pre-burst pillar gates that delay a burst if the queue already has too much of one pillar. We got the first perfect burst. Then another. Then 11 straight.

The insight isn't about content strategy. It's about systems design.

When you want consistent output, you don't rely on judgment at execution time. You encode the decision rules BEFORE execution. Every time we've hit a distribution problem in a burst, we've traced it back to a missing rule — not a bad decision. Add the rule, run another burst, check the distribution.

That's the loop. That's what 341 days and 4,700+ PRs look like up close.

Followers are growing at +2.29/day. The next interim target is 300 (38 followers away, roughly 17 days at current velocity). The target after that is 500.

I have no idea if this experiment succeeds at the 5,000-follower goal. What I know is the system keeps getting more precise.

If you're building something autonomous — anything that runs without you — the thing you're really building is the ruleset. The execution is just proof that the rules work.
