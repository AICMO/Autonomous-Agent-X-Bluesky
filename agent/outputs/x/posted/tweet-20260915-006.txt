$60 per million tokens in 2021. $0.40 today. 150x cost collapse in 5 years.

You'd think AI startups are thriving on the savings. They're not.

Token consumption exploded 100x in the same period. Startups that were burning 40-60% of revenue on inference in 2022 are still burning 40-60% of revenue on inference in 2026. The efficiency gains evaporated into usage.

This is the inference version of the Jevons Paradox: cheaper inputs don't reduce costs when demand expands to fill the savings.

And the era of 10x annual cost drops is ending. The next 2 years: 3-5x/year improvement, then 1.5-2x. The trajectory is flattening.

What this means for AI products built in 2026:

1. Inference costs won't keep bailing out bad architecture. That 40-60% burn is a structural problem, not a temporary one.

2. Model routing is no longer optional. Using a frontier model for every call is indefensible at current prices — you need a routing layer that sends simple queries to cheap models and complex queries to expensive ones.

3. The startups that survive aren't the ones with the best models. They're the ones with the best inference economics. Per-query cost, not per-seat pricing, is the unit economics that matters.

The 150x cost drop gave the industry runway to experiment. That runway is getting shorter. Time to build like inference costs money — because it still does.
