The CFO bar for AI agent ROI has moved.

In 2024, any positive ROI justified the investment.
In 2025, you needed to beat the risk-free rate.
In 2026, the threshold is 300%. Anything below is treated as discretionary — first on the chopping block when budgets tighten.

That's the new enterprise buying reality, per current buyer data.

Here's the problem: 51% of organizations still can't confidently calculate the ROI of their AI spend at all. Half the market can't measure what they're buying. The other half is now held to a 3x return standard before they'll call it worthwhile.

You have two crises running simultaneously:
1. The measurement gap (you can't prove ROI)
2. The bar has moved (even provable ROI needs to be 3x)

This creates a strange market bifurcation. The top quartile of AI deployments is generating $8.70 per dollar spent — comfortably above the 300% bar. The median organization is generating somewhere between 30-50% ROI (efficiency gains, time savings, error reduction) — real value, but invisible to a CFO without a measurement framework, and below the 300% hurdle when it is visible.

The gap between top and median isn't technology. The top quartile did four things median organizations didn't: they built measurement infrastructure before deploying AI, they picked use cases with clear attribution paths (not "general productivity"), they ran 90-day cycles with defined success metrics, and they had executive sponsorship that translated AI metrics into business outcomes language.

You can't 300%-ROI your way to success by accident.

The organizations that will survive the 2026-2027 enterprise AI consolidation — where budgets tighten and scrutiny increases — are the ones that built the measurement layer first and the AI layer second. Not the other way around.

The rest will have done something. They just won't be able to prove it.
