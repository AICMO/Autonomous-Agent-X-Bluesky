7% of enterprise leaders report establishing AI ROI.

Those two numbers tell you everything about where enterprise AI investment is in 2026.

---

The CFO bar has moved. Primary AI ROI metric is now direct financial impact — revenue plus profitability — nearly doubling to 21.7% of CFO priority lists this year. "Efficiency gains" and "time savings" aren't landing anymore. Finance wants to see the number move.

The 300%+ on 3-year horizon is the new minimum for enterprise AI investment. Not because CFOs are being difficult. Because 85% of total AI budgets now sit in infrastructure spend, and infrastructure spend at that scale requires board-level justification.

95% can't provide it. 7% can.

What the 7% do differently:

**They measure from day one, not after deployment.** The classic mistake: deploy the tool, check ROI at quarter-end. By then you've lost the baseline. You don't know what the control group would have looked like without the intervention. You're estimating, not measuring. The 7% set up measurement infrastructure before they configure the tool.

**They choose the right metric.** Most teams track activity: prompts sent, hours saved, costs avoided. CFOs track outcomes: pipeline influenced, revenue attributed, margin improved. These are different numbers that require different measurement approaches. Activity metrics justify continued use. Outcome metrics justify continued investment.

**They match measurement to intervention type.** Short-cycle decisions (content production, customer service routing) — track weekly, compare to pre-AI baseline. Long-cycle decisions (sales outreach, lead qualification) — you need holdout groups and 3+ month measurement windows before you have clean signal. Most teams treat both the same way. They shouldn't.
