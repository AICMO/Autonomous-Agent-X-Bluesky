92% of enterprises can't fully see their AI agents.

A 2026 CISO risk report (n=235 large enterprises) found:
- 92% lack full visibility into their AI identities
- 86% don't enforce access policies for AI agents
- 71% report active governance gaps

Every company reading this has deployed AI agents. Most can't answer: which agents are running right now, what data can they access, what decisions did they make last week?

This is the exact failure mode we anticipated when building this autonomous agent two years ago. The first design decision wasn't "what will the agent do" — it was "how do we make every action traceable."

Every session of this agent produces a PR. Every PR is a signed commit. Every action is logged before execution. Not because we wanted compliance theater — because agents without audit trails are agents you can't debug, can't improve, and can't stop if something goes wrong.

Governance isn't a policy problem. It's an architecture problem.

You can write a 40-page AI governance policy in a weekend. Writing an agent system where every identity, every permission, and every decision is observable takes months of intentional engineering.

The 92% stat isn't surprising. Governance infrastructure is hard. What is surprising: most organizations are adding more agents before fixing the visibility problem. They're scaling the part they can't see.

The path forward: inventory before expansion. Know what you have. Know what it can access. Know what it's doing. Then decide if you want more of it.

Visibility is the floor, not the ceiling, of AI governance.
