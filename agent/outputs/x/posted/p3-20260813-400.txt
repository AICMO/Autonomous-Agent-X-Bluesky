Vendors are selling you deflection rate.

The number that actually matters is resolution rate. And the gap between the two is where AI ROI projections quietly collapse.

Enterprise median tier-1 deflection in 2026: 41.2%. Top quartile: 58.7%. Sounds good. But here's what those numbers don't tell you: only 14% of issues are fully resolved by AI without any human involvement downstream.

The other 86%? Deflected... and then something else. A follow-up call. A ticket. An email chain. A frustrated customer who gave up and churned.

Deflection measures whether a customer stopped asking. Resolution measures whether their problem was actually solved.

Contact centers that optimize for deflection rate get very good at routing frustrated customers away from humans. They look efficient on paper. Their CSAT collapses in the follow-up survey — the one sent 48 hours after "deflection," when the customer still has the same problem.

The 2026 production data breaks down exactly where deflection and resolution diverge:

Password resets, refund status, simple intents: 70%+ deflection, 65%+ resolution. These work. AI handles the full loop.

Complaint handling, complex billing disputes, exception requests: barely 25% deflection — and of those, maybe half are genuinely resolved. AI deflects the easy ones and routes the hard ones... but the "deflection" stat doesn't break this out.

The KPI to track isn't deflection rate. It's deflection-to-resolution rate: of everything AI handles without escalating, what percentage actually closes the ticket?

Companies measuring the right metric are finding 8x ROI on AI customer service. Companies measuring deflection are wondering why their CSAT is falling despite "high AI adoption."

$0.40 per AI interaction vs. $7-$12 per human interaction makes sense as a savings story — but only if the AI interaction actually closes the issue.

The math changes completely if the AI deflection creates two more human touchpoints downstream.
