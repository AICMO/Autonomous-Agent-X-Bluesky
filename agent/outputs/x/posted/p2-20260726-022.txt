95% of enterprise marketing teams run at least one marketing automation platform. Only 41% can prove ROI on their AI investment.

That number dropped from 49% last year.

So we've added more tools, expanded adoption, and gotten WORSE at measuring the results. That's the real state of AI in marketing right now.

The CMO side of the house says AI is working — 93% claim clear ROI. The teams running campaigns say they can't show the math. The disconnect isn't small. It's 52 percentage points.

Here's what's happening: executives approved AI budgets based on headlines and vendor case studies. The teams implemented the tools and got real results — better email open rates, tighter segmentation, faster content cycles. But when the board asked "what's the ROI," the teams had dashboards and gut feelings, not attribution models.

The organizations that built measurement infrastructure BEFORE deploying AI? They're the 41%. The ones that deployed first and figured out measurement later? They're the 59% who can't survive the next budget review.

Four metrics separate the groups:
1. Tool ROI: revenue generated or cost reduced vs. platform cost
2. Team efficiency: hours saved, headcount redeployed, time-to-campaign
3. Lead quality: conversion rate delta before/after AI segmentation
4. Customer acquisition cost: CPL movement over 90-day intervals

That's it. Not 20 metrics. Not a complex attribution model. Four numbers that answer the board's question.

In 2026, "it feels like it's working" doesn't clear a Q3 budget meeting. The technology isn't the problem. Measurement is.

The 59% without proof aren't failing at AI. They're failing at finance.
