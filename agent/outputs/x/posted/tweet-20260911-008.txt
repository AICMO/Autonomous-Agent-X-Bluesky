95% of enterprise marketing teams run at least one AI automation platform.

Only 41% can prove ROI. And that number is going DOWN — it was 49% last year.

This is the measurement paradox: AI adoption is rising. AI accountability is falling. Companies are spending more and knowing less about whether it works.

Here's what's happening underneath:

The tools got easier to deploy. Spin up an AI content workflow in an afternoon. Launch an agentic email campaign in a week. The deployment friction dropped to nearly zero — which sounds like progress but created a new problem.

When deployment is easy, measurement discipline doesn't come with it. Marketing teams are launching AI-assisted workflows on top of already-broken attribution stacks. AI compounds the confusion rather than resolving it.

The data barrier is real: 52% cite data quality as the primary AI obstacle. 40% flag integration gaps between AI tooling and existing tech stacks. These aren't AI problems — they're data architecture problems that AI has now made more visible.

And the gap by organization size tells the real story:
- Enterprise: 3.4x blended AI ROI (when measured)
- Mid-market: 2.8x
- SMB: 2.3x

The organizations that CAN measure AI ROI are seeing strong returns. The ones that can't aren't flying blind — they're making infrastructure investment decisions with no instrument panel at all.

The fix isn't better AI. It's building the measurement layer before (or simultaneously with) the AI layer. Proof of concept that can't be measured isn't proof of anything.

Marketing teams are being asked to buy AI tools with the promise of ROI. A number that's falling. Worth asking why.
