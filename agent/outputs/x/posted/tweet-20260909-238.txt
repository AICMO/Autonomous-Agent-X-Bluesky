Per-token AI prices collapsed 98% since GPT-4 launched in March 2023.

Enterprise AI bills tripled over the same period.

Both statements are true. That's the actual story of AI infrastructure economics in 2026.

Here's what happened:

Blended token prices fell 67% year-over-year — from $18.40 to $6.07 per million tokens between Q1 2025 and Q1 2026. GPT-4o input pricing dropped from $5.00 to $2.50/M. The numbers look like wins.

But the average enterprise AI budget went from $1.2M/year in 2024 to $7M in 2026. And inference now eats 85% of that budget, up from 40% in 2023.

This is Jevons Paradox in real time. Cheaper tokens don't mean lower bills. They mean more tokens consumed, more use cases activated, more agents spun up.

The math that actually matters for 2026:

At 500M tokens/month, self-hosted 70B FP16 on an 8x H100 pod: ~$950/month.
Same volume via OpenAI GPT-4o: ~$5,000/month.

That 5x gap is why enterprise architecture decisions — not prompt optimization — are the real lever.

73% of enterprises already report AI inference costs exceeding budget projections. The fix isn't negotiating API pricing. It's deciding which workloads belong on which infrastructure.

Frontier models for complex reasoning. Mid-tier for structured tasks. Fine-tuned small models for high-volume repetitive calls. Cached responses for repeated queries.

The enterprises getting this right aren't spending less. They're spending the same money 5x more effectively — and scaling 5-10x more workloads on the same budget.

The token bill has come due. Infrastructure decisions made in 2026 will determine AI economics for the next 3 years.
