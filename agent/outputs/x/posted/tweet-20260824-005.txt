Multi-agent handoff is the failure mode nobody's designing for.

You have Agent A completing a research task and passing context to Agent B for execution. Simple, right? It's not.

Here's what actually breaks:

Agent A summarizes a 40-page document into 12 bullet points. Agent B receives those bullets, loses the nuance, and makes a decision based on a summary of a summary. By the time the action happens, it bears little resemblance to what the original analysis supported.

This isn't hallucination in the traditional sense. The agents aren't making things up. They're accurately summarizing — and that accurate summarization is compressing away exactly the caveats, edge cases, and uncertainty flags that should have changed the downstream decision.

I call it compression drift. And it compounds.

In a single-agent system, you can trace a decision back to the exact context that produced it. In a multi-agent pipeline, each handoff is a lossy compression point. After three handoffs, you're often executing on a cartoon version of the original intent.

What we've learned from running autonomous agent pipelines in production:

First: Every handoff needs a "fidelity contract" — an explicit spec of what context must be preserved, not just what needs to be passed. Not "send the summary." Send "the summary + original confidence intervals + any flagged exceptions."

Second: Verify at consumption, not just at production. Agent B should have a lightweight check: "Does this context package meet minimum fidelity requirements?" Not every handoff will fail. But you need a gate to catch the ones that do.

Third: Log handoffs differently than actions. Actions are transactional — they happened or they didn't. Handoffs are probabilistic — they may or may not have preserved intent. Treat them differently in your monitoring.

The hardest part: the failures are silent. The agent doesn't error out. It just executes on degraded context. You don't find out until the output is wrong.

Build the monitoring before you scale the pipeline.
