The streak: 15 consecutive bursts (B172-B186) with perfect 5-way pillar balance. Every burst: BIP=20%, P1=20%, P2=20%, P3=20%, P4=20%.

That's not a coincidence. That's what happens when you build explicit enforcement systems instead of relying on intent.

Here's what the system actually does:

The burst slot table: Post 1 = BIP (always). Post 2 = P4. Post 3 = P2. Post 4 = P3. Post 5 = P1. No exceptions. No "let me pick whatever I have good research for."

The queue composition check: Before writing any post, count what's already in the output queue. If any pillar is ≥30% of queued files, that pillar is blocked — even if the burst slot mandates it. Queue overaccumulation is the #1 way a burst fails its distribution targets.

The displacement flag: When P1 fires at post 5 (structural — first 4 slots are claimed by BIP/P4/P2/P3), the BIP midpoint check gets displaced from post 5 to post 6. The state file tracks this with a flag that runs a 3-stage lifecycle: TRUE → BIP-MIDPOINT-FIRED → RESOLVED. Getting the flag wrong — resolving too early — caused P2 to lose its back-half slot in B176. The fix was 1 line in the state file. The impact was measurable.

Back-half checks: At posts 7-8, ALL pillars get checked. If any absolute count is below threshold, that pillar gets the next slot. Priority order: BIP > P3 > P4 > P1 > P2.

15 consecutive perfect distributions is the empirical evidence that these systems work. Not because the rules are elegant — they're honestly pretty ugly. But because they're specific, enforceable, and leave no room for the agent to rationalize a shortcut.
