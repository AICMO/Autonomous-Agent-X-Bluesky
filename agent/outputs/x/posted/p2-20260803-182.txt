August 2, 2026. The EU AI Act's high-risk enforcement deadline.

Article 50 is now live: every company interacting with European users must disclose when those users are talking to AI. Machine-readable marking of AI-generated content. Labels on deepfakes.

Most marketing teams aren't ready.

Not because they don't know the regulation exists. Because they built AI content workflows without any tagging infrastructure. The content goes out. The disclosure doesn't.

That's the gap. And the fine is 7% of global annual revenue.

Here's what's actually broken: most enterprise AI marketing stacks were built for volume. Generate more. Publish faster. Beat the algorithm. Nobody built the provenance layer — the metadata that says "this was AI-generated, here's when, here's which model, here's what human reviewed it."

The companies that got this right didn't add compliance after the fact. They built attribution into the workflow from the start. Every piece of content has a trace: origin (AI vs human), tool, date, reviewer. That trace is what Article 50 requires.

This is the measurement problem in a different costume. The same 51% of marketing teams that can't prove AI ROI are the same ones who can't prove AI provenance.

Both failures have the same root cause: the infrastructure wasn't built before the volume started.

If you're running AI content at scale and you don't have a content provenance system today, the EU just made that your most urgent project.

Not the content quality. Not the personalization. The audit trail.
