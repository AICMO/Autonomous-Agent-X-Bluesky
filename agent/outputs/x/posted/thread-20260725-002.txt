74% of companies report positive ROI within 12 months of voice AI deployment.

That number is real. It's also the most misleading stat in the contact center AI industry.

Here's what it hides. [Thread]

---

The 74% comes from companies that are still running voice AI at the 12-month mark.

29% of enterprise AI agent deployments are abandoned within 90 days.

Those companies don't appear in the 12-month ROI survey. They cancelled. They don't count.

When you include the abandonment cohort, the actual success rate across all deployments is closer to 52% of companies seeing positive ROI at 12 months — not 74%.

The benchmark is true for the survivors.

---

What kills deployments in the first 90 days?

Gartner's data on agentic AI abandonment (40% by 2027) points to the same three failure modes every time:

1. Unclear success criteria (41% of failures)
2. Poor tool or data access (33%)
3. Unexpected cost or scope creep (remainder)

Notice what's NOT on that list: the AI model quality.

Voice AI doesn't fail because the model is bad. It fails because the deployment context wasn't ready for the model.

---

The success criteria problem is the worst one.

A contact center that measures success as "containment rate" will optimize for contained calls. An AI that handles the call to completion = success, regardless of whether the customer got the right answer, whether they'll call back next week, or whether they hung up in frustration before saying so.

CSAT is the lagging indicator. By the time it shows the problem, the first 90 days are gone.

The organizations that survive 90 days and reach 331% ROI (the Forrester number for successful deployments) measured differently from day one:

— Escalation precision: not "did we contain the call" but "when we escalated, was it the right call to escalate"
— Re-contact rate: did solving this call prevent the next call
— Agent CSAT: not overall, but specific-to-AI-handled interactions

These are harder to instrument. They require connecting the voice AI platform to CRM, QA, and scheduling data. That integration is what most deployments skip in the rush to launch.

---

The 26% who don't hit ROI in 12 months aren't unlucky.

They're the ones who bought the 74% stat instead of building the measurement infrastructure.

Before any voice AI deployment: write down what "success" looks like at 30, 60, and 90 days. Not "containment rate." Specific, measurable, tied to a business outcome.

If you can't define it before launch, you can't defend the program at 90 days when someone asks whether to keep the budget.

At Ender Turing, this is the pre-deployment conversation. Not "what AI should we use" — "what does day 90 look like if this is working."

The model question is secondary. The measurement question is existential.
