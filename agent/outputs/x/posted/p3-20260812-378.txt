Enterprise confidence in voice AI for complex conversations: 4.37 out of 7.

That's the number from a 2026 cross-industry study of organizations already running production voice AI. Above the midpoint, but well below where organizations will run fully autonomous workflows on sensitive calls.

93% of those same organizations say AI transparency is "very important" or "critical" for deployment.

Here's what's interesting about those two data points together: enterprises are running voice AI at scale while simultaneously not trusting it enough for high-stakes calls and demanding full transparency into what it's doing. That's not contradiction — that's mature deployment thinking.

The confidence gap (4.37/7) is where most contact center AI strategy gets wrong. Two common responses to it:

**Response 1:** "Confidence is low, so let's slow down." This misreads the signal. Confidence below threshold doesn't mean the AI shouldn't be deployed — it means you need a clear call taxonomy that maps AI confidence levels to escalation protocols. For call types where the AI operates at 6+/7 confidence: run it autonomous. For 4-5/7: run with live monitoring. For below 4: human handles with AI assist. The confidence score is a routing tool, not a deployment gate.

**Response 2:** "Confidence will improve, so let's wait." Also wrong. Confidence improves through production data. Waiting for the lab score to hit 7 before deploying means you never get the production data that gets you to 7.

The fraud risk dimension is where governance gets more complex. Synthetic audio has made voice impersonation cheap and scalable. 93% of enterprises prioritize AI transparency partly for this reason — if a voice agent can be deceived or impersonated, the contact center becomes an attack surface. Governing voice AI now includes identity verification protocols, anomaly detection on call patterns, and audit trails that can prove what the AI heard and decided.

What governing dozens of voice agents across chat, voice, and real-time assist channels across languages and regions actually requires: not a single policy document, but a live monitoring layer that surfaces performance variance by channel, agent type, call type, and geography simultaneously.

Most contact centers aren't there yet. They have voice AI running in production and a spreadsheet tracking incidents.
