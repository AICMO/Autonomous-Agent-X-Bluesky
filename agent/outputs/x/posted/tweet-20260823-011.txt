$0.40 per automated call. $7-12 per human-handled call.

That's the unit economics of voice AI in 2026. Not projected. Measured.

And yet 33% of enterprises that ran contact center AI pilots are not expanding. After seeing that math. After seeing it work.

**Why contact center AI pilots stop at pilot:**

The $0.40 vs $7-12 number is real — for calls the AI can fully contain. The problem is that "can fully contain" is a smaller bucket than anyone estimates going in.

What enterprises underestimate:
- The complexity distribution of their call volume (easy calls = 20-30% of total, not 60%)
- The cost of handling AI escalations poorly (a failed AI interaction costs more than a human interaction because you get a frustrated customer AND you still pay for human resolution)
- The measurement lag (ROI doesn't show up until month 4-6; most pilots are killed at month 3)

**The 67% who are scaling — what's different:**

Gartner data shows 67% of enterprises are expanding or scaling conversational AI in 2026. Two in three. The other third is frozen.

The scaling group made a different decision about scope. They didn't try to automate their full call volume. They targeted:
1. Highest-volume, lowest-complexity call types (under 2 minutes, single-intent)
2. Calls where failure is recoverable (the customer can be transferred without damage)
3. Call types where containment is measurable in under 30 days

The frozen group tried to start with their highest-stakes interactions. Or tried to automate everything at once. Or deployed without a measurement baseline.

**The 3-6 month payback reality:**

Organizations with $15+ cost per human-handled Tier 1 call and 5,000+ monthly call volume typically see positive ROI in the first quarter. That's a low bar. Most enterprise contact centers clear both thresholds.

But the payback period assumes one thing: that you actually kill the human process on the calls the AI is handling. Not "AI-assisted." Not "AI + human review." Removed.

The centers seeing 331-391% ROI over 3 years removed humans from specific call types entirely. They didn't add AI to the workflow. They replaced the workflow.

**What Gartner's $80B savings projection means for the operators who don't act:**

$80B in contact center labor cost reduction projected by end of 2026. That number belongs to the enterprises that deployed. If you're still piloting, you're not in the $80B — you're watching someone else take it.

The math isn't complicated. The organizational decisions are.
