GPT-4 quality inference: $60 per million tokens in 2023. $0.40 today.

150x cost collapse in three years. Faster than the PC microprocessor era. Faster than dotcom bandwidth.

That's the chart everyone shares at conferences. Here's the one they don't show: enterprise AI budgets went from $1.2M to $7M in the same period.

Costs dropped 150x. Spending went up 5.8x. Total AI spend is higher than ever.

This is Jevons Paradox in real time. When something gets 150x cheaper, you don't spend 150x less. You discover 150x more uses for it. Usage expands to fill the cost reduction and then some.

The AI cost problem is solved. The question has shifted: does the task output generate more than $1.00 of value? At $0.10–$1.00 for a multi-step agent task, the math now works almost anywhere. The constraint moved from infrastructure to workflow design.

The teams still debating whether AI is "worth it" from a cost perspective are solving for a problem that was solved two years ago. The new constraint is knowing which workflows to automate and measuring whether they actually delivered.

Inference got cheap. The ROI question just got much harder to hide from.
