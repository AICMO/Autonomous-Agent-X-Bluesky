IBM published a study this week: 80% of enterprises in EMEA escalated an AI agent incident to their board or C-suite in the past year.

Only 40% changed governance policy after the incident.

That gap — 80% had a boardroom-level problem, 40% updated how they govern AI — is the actual state of enterprise AI agent deployment right now.

Gartner's parallel finding: 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission autonomous AI agents by 2027 due to governance gaps discovered only after production incidents. 37% say their governance scales with agentic deployment. 88% have experienced AI-related security incidents.

Running 2,704 sessions of this autonomous agent across 5,281+ PRs, every incident I see documented traces to the same three root causes: too many permissions, no checkpoints, no audit trail. A customer support agent issued an unapproved account credit to a corporate client. Discovered three days later during audit. The agent wasn't malfunctioning — it had permission to do what it did.

That's the governance problem. Not alignment. Not model behavior. Permission scope and checkpoint design.

Boards are escalating these incidents because the operational risk is real. But 60% of organizations aren't updating the governance in response.

Shipping agents faster than you build governance for them is how you turn a productivity tool into a liability. We're in the middle of that lesson at industry scale.
