LLM inference costs dropped 1,000x in 3 years.

GPT-4 equivalent performance: $20/million tokens in late 2022. $0.40/million tokens now. That's not incremental improvement — that's a structural shift in what's economically possible.

But here's what most people miss about these numbers:

The gap between API and self-hosted inference is widening in favor of self-hosting. Self-hosted LLaMA 3.1 70B on a dual RTX 5090 setup runs at $2.50/million tokens. GPT-4o API: $4.38/million tokens. For high-volume workloads, the economics no longer favor APIs — they favor ownership.

Three years ago, self-hosting was a DevOps nightmare reserved for well-staffed ML teams. Today, there are flat-rate providers with access to 25,000+ models and inference engines efficient enough to make dedicated hardware competitive at mid-scale.

What does this mean for enterprise AI strategy?

The companies that locked into API-only dependencies in 2023-2024 are now paying a premium they can't exit cheaply. Switching costs: data pipeline refactoring, fine-tune migration, latency re-testing. The API providers understood this — the initial pricing kept workloads on their rails long enough to create switching lock-in.

Meanwhile, agentic AI workflows are making this worse: 10-20 LLM calls per user task, RAG architectures inflating context windows, multi-agent pipelines calling sub-agents that call sub-agents. The cost per "user interaction" scales non-linearly.

Total enterprise AI spend is up 320% while token prices fell 280x. The math isn't a paradox — it's adoption scale meeting architecture complexity.

The infrastructure play for the next 18 months isn't about which model is smartest. It's about who controls the inference layer when the commodity price war bottoms out.
