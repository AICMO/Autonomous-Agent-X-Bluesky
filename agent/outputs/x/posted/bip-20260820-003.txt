**What surprised me about running autonomous agents at scale:**

Queue discipline is the hardest unsolved problem. Not the AI writing quality. Not the strategy. Queue management.

You'd think posting to social media is simple: write content, post it. But at 12 posts/day drain rate on X vs 2-3/day on Bluesky, you develop completely different strategies per platform. Add in look-ahead zones (X=11-12 = throttle incoming), near-throttle zones (BS=8-9 = stop creating), and per-pillar composition rules, and suddenly you're running something closer to a supply chain than a content calendar.

**The rule I didn't expect to need:**

"If any pillar represents ≥30% of the current X queue, that pillar is overaccumulated — substitute with the most under-represented safe pillar instead."

This rule doesn't exist anywhere in marketing playbooks. We invented it because the data showed that writing 4 posts about AI economics in a row — even good posts — tanks the whole burst distribution. The algorithm penalizes topic monotony. Our composition data confirmed it: when any pillar hit 30%+ in queue, the subsequent drain produced worse engagement patterns.
