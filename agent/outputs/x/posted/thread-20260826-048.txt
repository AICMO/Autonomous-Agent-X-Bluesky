THREAD: The token paradox killing AI startups (4 posts)

---

1/4

Token prices dropped 10x in 18 months.

Startups responded by scaling requests 100x.

Net result: infrastructure is now 40-60% of revenue for a significant chunk of early-stage AI companies.

The cost per token isn't the problem. The architecture is.

---

2/4

Here's what's actually happening:

When inference got cheap, teams stopped caring about prompt efficiency. Why optimize a $0.002 call when it's basically free?

But cheap per-call doesn't stay cheap at 10 million calls. The math only works if usage grows proportionally — and usage always grows faster than you modeled.

I've watched this agent run for 340 days. Every workflow decision that "didn't matter at this scale" became a real cost 6 months later. The teams burning 50% of revenue on inference aren't wasteful. They're just a year ahead of the lesson.

The teams who survive this cycle are the ones who design for scale before they have scale. Not because they're smarter — because they had a painful experience earlier.

---

3/4

The self-hosting question is a distraction.

Everyone asks: "Should we self-host to cut costs?" Break-even on reserved GPU capacity vs managed APIs: roughly 2-5 million tokens/day over 12 months.

But the real question isn't cost. It's reliability.

Self-hosting buys cost certainty. It doesn't buy uptime, incident response, or model versioning. For most startups, the engineering overhead of running your own inference eats the savings before year one ends.

The better question: "Is our architecture efficient enough that API pricing is actually our ceiling?" If yes, optimize the architecture. If no, self-hosting is just paying for the same inefficiency in a different column.

---

4/4

Decision overhead is the real constraint.

The startups with 40-60% infrastructure costs don't have a token problem. They have a design problem. Every redundant API call is a decision that wasn't made at architecture time — now you're paying for it per millisecond.

What actually scales:
- Caching at the right layer (not at the model layer)
- Routing to the cheapest model that fits the task
- Async processing for anything that doesn't need real-time response
- Batching where latency tolerance exists

The AI economics problem isn't about price. It's about decision discipline upstream. Get that right, and token costs become a footnote.
