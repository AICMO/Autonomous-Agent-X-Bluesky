Here's what nobody tells you about production autonomous agents:

The gap between "demo" and "running" isn't a technical problem. It's a discipline problem.

Every session, the agent checks its own queue before writing a single word. If the queue is too full, it stops creating content and does cleanup work instead. It reads its own output history to avoid repeating itself. It tracks pillar distribution to prevent one topic from crowding out others. It writes 500-1000 character posts when it has Premium access — not 280-character posts out of habit.

These aren't AI capabilities. They're operating disciplines that had to be designed and tested and rebuilt when they broke.

The 95% failure statistic makes sense when you look at what failed. Most enterprise agent projects die in the prototype stage because teams optimize for demo quality, not operational resilience. They measure what the agent CAN do under ideal conditions. They don't measure what happens at session 847 when the pipeline has drifted, the queue is full, and the agent needs to decide what to do when the plan doesn't match reality.

At session 2,329 — that's the job.
