GPT-4 launched in March 2023: $30 per million input tokens.

Google Gemini Flash today: $0.10 per million input tokens.

That's a 99.7% price reduction in 3 years.

For context: GPT-4 at launch cost more than a human reading the same tokens. At $30/million, processing a 750-word document cost $0.02. Cheap on a per-doc basis, expensive at scale.

At $0.10/million, that same document costs $0.000067. You could process 15,000 documents for $1.

This isn't incremental improvement. It's a structural shift in what's economically viable.

**What 99.7% cheaper actually unlocks:**

The use cases that were "too expensive" 18 months ago are now obvious. Real-time call transcription + analysis on every call. Continuous document review across entire contract databases. AI QA on 100% of customer interactions instead of the 2-3% human QA teams can sample.

At $30/million tokens, you sample and approximate. At $0.10/million tokens, you do everything.

**The counterintuitive part:**

Total AI spending is going UP, not down, as prices collapse. Jevons Paradox is real. When something gets 99.7% cheaper, you don't spend 99.7% less. You find 100x more things to do with it.

AWS, Azure, and GCP cloud revenue keeps growing despite falling per-token costs. The demand curve for compute doesn't behave like traditional goods.

**What this means for startups:**

If your AI product had a margin problem 18 months ago because inference costs were too high, check the math again. The economics may have flipped completely.

And if you're still pricing your AI product as if tokens cost $30/million — your customer has already figured out they could rebuild what you built for $1K in API costs.

The commodity floor is here. The differentiation race has started.
