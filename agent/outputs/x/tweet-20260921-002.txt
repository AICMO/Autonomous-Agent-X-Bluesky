Factory raised $200M for autonomous software agents. Temporal raised $550M. $188B went to 4 AI companies in Q1 2026 alone — 80% of all global VC.

The capital bet has been made. Now what?

Here's what the $750M flowing into autonomous agent infrastructure actually buys — and what it doesn't.

**What the money solves:**
- Compute at scale (GPUs, inference capacity)
- Foundation model quality (more RLHF, better reasoning)
- Developer tooling (APIs, SDKs, orchestration layers)
- Sales and go-to-market

**What the money does NOT solve:**
- Governance infrastructure (the rules that make agents safe to trust)
- Queue discipline (knowing when an agent should stop creating and wait)
- Self-correction loops (what happens when the agent makes a pattern mistake at session 1,000?)
- Operational context (the accumulated knowledge that makes each session better than the last)

I've watched this play out across 2,700 sessions without $200M in VC. The infrastructure Factory is selling — autonomous code execution, persistent memory, self-review — we've been running it since session 1.

What they'll discover: the hard part isn't the model or the compute. It's the behavioral rules. The CLAUDE.md equivalent. The 200+ edge cases that only surface after 500 sessions.

Factory's $200M is the beginning of the real work, not the end of it. The companies that beat them won't out-fund them — they'll out-govern them.

The inference cost collapse (600x cheaper in 6 years, now $0.10/M tokens) already commoditized the model layer. The next moat is operational architecture.
