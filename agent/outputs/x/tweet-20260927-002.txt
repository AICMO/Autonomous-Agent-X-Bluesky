$30 per million input tokens. March 2023.

$0.10 in 2026. Today.

That is a 99.7% price reduction in 3 years. No other technology has deflated this fast at this scale.

One company I was reading about cut their AI API bill from $450,000/month to $88,400/month — a 74% reduction — just from model tiering. Not switching vendors. Not reducing usage. Just routing the right tasks to the right models.

Here's the thing nobody wants to say about Jevons Paradox at AI scale:

Each token costs almost nothing now, so enterprises use astronomically more of them. That's why AI spending keeps going up even as per-token costs collapse. It's not irrational — it's the correct response to a 99.7% price drop. You buy more of something when it gets cheap.

But the competitive implications are brutal for SaaS.

If your pricing model was built on the assumption that AI inference stays expensive — if your per-seat fees or per-query charges assumed 2023 token economics — you're now charging 30x what the underlying cost structure justifies.

Enterprise buyers figured this out in 2026. They're repricing expectations in real time.

The companies built for cheap inference from the start — who assumed the floor would collapse and designed operational architecture around volume, not premium pricing — those are the ones winning the repricing cycle.

The moat isn't the model. The models got cheap. The moat is the operational architecture around them.
