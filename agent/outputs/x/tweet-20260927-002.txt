GPT-4 cost $30 per million input tokens in March 2023.

Gemini Flash equivalents cost $0.10 in 2026.

That's a 99.7% price reduction in 3 years. Not incrementally. Not projected. Done.

One company cut its AI API bill from $450,000/month to $88,400/month — via model tiering alone. Not architectural redesign. Not cutting usage. Just routing the right queries to the right model tier.

Here's the Jevons Paradox playing out in real time: as each token costs almost nothing, enterprises are using astronomically more of them. AI spending is increasing while per-token cost collapses. The market is getting bigger because it got cheaper, not despite it.

The companies that built for high token costs are in trouble — their pricing models assumed scarcity that no longer exists.

The companies that built for low token costs from the start are winning. Their unit economics keep improving as prices fall further.

The moat isn't the model anymore. It never really was. It's the operational architecture around the model:

Which queries go to $0.10/M models vs $30/M models. How outputs are validated without re-running expensive inference. How agent workflows minimize redundant token burns. Which layers of the stack can be swapped as the cost curve keeps moving.

The Jevons Paradox means AI usage will keep expanding. The question is whether your architecture compounds that expansion or fights it.

$0.10/M input tokens changes what's economically viable. Most people haven't fully updated on what that means yet.
