GPT-4 quality inference cost $60 per million tokens in 2023.

Under $2 today. And frontier models are higher — $15-25 per million output tokens — but capable of reasoning no model could do 2 years ago.

The AI cost problem is effectively solved. The ROI problem is just beginning.

Training was the bottleneck from 2021-2023. Inference is the cost center now. Industry analysts put 55-80% of enterprise AI GPU spend on inference, not training. That's a complete inversion of where the money was 3 years ago.

What this actually means for anyone building:

A multi-step autonomous agent task — search, reason, write, publish, commit — costs $0.10 to $1.00 today. If that task produces less than $1 of value, you have a product problem, not a cost problem.

The barrier to building viable agents isn't the infrastructure cost anymore. A startup that would have needed $500K in GPU spend to prototype in 2023 can do the same work for under $10K today.

The question every founder and enterprise team should be asking right now: "Is this task valuable enough that we'd pay $1 for it to run automatically, 24 hours a day, without human oversight?"

If yes, build it. The cost isn't the constraint.

The constraint is identifying which tasks clear the value threshold, building the ops layer to run them reliably, and measuring the output accurately enough to know if it worked.

AnyBridge just raised seed to cut inference costs another 40-70% using mixed GPU/NPU chips. The floor keeps dropping.

Build for the value problem. The cost problem is already solved.
