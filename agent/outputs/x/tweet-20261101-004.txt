Forrester is reporting 331–391% three-year ROI on enterprise voice AI deployments.

The math is simple: $0.40 per AI-handled call versus $7–$12 for a human agent. That's a 90–95% per-interaction cost reduction. Gartner projects this shift will lower contact center labor costs by $80 billion this year alone.

But the deployment failure rate tells a different story. Most enterprises are leaving that ROI on the table.

Here's what I see at Ender Turing working with contact centers on AI implementation:

The ROI is real when you deploy to the right call types first. McKinsey data shows 50–60% of contact center interactions are transactional — order status, basic troubleshooting, appointment scheduling, FAQs. These are fully automatable today. The voice AI is faster (35% shorter handling times), customers like it better (30% higher CSAT), and it costs a fraction.

The ROI falls apart when you try to start with complex interactions. Complaint escalations, high-value retention calls, complex technical support — these are the 40–50% of calls where human judgment genuinely adds value. Deploying voice AI here first is why pilot programs fail.

The 4.1-month payback period isn't a universal number. It's the median for deployments that started with transactional volume. Teams that started with complex volume report 18–24+ month paybacks and often pull the program.

Three things that predict whether a contact center voice AI deployment succeeds:

1. Call categorization before deployment — do you know what percentage of your volume is transactional vs. complex?
2. Hybrid routing architecture — clear rules for when the AI hands off to a human, not just a fallback for failures
3. CSAT measurement at the interaction level, not the session level — aggregate scores hide where the AI is underperforming

The $80 billion opportunity is real. The question is whether you're in the group capturing it or the group that ran a 6-month pilot, got 60% of the way there, and then moved the budget to something else.
