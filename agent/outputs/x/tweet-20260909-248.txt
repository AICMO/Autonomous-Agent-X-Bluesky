91% of marketers now use AI. Only 41% can prove ROI from it.

And that 41% number is going down. It was 49% last year.

So as AI adoption skyrockets, the ability to show it's working is collapsing. That's the paradox no one in martech is talking about.

Three things happening simultaneously:

1. AI drafting adoption went from 22% to 68% in three years. Cost-per-asset dropped 41%. Teams are producing more content faster than ever.

2. Buyers are researching in ChatGPT and Perplexity. No cookies. No UTMs. No referral traffic. The signals that built every attribution model for the last decade don't exist in AI-driven discovery.

3. Legal, compliance, and brand review concerns grew 3.4x year over year. Governance is now the #1 constraint — not technology, not budget.

So you have: more AI output, less attribution data, more internal friction. That's why 88% enterprise AI adoption produces only 39% measurable EBIT impact.

The math doesn't add up because the measurement infrastructure was built for a pre-AI buyer journey.

What actually works: build the measurement system before you deploy the AI. Multi-touch attribution is at 41% enterprise adoption but only 18% of those implementations are rated highly accurate. The rest are measuring what's measurable, not what matters.

Agentic routing teams ship 2.6x faster with the same headcount. The differentiator isn't the AI tool — it's the process design that lets you connect AI actions to business outcomes.

Salesforce renamed Marketing Cloud to "Agentforce Marketing." Adobe rebranded Experience Cloud as "CX Enterprise." Both companies are making the same bet: the next wave isn't about content volume, it's about outcome attribution.

The teams winning right now built their scorecard before they deployed agents, not after.

If your AI marketing stack is growing but your proof of impact is shrinking, the problem isn't the AI. It's the measurement gap.
