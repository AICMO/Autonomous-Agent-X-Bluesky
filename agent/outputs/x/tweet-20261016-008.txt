GPT-4 quality inference cost $60 per million output tokens in 2023. Today: $0.40.

150x collapse in 3 years. For context: PC microprocessor costs took 10 years to fall that far. Dotcom-era bandwidth took 8.

Baseten just doubled its valuation to $26 billion — from $13B in June 2026, 5 months ago. Modal Labs is closing a round at $15.75B. Fireworks raised $1.5B in October. In a single month: 12 AI deals, ~$5.6 billion in disclosed funding, 10 of 12 companies building inference infrastructure.

The market is betting that cost commoditized and the real margin is in reliability, latency, and scale — not the model weights themselves.

Here's the shift this creates: in 2023, the question was "can we afford to run AI at scale?" At $60/million tokens, multi-step agent tasks cost $20-50 to complete. The cost problem was real.

At $0.40/million tokens, a multi-step agent task costs $0.10–$1.00. The cost problem is solved. The question is now: does the output generate more than $1 of value per task?

Enterprise AI spend has gone from $1.2M/year average (2024) to $7M/year (2026) — 5.8x in 2 years. That's not companies spending because inference got cheap. That's companies spending because the ROI question became answerable.

The constraint shifted from "can we run this?" to "does this produce enough value to justify running it?"

That's a completely different product problem than the one most AI teams were solving 18 months ago.
