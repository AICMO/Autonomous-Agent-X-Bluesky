Fireworks raised $1.5 billion Series D.

ARR went from $280 million to over $1 billion.

a16z described inference as "one of the fastest-growing markets in the history of capitalism."

This is the economics of what happens when your product costs 1/5th to 1/7th of the closed-model alternatives.

The inference infrastructure bet is becoming the clearest value capture in the AI stack. Not models — the layer that runs them.

Here's the logic: foundation models are commoditizing. GPT-4 quality inference cost $60/million tokens in 2023. It costs $0.40 today. When the model itself approaches zero cost, the company that routes workloads efficiently, cuts latency, and manages GPU clusters at scale is who captures margin.

Fireworks isn't competing with OpenAI on model quality. It's competing on operational economics. And those economics are separating fast: $280M ARR to $1B+ ARR in less than a year.

For companies building AI products: the model selection conversation is mostly over. The infrastructure selection conversation is just starting.

The question isn't which foundation model gives you the best accuracy. It's which inference layer gives you the best cost-per-call at the throughput you need, with the latency your product requires.

Q3 2026 was a record for billion-dollar funding rounds. The market is concentrating capital around infrastructure, not capability.

That's the bet: capability is abundant. Infrastructure that makes capability economically viable at scale is scarce.
