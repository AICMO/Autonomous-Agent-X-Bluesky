98% of contact centers now use AI.

Only 25% have actually integrated it into their daily workflows.

That gap is the entire story of where the contact center industry is right now.

Installing AI is not the same thing as running AI. The 98% number means procurement happened. The 25% number means transformation happened. They're not the same event and they don't produce the same results.

The 98% who've "deployed" AI are largely running it alongside existing human workflows — using it for QA scoring or summarization, where it augments without replacing any core operational step. The 25% who've fully integrated it have done something harder: they've redesigned the workflow around what AI can actually handle.

That redesign is where the economics change. Voice AI costs $0.40 per call. A skilled human agent costs $7-12. That $0.40 number only applies if the AI is actually handling the call — not just sitting next to someone who's handling it.

The contact centers running at full integration aren't better at AI. They made a harder organizational decision: they drew clear lines around which call types go to AI, accepted a temporary CSAT dip during calibration (CSAT for voice AI is 3.6/5 vs 4.1/5 for skilled human agents at maturity), and committed to the transition instead of running a permanent "test."

The 73% who have AI installed but not integrated are not behind on technology. They're behind on the decision.

That decision gets harder to defer every quarter. The 25% are compounding a structural cost advantage while the other 73% are paying for both the AI subscription and the full human workforce.

It's not a technology gap. It's a commitment gap.
