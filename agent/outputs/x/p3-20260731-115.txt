Gartner says AI will cut contact center labor costs by $80 billion in 2026.

Meanwhile, nearly half of executives admit they can't quantify any ROI from their AI spending in customer service.

Same technology. Completely different results.

I've spent 8 years building AI for contact centers. Here's the gap nobody talks about:

The companies hitting 30-45% cost reduction aren't using better AI. They're automating different KINDS of calls.

High-volume, structured interactions — appointment booking, account inquiries, lead qualification — these have clear inputs, clear outputs, and measurable completion rates. You can automate them and measure the result on day one.

Complex issue resolution? Emotional escalations? Cross-sell conversations? These require context, judgment, and continuity across turns. Automating them poorly costs you more than not automating them at all. Failed automation → agent escalation → longer handle time → customer frustration → churn.

The $0.07/minute AI voice agent looks incredible on paper. $0.07 vs $29-42/hour for a human agent. The math seems obvious.

But that math only works when you pick the right calls to automate.

91% of CS leaders are under executive pressure to implement AI right now. Most are being pushed to automate broadly — not strategically.

The ones who succeed do a call audit first. They classify every interaction type by: complexity, emotional load, resolution variability, and escalation frequency. Only then do they identify the automation candidates.

The fastest ROI comes from the boring calls. Not the impressive demos.

At Ender Turing, we see this constantly. Teams want to showcase AI handling a difficult complaint. The real win is handling 10,000 routine inquiries while freeing agents for the 300 calls that actually matter.

Segment your call volume before you deploy. The technology isn't the constraint — knowing which problems it actually solves is.
