$510 billion.

That's how much startups raised in the first half of 2026 alone. More than all of 2025 combined.

Then September arrived and two companies raised $2.5 billion more in a single week: Temporal Technologies at $550M, Cognition AI at $2B on a $48 billion valuation.

Cognition is 2 years old. Their ARR went from $492M to $900M in 90 days. For comparison: Salesforce took over a decade to reach $1B ARR.

This is not a bubble. It's a structural shift in how capital flows into infrastructure layers.

Here's what the VC data actually says about where this goes:

The $510B isn't spread across sectors. It's concentrated in AI infrastructure — the compute, orchestration, and tooling layers that everything else runs on. Investors are not betting on applications. They're betting on the plumbing.

Why? Because the infrastructure bet has historical precedent. AWS built for 2-3% margins that scaled to billions. The same logic applies here: whoever owns the inference layer, the agent orchestration layer, the enterprise memory layer — they capture margin at every transaction above them.

The Cognition $48B valuation is less about Cognition and more about the market's answer to one question: how fast does AI coding infrastructure compound?

Their answer: $492M → $900M ARR in 90 days. That's the answer.

What this means for operators and founders:

1. The infrastructure window is closing. Early rounds for the "plumbing layer" are already pre-empted at 9-figure valuations. If you're building infrastructure, you're competing with $510B of capital behind established players.

2. The application layer is still wide open. $510B going into infrastructure is a signal, not a ceiling. Applications built on that infrastructure — especially vertical ones with real workflow depth — are still in early innings.

3. The Jevons Paradox continues: AI inference costs dropped 99.7% in 3 years. Usage is up astronomically. More spending, not less. The cheaper it gets, the more enterprises use. This is the infrastructure math that justifies $48B valuations.

The bet is getting bigger, not smaller. And so far, the data is on the side of the bettors.
