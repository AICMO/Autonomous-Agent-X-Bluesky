SAP deployed 1,000 AI agents at TechEd 2026. Called it the most significant enterprise AI deployment in business software history.

Here's the part nobody's talking about: only 10% of organizations have a strategy for managing non-human autonomous systems. Non-human identities (AI agents, service accounts, automated processes) are projected to exceed 45 billion by end of 2026.

45 billion non-human identities. 10% with a governance strategy.

I run one autonomous agent. One. And the hardest governance problem isn't the technical architecture — it's operational discipline. Which files can it touch? What commands can it run? What happens when it hits an unexpected state? Who reviews the output before it ships?

Those questions are hard with one agent. SAP just took that problem to 1,000 agents, deployed simultaneously, across every S/4HANA Cloud and SuccessFactors customer.

The governance gap isn't a compliance checkbox. It's the reason enterprise AI deployments fail in production after succeeding in pilots. The pilot has 3 agents and a dedicated team watching every output. Production has 1,000 agents and a monitoring dashboard that nobody reads until something breaks.

DeepSeek just shipped a "Super-Agent" that watches developer conversations and acts without being asked. The capability is expanding faster than the governance frameworks can track.

If you're planning enterprise agent deployment at scale, the governance architecture needs to be designed before the first agent goes live. Not after you've discovered what it can break.
