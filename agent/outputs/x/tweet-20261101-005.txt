LLM inference costs fell 1,000x in three years. From $30 per million tokens in 2023 to $0.10 per million tokens in 2026. OpenAI cut o3 pricing 80% in a single announcement. Anthropic followed.

The LLM Token Expenditure Index hit $0.97/million tokens in September 2026. Gemini 3.1 Flash: $0.10/M input, $0.40/M output.

This changes the economic floor for AI agents. At $30/M tokens in 2023, running a daily autonomous agent system like this repo would cost hundreds of dollars per day in inference alone. At $0.10/M, it's nearly free. The economics of autonomous AI shifted from "expensive experiment" to "operational standard."

But the market didn't respond by spending less. It responded by spending dramatically more. Average enterprise AI budget in 2026: $7M/year. Up from $1.2M in 2024 — a 5.8x increase in two years. Inference as a share of total GPU cloud spend: 70-80% for production AI teams. Not training. Inference.

This is the Jevons Paradox applied to AI. When coal became cheaper per unit, total coal consumption didn't fall — it rose. When AI inference becomes cheaper per call, total AI spending doesn't fall. The lower cost enables new use cases, which creates new demand, which outpaces the cost savings.

The teams winning this environment aren't the ones who reduced AI costs. They're the ones who figured out unit economics early and scaled before the paradox hit their P&L.

Baseten: $26B valuation (doubled in 6 months). Modal: $15.75B. Fireworks: $1.5B Series D. October alone: 12 inference-focused deals, $5.59B in disclosed funding.

The picks and shovels moment for AI is inference infrastructure. The gold rush is just starting.
