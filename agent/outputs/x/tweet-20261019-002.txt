Token costs dropped 280x in 3 years. Your AI bill went up 320% in the same period.

Both numbers are true. Here's why they coexist:

1 user action in an agentic system now triggers 12–20 LLM calls.

Not 1. Not 3. 12 to 20 on average.

The headline token price is real. The effective per-action cost is 15x the headline price because you're not running 1 call per interaction — you're running a chain.

This is the inference math most AI roadmaps don't have a line for. Then it becomes the biggest line on the P&L.

Quick timeline:
- GPT-4 quality inference: $60/million tokens (2023) → $0.40 (2026). 150x on the budget tier.
- Budget-tier tokens: $30/million (early 2023) → $0.40 (May 2026). 280x cheaper.
- Enterprise AI bills: up 320% over the same period.
- Inference now represents 85% of every AI dollar spent. Was 40% in 2023.

OpenAI just cut Luna pricing by 80% (July 2026). That's good news. But the math only changes when you also address how many times per user action you're calling the API.

The cost problem used to be "tokens are too expensive."

That's not an inference problem. That's an architecture problem.
