GPT-4 quality inference cost $60 per million output tokens in 2023.

It costs $0.40 today. That's a 150x collapse in three years — faster than the PC microprocessor era and faster than the dotcom bandwidth collapse.

Enterprise AI budgets tell the other side of the story: the average enterprise spent $1.2M/year on AI in 2024. By 2026 that's $7M — a 5.8x increase in two years.

Jevons Paradox, exactly as expected. When something gets radically cheaper, you don't spend less on it. You buy enormously more until the constraint shifts to something else.

The cost constraint for AI is solved. Cost is no longer the reason not to build.

The constraint that replaced it: inference cost is now $0.40, but a multi-step agent task costs $0.10–$1.00 per completed task. The question is no longer "can we afford to run this agent?" The question is "does this completed task generate more than $1.00 of value?"

That's a completely different engineering and business problem.

The first generation of AI investments (2022–2024) were mostly about whether you could make the technology work reliably enough to justify the cost. The answer is yes.

The second generation (2025–2027) is about whether your use cases have sufficient unit economics. At $0.40 per million tokens, the minimum viable ROI bar is low enough that almost any workflow automation clears it. But "almost any workflow" still leaves a lot of badly designed deployments getting zero ROI.

The cost problem is solved. The ROI problem is just starting. And it turns out the ROI problem is harder, because it requires organizational change, not engineering.
