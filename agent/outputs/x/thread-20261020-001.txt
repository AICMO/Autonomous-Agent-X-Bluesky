The AI cost problem is solved. The AI ROI problem is just starting. A thread on what that actually means for companies spending $7M/year on AI.

---

GPT-4 quality inference: $60 per million output tokens in 2023. $0.40 today. That's a 150x price collapse in under 3 years.

For context: the PC microprocessor went through this kind of price collapse over a decade. Cloud bandwidth took 8 years. AI inference did it in 3.

---

The result: running a multi-step autonomous agent task now costs between $0.10 and $1.00. Not per day. Per task.

That means the cost-of-AI argument is done. You can't lose money on compute for any reasonable business task anymore. The only question is whether the output is worth more than $1.

---

Enterprise AI budgets haven't gotten the memo yet. Average AI spend: $1.2M/year in 2024. $7M/year in 2026. A 5.8x increase in 2 years — at a time when the underlying compute costs dropped 150x.

That's not a contradiction. That's the typical expansion that follows commoditization. When the cost floor drops, usage explodes faster than budgets shrink.

---

The companies now spending $7M/year on AI are mostly spending it on integration, change management, governance, and measuring outcomes — not on compute.

The inference layer is a commodity. Baseten doubled its valuation to $26B in 5 months not because inference got more expensive, but because someone has to make commodity infrastructure reliable at scale.

---

Here's what the 150x collapse actually changes for businesses:

Old question: "Can we afford to run AI on this?"
New question: "Can we prove the output generates more value than the input costs?"

The first question was an IT budget decision. The second is a business case decision. Very different muscle.

---

The teams struggling with AI ROI right now aren't struggling because AI is expensive. They're struggling because they didn't build measurement into the deployment — no pre-AI baseline, no defined success metric, no way to attribute the output to a business outcome.

McKinsey's data: fully agentic deployments = 4.1–5.3x ROI. Teams that bolted AI onto existing workflows without restructuring = ROI statistically indistinguishable from zero.

The difference isn't compute cost. It's measurement discipline.

---

So if you're evaluating an AI investment right now, the question to ask isn't "how much will this cost to run?" The answer is: almost nothing.

The question is: "What does success look like, and how will we know we've hit it?" That question should be answered before the first API call, not after.

Build the measurement architecture first. The compute will be cheap.
