CC AI ROI isn't a one-time savings calculation. It compounds.

Year 1 median payback: 5.4 months.
Year 2 ROI: 4.1x median, 6.7x top quartile.
Year 3: 331-391% (Forrester, across 500+ deployments).

But most contact centers are still in year 1 math. They're measuring whether AI handles calls correctly. That's the wrong question to be asking in year 1.

The right question in year 1: are you building the infrastructure that makes year 2 and 3 compounding possible?

Here's what the top-quartile performers do differently in year 1:

**They redesign QA before scaling volume.**

Standard QA reviews 3-5% of calls manually with human criteria. AI-first environments need different review criteria — containment quality, escalation trigger accuracy, sentiment divergence rates. Teams that build AI-native QA in year 1 aren't spending 18 months retrofitting their compliance process in year 2.

**They instrument handle time separately for AI-resolved vs AI-assisted vs human-only calls.**

Handle time drops 35-40% on AI-resolved calls, but that improvement gets diluted in blended averages. Teams that don't segment performance data can't prove the ROI internally — so AI budget gets cut before year 2 compounding materializes.

**They treat the human-AI split as an architecture decision, not a staffing call.**

76% of CC leaders are formalizing this split now: AI owns routing, availability, and volume; humans own complexity and emotional stakes. Organizations that draw this line deliberately in year 1 don't spend year 2 managing agent attrition from poorly-scoped AI handoffs.

The $80B in projected CC labor cost reduction in 2026 isn't going to the 88% who deployed AI.

It's going to the 25% who operationalized it.

Deployment is table stakes. Operationalization determines whether the ROI compounds or disappears at renewal time.

At Ender Turing we watch this play out in production. Year 1 is where most of the compounding infrastructure gets built — or doesn't.
