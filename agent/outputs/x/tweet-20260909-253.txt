OpenAI loses $1.35 for every $1 it earns.

$3.7B in 2025 revenue. $5B in operating losses. Primary driver: inference costs from serving billions of daily ChatGPT and API requests.

You're not paying $0.40/M tokens because AI got efficient. You're paying $0.40/M tokens because Sequoia, Microsoft, and SoftBank are subsidizing the difference.

**What happens when the subsidies end:**

Frontier model pricing will normalize upward within 12-24 months. It won't be subtle. A customer paying $20/month and consuming $18-25 in inference already eats your margin — that's under the current subsidized rate. When true costs surface, that math gets worse.

**Three structural reasons inference stays expensive:**

1. Sequential token generation can't be parallelized. LLMs produce one token at a time. That's a physics constraint, not a software problem.
2. Agentic workloads burn 5-30x more tokens per task than a chatbot. Context windows inflate 3-5x with RAG. The more capable your AI, the bigger the bill.
3. Frontier providers are in a deliberate below-cost pricing war. They're buying market share with VC money. That game has an end date.

**What's actually changing:**

IndexCache: 15-25% compute reduction. Attention Residuals: 1.25x efficiency. AWS Bedrock + Cerebras: 5x throughput improvement. Real breakthroughs — but they're cost optimizations inside a broken unit economics model, not a fix for it.

**The enterprise mistake:**

Companies locked in multi-year agent workflows and internal pricing assumptions based on today's subsidized rates. When normalization hits, those assumptions blow up.

The startups that survive this aren't the ones with the lowest burn rate. They're the ones who built model routing, tiered usage controls, and caching before they scaled — so when the subsidy ends, their unit economics still work.

The ones who just plugged in the API and prayed? That's a different story.
