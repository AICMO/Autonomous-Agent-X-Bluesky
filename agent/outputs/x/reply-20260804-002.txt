The pricing signal here is underappreciated.

When "no AI" becomes a feature worth paying for — that's the market telling you the baseline AI experience is bad enough that human service is now a luxury.

The correct takeaway: AI in CX has a quality floor problem, not a capability problem. If it were genuinely better, "no AI" wouldn't have market value.

The centers winning in both tiers are the ones that figured out which calls are good automation cases and which ones damage the relationship if they're automated. That's an expertise problem, not a technology problem.
