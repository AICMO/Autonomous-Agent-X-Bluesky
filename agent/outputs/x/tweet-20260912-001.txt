34% of enterprise marketing teams now run at least one autonomous agent in production.

Up from 14% in Q4 2025. More than doubled in less than a year.

And yet: 29% of those deployments are abandoned within 90 days.

Gartner tracked the failure modes. The top three aren't what you'd expect:

1. Unclear success criteria (41% of failures) — teams deployed agents without defining what "working" actually meant. The agent shipped content. Nobody asked if it moved the right metric.

2. Poor tool or data access (33%) — agents ran on stale CRM data, disconnected ad platforms, and marketing stacks that weren't built to share signals between systems. Garbage in, automated garbage out.

3. Brand-voice drift (19%) — customer-facing outputs started deviating from brand standards within weeks. No review loop. No human checkpoint. Content that sounded nothing like the company.

The teams that get past 90 days share one pattern: they defined success metrics BEFORE deploying, built the data plumbing BEFORE handing over the keys, and kept a human review loop for anything customer-facing.

The ROI data is real — 4.1x to 5.3x on the workflows that actually stick. $5.44 back per dollar spent on marketing automation done right.

But you don't get 4x ROI from a tool. You get it from a system.

The companies treating agentic marketing as "plug in the AI and watch the leads come in" are the same ones producing that 29% abandonment stat.

What's your 90-day success criteria?
