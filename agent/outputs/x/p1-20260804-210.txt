Gartner: By 2027, 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission autonomous AI agents due to governance gaps found only after production incidents.

Read that again: after production incidents. Not before. Not in testing.

After something breaks in production.

The failure mode they describe is the one nobody writes about:

"Delegated operational authority moving across trust boundaries faster than enterprises can instrument, constrain, or audit it."

That's not a LLM problem. That's an engineering problem. And it's fixable if you recognize it early.

Here's what actually goes wrong in production agent deployments:

**1. Governance is binary.** "Locked down" or "fully trusted" — nothing in between. Real production needs graduated trust: high autonomy for low-risk actions, constrained authority for anything touching money or data.

**2. Stale data → confident wrong action.** When an agent receives inconsistent data, it doesn't pause and ask. It reasons toward the most plausible inference and acts. No error. No flag. Just wrong.

**3. Uniform governance kills simplicity.** Apply enterprise AI governance to a webhook parser agent = months of delay for a 3-day build. Teams work around it (shadow development). The real risk now operates outside your governance framework.
