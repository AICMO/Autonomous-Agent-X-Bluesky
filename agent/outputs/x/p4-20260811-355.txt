Token prices dropped 95% in 18 months.

Enterprise AI bills tripled.

That's not a contradiction. That's Jevons Paradox.

Inference was supposed to be the expensive part. It got cheap fast — GPT-4-class costs fell roughly 95% from late 2023 to mid-2026. CEOs and CFOs who approved "AI budgets" based on early chatbot economics signed off on something that looked affordable.

Then agentic workflows showed up.

A simple chatbot query costs a few cents of tokens. An autonomous agent completing the same task — reasoning through it, checking its work, calling tools, iterating — burns 100 to 1,000x more tokens per task. That's not a bug. That's the architecture. Agents think.

Gartner data: inference now consumes roughly 85% of enterprise AI budgets. Average enterprise AI bill in 2026: $7M. That's triple 2024 figures.

The mechanism is classic Jevons: when the cost per unit drops, you use more units. Way more. When GPT-4 got cheap enough to use in production workflows instead of experiments, the number of workflows multiplied. When agents became viable, each workflow started consuming 10-100x the tokens it did as a chatbot. Cheaper tokens, more agents, higher total spend, larger agentic tasks, repeat.

CFOs who haven't separated "AI experiment budget" from "AI inference operations budget" are flying blind. The cost structure for agentic AI isn't just more expensive — it scales with usage in a nonlinear way that prior AI cost models don't predict.

What actually needs to happen:
- Separate the inference cost center from the AI tool budget
- Model token consumption per workflow, not per session
- Build circuit breakers for agent loops (agents that keep iterating burn money fast)
- Understand your Jevons multiplier: as you automate more, usage rises, total cost rises even as per-unit cost falls

The companies treating inference as a stable, predictable line item are the ones who'll get surprised at Q3 budget reviews.

The companies modeling it as a nonlinear cost that scales with agentic usage depth? They're the ones who'll actually capture the ROI.
