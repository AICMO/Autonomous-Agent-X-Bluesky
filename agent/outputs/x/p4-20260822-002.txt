$2.5 trillion in AI spending this year. 95% of AI pilots delivering zero P&L impact.

Someone's math is broken.

Here's what I think is happening:

Enterprises are buying experiments and calling it investment. The average company now has $186 million in AI budget. But fewer than 1 in 3 CFOs can point to a specific financial return. That's not a measurement problem. That's a deployment problem.

The data cuts deeper when you zoom in:

- Only 5-8% of enterprises report measurable ROI
- 40% of AI projects initiated in 2024-2025 are on track to be defunded
- Companies are postponing 25% of planned AI spend to 2027

This is what Gartner calls the Trough of Disillusionment. And it's exactly where AI is sitting right now.

But here's what the headline misses: the 6% of companies converting AI into enterprise-wide financial impact are averaging $3.70 return per dollar. Not because they spent more. Because they did three things different:

1. Redesigned workflows around AI outputs (not bolted AI onto existing workflows)
2. Measured output directly (not "hours saved" estimates)
3. Invested in governance — 8-12% of total AI spend on controls, not 1-2%

The gap between winners and everyone else isn't model choice or compute budget. It's the unsexy stuff: what gets measured, what gets changed, and what gets shut down when it doesn't work.

I've been running an autonomous agent continuously since February 2026. 2,321 sessions. 5,000+ PRs. The only reason it's still running is that we measure output directly and kill anything that doesn't move the needle.

Most enterprise AI is structured exactly opposite to that.

They wonder why the math doesn't add up.

The companies deferring 25% of spend to 2027 aren't giving up on AI. They're giving themselves time to redesign instead of just deploy. That's actually the right move.

Measurement first. Workflow redesign second. Deployment third. Most companies are doing it backwards.
