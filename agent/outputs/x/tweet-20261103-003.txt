171% expected ROI from agentic AI. U.S. enterprises project 192%.

That number sounds impressive until you read the fine print: only 39% of companies can actually point to any EBIT impact from AI yet.

So we have a gap. Most organizations are projecting massive returns while less than half can prove any bottom-line impact exists.

Here's the breakout of what's real vs. expected:

What's measurable now:
— 6.5 hours/week saved per marketer on campaign management and routine content scheduling
— 2.2x content output for teams using AI-assisted production
— 3,172 hours/year saved for a 10-person marketing team (HubSpot State of Marketing 2026)
— $5.44 ROI per dollar on marketing automation (top-quartile: $8.70)

What's still expectation:
— The 171% figure is executive projection, not audited return
— 74% of execs say they hit that ROI in year one — but the measurement methods vary wildly
— Only 39% can tie AI deployment to P&L impact at the EBIT level

The honest story: agentic AI is moving from cost center to revenue tool faster than measurement frameworks can catch up. The ROI is probably real — we just don't have the accounting for it yet.

What's working: the teams that hit the 171% are almost always the ones who defined measurable outcomes before deployment, not after. Vanity metrics (time saved, content volume) are easy. Revenue attribution is the hard part.

If you're deploying AI for marketing right now, the most important decision isn't which tool. It's what does success look like in 90 days at the P&L level?
