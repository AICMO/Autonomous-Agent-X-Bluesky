$30 per million input tokens in March 2023.

$0.10 in September 2026.

That's not a discount. That's a 99.7% price collapse in 3 years.

Here's what that number actually means for the companies building on AI infrastructure — and the ones who haven't figured it out yet:

---

One SaaS company quietly cut its monthly AI API bill from $450,000 to $88,400. Same workload. No architectural overhaul. Just model tiering: route low-complexity tasks to cheap models, reserve expensive inference for decisions that actually require it.

74% cost reduction. $4.3M annualized savings. Via config changes, not re-engineering.

This is the current state of AI infrastructure optimization. Most companies haven't touched it.

---

The Jevons Paradox is playing out at AI scale:

As costs drop 99.7%, total enterprise AI spending is *increasing*.

When tokens were expensive, companies rationed AI usage carefully. At $30/million, every call was a cost decision. At $0.10/million, you stop counting and start using.

The result: enterprises that carefully controlled AI usage in 2023-2024 are now running 500-1000x more inference volume. Cost per unit is down 99.7%. Total spend is up. The math works because AI is now woven into every workflow, not just premium use cases.

---

Here's the implication that almost nobody is talking about:

The companies that built for *high token costs* are repricing their entire model. Per-seat SaaS with AI features embedded is getting squeezed — the AI component cost is approaching zero, but license fees haven't moved.

The companies that built for *low token costs from day one* are now widening their moat. When your cost structure assumes $0.10/million, you design differently: more AI calls per user interaction, more automated reasoning loops, more aggressive agent architectures.

---

Operational architecture — not the model — is the competitive advantage now.

In 2023: the moat was access to the best model.
In 2026: the moat is the operational layer built around commodity inference.

Who decides which tasks go to which tier? How do you instrument the routing? How do you measure output quality vs cost tradeoff at scale?

That's the work. Most enterprises haven't started it.
