88% of contact centers use AI. Only 25% have operationalized it.

That gap — 63 percentage points between "uses AI" and "actually deployed it at scale" — is the most important number in contact center technology right now.

It means the market isn't waiting for better AI. The AI works. 391% three-year ROI from voice AI (Forrester, 2025). Payback in under 6 months. Cost per call drops from $7-12 (human) to $0.40 (AI). That's a 95% cost reduction that's been verified in production.

The market is waiting for someone to close the operationalization gap.

Here's what that gap actually is — not a technology problem, but three compounding organizational failures:

**1. Deployment without governance**
Organizations stand up AI routing, AI quality scoring, AI call summaries — but no one owns the performance loop. The AI runs. Nobody measures it. Nobody knows if it's drifting.

**2. Pilots that stay pilots**
67% of Fortune 500 companies are running production voice AI systems. The rest are in endless pilot mode. Pilot-to-production failure rate: 88% (IDC). Not because the pilots fail technically — but because success criteria weren't defined before the pilot started.

**3. Data infrastructure lag**
Voice AI that delivers ROI is built on verified interaction data, QA from deployment, and feedback loops that keep performance calibrated. Most contact centers have none of this when they deploy.

The 75% of contact centers that are stuck aren't stuck because AI is hard. They're stuck because they deployed AI into an organization that wasn't ready to own it.

That's a consulting and integration problem, not a technology problem.

The next 18 months will see consolidation: vendors who solve the operationalization problem specifically (not just model performance) will capture the market that the pilot-stuck 75% represents.

It's a large market. $80B in labor cost reduction is the Gartner number for 2026 alone.
