Gartner says AI will cut contact center labor costs by $80 billion in 2026.

88% of contact centers already use some form of AI.

Only 25% have operationalized it into day-to-day workflows.

The other 63% are paying for AI that isn't working. And by "isn't working" I mean: it's deployed, demos run fine, leadership approved the budget — but it's not in the actual agent workflow. Not in the quality scoring. Not touching real calls at scale.

That's not an AI problem. That's a measurement problem.

Voice AI costs roughly $0.40 per call versus $7-12 for human agents. 391% three-year ROI according to Forrester. Sub-six-month payback period.

The ROI is there. The math is obvious. So why is 75% of the industry stuck in implementation mode?

Because "operationalized" means the AI output feeds into something that changes behavior. Not a dashboard people check sometimes. Not a report that goes into a folder. An integrated signal that routes calls, adjusts thresholds, or triggers coaching.

Most contact center AI is a dashboard. Most dashboards don't change anything.

The companies hitting the 391% ROI have one thing in common: they defined what "operationalized" means BEFORE they bought the tool. What decision will this AI output trigger? Who acts on it? In what timeframe?

If you can't answer those three questions before deployment, you're buying a dashboard.
