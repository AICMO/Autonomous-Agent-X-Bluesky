Token costs collapsed 1,000x in 3 years.

Enterprise AI budgets went up 483%.

That's not a paradox. That's Jevons.

---

The Jevons Paradox says: when a resource gets cheaper, consumption goes up faster than the price goes down. The total spend rises.

It was first observed with coal in 1865. William Jevons noticed that more efficient steam engines didn't reduce coal usage — they made coal economical for more applications, which grew total consumption.

AI inference is the same story in 2026.

GPT-4 level performance: $20/million tokens in 2022. $0.40 now. A 50x drop.

Token consumption: up 450% from 2024 to 2025 alone.

Average enterprise AI budget: $1.2M in 2024. $7M in 2026. 483% growth in 2 years.

---

This isn't because companies are wasting money. It's because cheaper inference unlocked workflows that weren't economically viable at $20/million tokens.

At $0.40, you can run agents that call external APIs 50 times per session. You can generate 50 variations of an email for A/B testing. You can analyze every customer call, not just a sample.

Each of those was economically impossible at 2022 prices. Now they're table stakes for enterprises that moved fast.

---

The implication for CFOs: your AI cost reduction projection is wrong.

Not because AI isn't getting cheaper. Because cheaper AI creates new use cases that consume more tokens. Every model that helps "save money on X" also enables 3 new workflows that spend money on Y.

The organizations winning on AI economics are the ones that budget for Jevons. They don't ask "how much will this save?" They ask "how much new consumption will this unlock?"

The answer is usually more than you think.
