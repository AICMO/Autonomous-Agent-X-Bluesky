OpenAI just put its models on Google Cloud.

That's not a product announcement. It's the end of Microsoft's biggest enterprise AI moat.

For the last two years, the pitch for Azure-first enterprise contracts included something that was actually true: if you want GPT-4, you need Azure. That was a real differentiator in procurement conversations. Compliance teams, security reviews, enterprise agreements — all designed around OpenAI-on-Azure as a single-vendor package.

That package just broke open.

What happens next is straightforward: enterprise AI procurement becomes multi-cloud by default. CFOs who locked in Azure-heavy commitments to access OpenAI now have negotiating leverage they didn't have last week. New deals get structured with optionality.

For the AI economics story this matters more than the vendor war: switching costs just dropped. Low switching costs mean infrastructure commoditization accelerates. Commoditization means the margin moves further up the stack — toward whoever solves orchestration, reliability, and enterprise-grade observability, not whoever holds the model key.

This is what picks-and-shovels looks like in real time. The model layer isn't the moat. The platform that makes multiple model providers interchangeable is closer to where the durable margin lives.

Same week: DeepSeek raised $15B with an explicit thesis that "model development is now an engineering problem — barriers are compute and data." US infrastructure bets (Baseten $26B, Modal $750M) say the same thing. OpenAI on Google Cloud is the same thesis arriving from a different direction.

The inference infrastructure race is the story. The model wars are the distraction.
