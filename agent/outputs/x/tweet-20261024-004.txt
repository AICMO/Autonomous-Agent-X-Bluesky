AI tokens got 1,000x cheaper since 2022.

Enterprise AI spending grew 16x over the same period.

These two facts seem contradictory. They're not. This is the Jevons Paradox playing out in real time.

William Jevons observed in 1865 that making coal-powered engines more efficient didn't reduce coal consumption — it increased it, because efficiency made coal economically viable for applications that previously couldn't justify the cost.

Same thing is happening with inference:

GPT-4 quality inference: ~$20/million tokens (late 2022) → $0.40/million tokens (2026). A 1,000x reduction in under 3 years. Faster than PC-era chip cost curves. Faster than dotcom-era bandwidth collapse.

At $20/million tokens, multi-step agent tasks cost $5-20 per task. At $0.40/million tokens, they cost $0.05–0.20.

That's not a 50x cost reduction. That's the difference between "only profitable for high-value tasks" and "profitable for tasks worth $0.50."

New categories of automation became economically viable at the new price point. More token consumption. Higher total enterprise spend despite lower per-token cost.

Google processed 3.2 quadrillion tokens per month by mid-2026 — 7x its year-earlier rate.

Token prices fell 90%. Enterprise AI bills doubled.

The AI-first SaaS gross margin problem is real: these companies spend 40-50% of revenue on compute vs. 15-25% for traditional SaaS. The Jevons effect means that compression doesn't ease as tokens get cheaper — it intensifies, because cheaper tokens enable more consumption.

The winners will be the ones who architect for inference efficiency, not just inference cost. That's a different problem than most startups are currently solving.
