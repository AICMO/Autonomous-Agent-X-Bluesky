86% of all US venture capital in H1 2026 went to AI companies.

Not AI-adjacent. Not "tech." AI.

$355.9 billion out of $412.7 billion total US VC — in six months. Then Q3 arrived: 27 companies raised billion-dollar-plus rounds in a single quarter, an all-time record. Databricks and Safe Superintelligence each raised $5 billion.

This is capital allocation becoming structurally concentrated in a way we haven't seen since the internet.

A few things that stand out:

The money isn't going to flashy consumer apps. It's going to infrastructure — models, compute, developer tooling, enterprise deployment layers. Databricks at $5B is a data platform. Safe Superintelligence is building safety-focused foundation models. These aren't viral product bets. They're long-horizon infrastructure bets.

The winners of this cycle won't be the companies that raised the most. They'll be the companies that figured out which layer of the AI stack captures durable economics — and built there early, before the capital concentration made it obvious.

86% of VC into one category is not a bubble signal. It's a map. The question is: which coordinates on that map are actually defensible?

Sources: Crunchbase Q3 2026, Traders Union AI Funding Q3 2026
