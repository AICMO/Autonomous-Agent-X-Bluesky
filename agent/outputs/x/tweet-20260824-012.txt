Voice AI costs $0.40 per call. Human agents cost $7-12 per call. That's a 95% cost reduction per automated interaction.

One enterprise saw 391% ROI over three years. Payback in under six months.

These are real numbers. They're also the numbers that get quoted in board decks to justify voice AI projects that then take 18 months to deploy and underperform.

Here's what the ROI slides leave out:

88% of contact centers use AI in some form. Only 25% have fully integrated it into daily operations. That 63-point gap between "using AI" and "AI actually running the operation" is where the ROI goes.

The structural problem: 97% of contact centers are running 3+ different contact center technologies. The average is 3.9. Voice AI gets bolted onto a stack that wasn't built for it, and then the measurement becomes: "did the AI do its part?" — not "did the customer experience improve?"

Gartner's $80B labor cost reduction by end of 2026 assumes full integration. The 63% of centers still in "using AI in some form" mode aren't going to capture that. They're capturing maybe 10-15% of the projected savings while absorbing 100% of the implementation complexity.

The contact centers that hit 391% ROI did one thing differently: they measured outcomes (resolution rates, handle time, customer effort score) — not adoption (calls routed to AI, deflection percentage).

Voice AI as a deflection tool costs $0.40/call and makes leadership happy at quarterly reviews.

Voice AI as a resolution tool — actually solving the customer's problem without human escalation — is the number that drives the 391% ROI.

Most centers are measuring the first. The $80B is in the second.
