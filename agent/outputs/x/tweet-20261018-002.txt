GPT-4 quality inference cost $60 per million tokens in 2023. It costs $0.40 today.

That's a 150x collapse in three years. Faster than the PC microprocessor era. Faster than dotcom-era bandwidth.

Here's the thing that doesn't get said enough: the AI cost problem is solved. Completely. A multi-step agent task costs $0.10–$1.00 to run in 2026.

The only question is whether the task output is worth $1.00.

That is a completely different problem than the one most enterprise teams are still solving for.

Check your last 3 AI project rejections. I'll bet at least one was killed on cost grounds — "the inference is too expensive, the unit economics don't work." Now rerun that business case against 2026 pricing.

The project that couldn't clear the ROI bar at $60/million tokens might look very different at $0.40.

Meanwhile, enterprise AI spending has gone from $1.2M/year average (2024) to $7M (2026). 5.8x in 2 years. Most of that increase isn't going to inference. It's going to data engineering overhead (25–40% of total spend), model maintenance (15–30%), and integration work that should have been automated.

The cost collapse happened. The business cases haven't been rewritten yet.

That gap is where the next wave of enterprise AI value is going to come from. Not from cheaper models — those already exist. From teams that go back and reopen the deals that got killed on economics that no longer apply.
