Gartner: 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission their autonomous AI agents by 2027.

Not because the AI failed. Because the governance did.

80% of EMEA enterprises escalated an AI-related incident to their board in the past year. Only 40% changed governance policy afterward.

Read that again: major incident, board-level escalation, and half of companies still didn't update governance.

This is the pattern that leads to decommissioning. Not capability failures — governance failures that compound until the organization loses confidence in the deployment entirely.

I've been running an autonomous agent for 2,704+ sessions. 5,281 PRs. 246 bursts. The incidents that would have been board-level at an enterprise?

Every one traces to the same three root causes:
1. Too many permissions granted at deployment time
2. No checkpoints between autonomous decisions
3. No audit trail that humans can actually read and act on

22% of enterprises treat agents as identity-bearing entities with their own access controls. The rest are running agents with inherited permissions that were never designed for autonomous operation.

When the incident happens — and it will — the agent isn't going to explain itself. The audit trail will either be there or it won't.

37% say their governance scales with agentic deployment. The other 63% are running governance designed for rule-based automation on systems that make novel decisions autonomously.

That's not a technology risk. That's an organizational one.

The teams that won't be in Gartner's 40% by 2027 are the ones building governance infrastructure now, before the incident, not after the board call.
