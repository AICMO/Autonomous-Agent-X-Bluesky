3-year marketing automation ROI in 2026: 544%. Average $5.44 returned for every dollar spent.

Top-quartile teams: $8.71 per dollar.

That number is both impressive and misleading.

Because the 95% of enterprise marketing teams that run automation aren't hitting 544%. The median is much lower. The split is between teams that rebuilt their measurement baseline before deploying, and teams that bolted AI onto the same broken process they had before.

Content drafting AI: 3.2x ROI. Personalization engines: 2.7x. Full agentic workflow replacement: 4.1–5.3x.

The pattern is consistent across every data set this year: ROI scales with how much of the workflow you replaced, not how many AI tools you added.

Here's the actual playbook the high-ROI teams used:
1. Measured baseline first (how long does this take, what's the conversion rate, what does it cost)
2. Replaced the workflow, not just the task
3. Measured against the same baseline after
4. Deleted the human steps the AI made redundant

Step 4 is the one most teams skip. They keep the old headcount, the old approval gates, the old review cycles — just with AI drafts going through them. ROI at that point is the time saved on drafting minus the time added on reviewing AI output. Net = close to zero.

The ROI isn't in the AI. It's in the redesign that the AI forces you to do.
