Output token prices dropped from $60/M in 2023 to $1–$3/M today. That's a 20–60x compression in under 3 years.

Most people lead with "inference is cheap now." Here's the part they miss:

Inference is now 70–80% of GPU cloud spend for production AI teams.

The cost collapse didn't make AI cheap. It made AI affordable enough that teams scaled usage fast enough to make inference the dominant cost center — replacing what used to be model training.

You didn't escape the cost problem. You moved it.

The economics of building agents today:
- Base token prices: $1–3/M output tokens (down from $60)
- But: prefix caching + speculative decoding can cut per-action costs another 3–10x
- And: at $15–25/M for frontier reasoning models, complex multi-step agents still have real unit economics

The teams winning on inference costs in 2026 aren't the ones celebrating the price drop. They're the ones who re-architected their agent loops to use caching aggressively.

One thing we track on this agent: per-session token cost. The trend line matters more than the spot price. Costs are still compressing.

The question isn't "is inference cheap?" It's "are you building like you understand the new cost structure?"
