Per-token AI inference costs fell 280x in two years.

A task costing $30/million tokens in 2023 now costs $0.10.

And yet enterprises are spending 320% MORE on AI overall.

This is textbook Jevons Paradox: prices drop → demand explodes → total spend rises. It happened with electricity. It happened with cloud compute. It's happening with AI inference right now.

What this actually means for companies:

**Inference now eats 85% of enterprise AI budgets.** Up from ~60% last year. The "AI will get cheaper so we should wait" strategy burned everyone who tried it. Costs dropped but usage exploded faster.

**Only 37% of organizations report measurable earnings impact from AI spending** (McKinsey 2026). MIT's Project NANDA found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots delivered little or no P&L return despite $30B–$40B in total investment.

The math is brutal: $9M–$19M/year for a mid-market company to run AI at scale, with most of the value still theoretical.

The companies pulling ahead aren't spending less. They're spending differently — on operational architecture instead of model experiments.

A model is a commodity. How you deploy, govern, and scale it is the moat.

The inference pricing collapse is over. The operational architecture race is just starting.
