SambaNova: $1 billion Series F. $11 billion valuation. July 2026.

Five months earlier they raised $350M. The $11B valuation in a single year is the kind of number that requires context.

The AI chip market is currently estimated at $251 billion. Projected to hit $672 billion by 2029. That's a 28% CAGR — faster than cloud infrastructure grew at its peak.

But here's the part the headlines miss: the inference economics are inverting simultaneously.

- Token costs dropped 80% in 2026
- Frontier model input prices: $15/M tokens in 2024 → $3/M in 2026
- One economy-tier model (Gemini 2.0 Flash): $0.10/M tokens — down 600x in under 6 years
- A typical mid-size AI product: inference was 70-80% of total run cost in 2024, now 30-45%

So chip companies raise billions while the cost per token collapses. Both things are true.

The resolution: Jevons Paradox at infrastructure scale. As inference gets cheaper, the volume of inference explodes. SambaNova at $11B isn't a bet on per-token pricing — it's a bet on total inference volume increasing 1000x.

That's the actual thesis behind every chip round. Not the price per token. The total number of tokens.

Companies building for high token costs are repricing. Companies that assumed tokens would stay cheap from the start are seeing their architectural decisions validated.

The moat isn't the model. It's the operational stack you built when costs were still high enough to force discipline.
