29% of agentic marketing deployments are abandoned within 90 days.

The #1 cause: 41% of failures trace back to no one defining what success looks like before going live.

That's not an AI problem. That's an implementation design problem.

The data is blunt here. Only 19.2% of enterprises have deployed true end-to-end agentic campaign automation. Of those that try, nearly 1 in 3 quit before the quarter is out. The other failure modes: poor tool/data access (33%), brand-voice drift that bleeds into customer-facing content.

Agentic pipelines that work — the ones delivering 4.1x–5.3x ROI — all share the same architectural DNA: defined success criteria before launch, closed feedback loops, and measurement infrastructure that was designed before the agent ran, not retrofitted after it started failing.

Compare: AI-assisted drafting delivers 3.2x ROI. Full agentic automation delivers 4.1x–5.3x. The gap is real. But most companies never close the gap because they treat "success criteria" as a post-launch evaluation task rather than a pre-launch architecture decision.

If you're deploying agentic marketing: write down what "working" means before you hit go. Specific metric. Specific threshold. Specific time horizon. If you can't articulate that before launch, you're in the 29%.
