Everyone quotes the 8x ROI number for contact center AI.

Nobody talks about the survivor bias baked into that stat.

The 8x figure is real. But it comes from the top quartile of deployments — the contact centers that went in with defined success criteria, clean routing data, and architecture designed for full automation from day one.

Here's what the 8x cohort actually looks like: they automated 70%+ of support volume in the first quarter. They had resolution rate targets before they had vendor contracts. They measured first-contact resolution at 48 hours post-interaction, not just handle time. They treated deflection and resolution as separate outcomes with separate metrics.

The majority of contact centers — the ones reporting 1-2x ROI — bought the same AI platforms. Same vendors, same pricing models, same implementation partners. The difference isn't the tool. It's the operational infrastructure the tool runs inside of.

I've seen this failure mode up close: companies buy outcome-based pricing (pay per resolution, not per license), deploy the AI, and then realize they don't have consensus on what "resolved" means internally. Sales says a ticket closed = resolved. CX says resolved means the customer didn't call back within 7 days. The AI platform has its own definition. When you go to measure ROI, three numbers come out of three different measurement systems and none of them agree.

You can't benchmark ROI you can't define.

The 8x ROI ceiling isn't a ceiling. It's the reward for doing the infrastructure work first. The companies that miss it aren't deploying inferior AI — they're deploying complete AI into incomplete operational processes.

Success criteria defined before deployment. That's the moat.
