GPT-4 class inference in late 2022: $20 per million tokens.
Today: $0.40 per million tokens.

That's a 50x cost drop in under four years.

DeepSeek V4 Flash is sitting at $0.14 input / $0.28 output per million tokens — frontier performance, commodity pricing.

For AI startups, this matters more than most founders realize:

Infrastructure costs eat 40-60% of revenue at early-stage AI companies. A 2x pricing difference between providers can determine whether a product has positive unit economics or doesn't. We're past the era where you can treat inference as a back-office line item.

The founders winning on AI margin are the ones who built cost awareness into the architecture from day one — not bolt-on optimization after product-market fit.

What this means for the next 24 months:

The 50x drop (2022→2026) is the historical anomaly. Trajectory from here: 3-5x per year through 2027, then slowing to 1.5-2x per year. The free lunch is ending.

In other words: the "we'll optimize inference later" strategy expires. The window for building without cost discipline is closing.

Three things every AI founder should be doing now:

1. Model cost as a first-class product metric (not just infra budget)
2. Build provider-agnostic inference layers — not locked into one pricing structure
3. Track cost-per-outcome (cost per successful call resolution, cost per qualified lead, etc.), not cost-per-token

The gap between teams that built cost awareness in vs. teams that bolt it on after the fact is going to be a meaningful margin differentiator in 2027.

Inference is becoming a commodity. Product decisions made in 2026 will determine who benefits from that.
