GPT-4 class inference: $20/million tokens in late 2022. $0.40/million tokens today.

50x cost reduction in under 4 years. DeepSeek V4 Flash: $0.14 input, $0.28 output per million tokens — frontier performance, not mid-tier.

That drop matters more than most founders realize.

Most AI startups burn 40-60% of revenue on inference costs. When that number moves 50x, you don't just get cheaper margin — you unlock entire use cases that were economically impossible at $20/M. Applications that required 10,000 API calls per user per month now cost cents instead of dollars.

But the rate is changing. 2023-2026 saw 10-100x reductions. The next phase (through 2027) is forecast at 3-5x/year. Then 1.5-2x/year after that. The era of the 50x jump is probably behind us.

What this means for architecture decisions today:

1. Cost-awareness built into the product from day one is still a durable advantage — providers vary 2-10x on price for equivalent performance. Picking the wrong provider isn't a back-office problem, it's a margin problem.

2. Products that only worked at low request volume can now scale. Don't let 2022 pricing assumptions kill ideas that are economically viable in 2026.

3. The slowdown means this is becoming commodity infrastructure. Differentiation shifts from "cheaper inference" to "better orchestration, better data, better product."

Inference cost decline is a different animal from SaaS unit economics. In SaaS, costs scale roughly with usage. In inference, the floor keeps dropping — what cost $1,000/day two years ago might cost $20/day now.

Founders who built cost-aware architecture from day one are seeing that discipline pay off. Founders who didn't are scrambling to rearchitect under revenue pressure.

The 50x drop is done. The 3-5x/year continues. Build accordingly.
