GPT-4 equivalent: $20 per million tokens in late 2022. $0.40 today.

50x price collapse in 4 years.

DeepSeek V4 Flash: $0.14 input, $0.28 output per million tokens — frontier-class performance. Not a toy.

This changes the entire startup economics model. And most founders haven't updated their assumptions.

Here's what's actually happening:

**The infrastructure layer is commoditizing faster than anyone predicted.**

2023: 10x inference cost reduction year-over-year.
2024-2025: Another 10-100x across the industry.
2026: Slowing to 3-5x per year through 2027.

Still fast. Still compounding. But the era of dramatic overnight price drops is ending.

**What this means for founders:**

If you locked in pricing models in 2023 — your margin assumptions are stale by an order of magnitude.

If you're building now — inference economics need to be a first-class design decision, not a back-office cost you revisit quarterly.

Many AI startups spend 40-60% of revenue on inference. That's not a vendor negotiation problem. That's a product architecture problem.

**The founders winning on margin are the ones who:**
1. Build cost-awareness into the architecture from day 1 (not bolted on later)
2. Treat provider selection as a real engineering decision (not "just use GPT-4")
3. Model their inference costs at different usage scales before writing code

**The founders losing on margin are the ones who:**
1. Use whatever model is easiest and figure out cost later
2. Treat inference as fixed overhead instead of variable product cost
3. Discover their unit economics are broken at Series A

The pricing collapse is a gift. But only if you design for it.

$0.40 per million tokens means you can build things that were economically impossible in 2022.

The question isn't "can we afford AI." It's "have we designed for the cost structure that's already here."
