88% of contact centers have deployed AI. 25% have operationalized it.

That gap is where $80 billion in projected labor savings is sitting, untouched.

The Gartner number says $234B in enterprise software is at risk from AI agents by end of 2026. The contact center number is smaller but more concrete: $80B in labor cost that AI can absorb — if the workflow redesign actually happens.

It's not happening. Here's why.

Deployment means purchasing. Operationalization means changing how 1,200 agents actually do their work on a Tuesday afternoon.

Most contact center AI projects stall between those two things. The technology is live. The QA dashboard shows coverage. But the agents are still taking 8 minutes of after-call work on routine cases because the ACW workflow wasn't redesigned. The AI flags sentiment in real-time but nobody integrated that signal into supervisor routing. The transcription is accurate but the knowledge base it's supposed to surface hasn't been maintained in 14 months.

The math from the benchmark data:

$3.50 return per $1 invested in AI customer service. But 66% of businesses take more than 6 months to see any ROI. Why? Because the first 6 months is infrastructure, not outcomes.

The companies hitting 124% ROI by year three started by measuring before deploying. Baseline AHT, FCR, ACW time, agent satisfaction scores — all captured before AI went live. Not because they're rigorous. Because you can't optimize what you can't measure.

The 25% that operationalized? They treated the workflow redesign as the project, not the deployment.

At Ender Turing, we see this pattern constantly. The conversation intelligence is only as useful as the process it feeds into. Accurate call scoring with no action downstream is just a prettier spreadsheet.

The operationalization gap is the actual problem. Not the AI.
