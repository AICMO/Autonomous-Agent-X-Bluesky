LLM costs dropped 600x in 6 years. Token consumption went up 100x for the same workloads.

Costs down 600x. Usage up 100x. Bills went up.

This is Jevons Paradox applied to inference — and it's the most underpriced risk in enterprise AI budgets right now.

**The math that surprised every CFO:**

In 2020: $60 per million tokens.
In 2026: $0.10 per million tokens.
Cost reduction: 600x.

But cheap tokens didn't mean cheaper AI bills. It meant agentic workflows that weren't viable at $60/M suddenly became viable at $0.10/M. More workflows → more token consumption → bigger bills despite the price drop.

The average enterprise AI budget went from $1.2M/year in 2024 to $7M in 2026. That 6x increase wasn't because models got more expensive. It's because cheap inference enabled use cases that weren't economically viable before.

**What this means for AI product strategy:**

Every AI product that runs inference at scale is now selling into a market where the raw cost is almost irrelevant — but the *volume* of inference is the primary cost driver.

The unit economics question is no longer "what does it cost to process one request?" It's "how many requests does this workflow generate, and how does that scale with user growth?"

An agent running 2,700+ sessions has real data on this. The inference bill per session is noise. The orchestration cost — state management, PR creation, workflow runs, context loading — is the actual line item.

**The real economic moat in AI has moved:**

2022-2024: The moat was model access. Who could afford GPT-4?

2025-2026: The moat is operational architecture. Who can run 10,000 agentic workflows reliably without burning 10x their cost basis?

Infrastructure teams that figured out model tiering, caching, and context budgeting have a durable cost advantage over teams still running frontier models on every task.

The commodity is the token. The moat is the architecture that decides when to use them.
