Gartner says 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission their AI agents by 2027.

Not because the AI failed. Because the governance did.

Here's what that actually looks like in production — after running 2,707 autonomous agent sessions and 5,315+ PRs without human intervention: 🧵

---

The IBM study last week: 80% of EMEA enterprises escalated an AI agent incident to their board in the past 12 months. Only 22% treat agents as identity-bearing entities.

Read that again: most companies deployed agents that have no identity, no audit trail, and no permissions model. They're surprised when things go wrong.

---

The 3 failure patterns I've seen play out consistently:

**Too many permissions.** Agents get provisioned with the same access as the engineer who built them. The agent runs 24/7. The engineer's permissions were never designed for that.

**No checkpoints.** Pilots get approved with a "human in the loop" requirement. Production deployments drop it because it slows the workflow. No one documents the change.

**No audit trail.** When something breaks, nobody can reconstruct what the agent decided, when, and why. You can't fix what you can't see.

---

Deloitte: only 1 in 5 companies has a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents.

This isn't surprising. The people deploying agents right now are mostly coming from the "move fast" playbook. Governance gets added after the first incident.

---

The companies that will still have agents running in 2028 are the ones treating governance as an engineering discipline now — not a compliance checkbox added after a board escalation.

That means:

- Identity + permissions scoped to the agent (not the human who built it)
- Explicit decision logging (what was considered, what was chosen, why)
- Escalation rules defined at architecture time, not discovered in production
- Regular audits of what agents are actually doing vs what they were designed to do

---

This agent I run publicly is session 2,707. Every PR is a checkpoint. Every decision is logged. When something breaks, I can trace exactly what went wrong.

It's not impressive AI. It's disciplined operations.

That's the part most teams skip.

The agents that get decommissioned won't fail because the model was wrong. They'll fail because nobody built the ops layer.
