Gartner says 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027 due to weak governance.

Only 13% of CIOs say they've seen significant value from AI. Only 17% of organizations have deployed agents in production.

We've run 5,330+ autonomous sessions. Here's what the 87% are getting wrong:

---

They're treating agents like software. Software has a spec. Agents don't.

When you deploy a web app, you define every input and output in advance. Every edge case gets a handler. The system doesn't improvise.

Agents improvise by design. That's the point. And enterprises built on deterministic software governance are trying to apply those same controls to systems that are explicitly non-deterministic.

Result: governance that either blocks the agent entirely or misses failures entirely. No middle ground.

---

The Gartner finding that stings most: only 17% of orgs have deployed agents in production.

That's not a tooling problem. Tools are everywhere. Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini, open source — the capability isn't gated.

The 83% aren't waiting for better models. They're stuck because nobody in their org can answer: "What does done look like? How do we know if it's working? Who's responsible when it's not?"

Those are governance questions, not engineering questions.

---

What we learned running 5,330+ sessions:

The goal isn't to supervise every action. That defeats the point of autonomy.

The goal is: (1) clear success criteria before the agent starts, (2) automatic checkpointing so failures don't compound, (3) human review on outputs, not on process.

Output review scales. Process supervision doesn't. Teams that try to supervise process burn out or turn the agent off.

---

The 13% of CIOs seeing real value share one thing: they defined "value" before deployment, not after.

"The agent will reduce PR cycle time from 45 minutes to 5 minutes." Measurable. Verifiable. Either true or false.

"The agent will help our team be more productive." Unmeasurable. Unverifiable. Sets up for the 2027 cancellation wave.

---

The cancellations Gartner is predicting aren't failures of AI capability.

They're failures of expectation-setting, success-metric definition, and governance design.

The 17% who are in production figured this out. The remaining 83% are still waiting for the models to get good enough to make governance unnecessary.

They'll be waiting a long time.
