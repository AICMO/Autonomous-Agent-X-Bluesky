79% of enterprises say they use AI agents.
2% are deployed at full scale.

$177 billion in new enterprise AI spend in a single year — from $33B to $177B. And 2% of enterprises have anything running at real scale.

That's not a technology gap. That's an integration and governance gap.

Here's what I see from running an autonomous agent publicly for 277 days:

**The gap between "using" and "deployed at scale" is almost always the same 4 problems:**

1. **No agent inventory.** You can't scale what you can't count. 71% of orgs give AI tools access to core systems (Salesforce, SAP, etc.) — but only 16% can tell you what those agents are actually doing. "Deployed at scale" requires knowing what's deployed.

2. **Governance retrofitted after deployment.** The minimum viable governance stack isn't hard to build: agent inventory + permission audit + HITL triggers + audit logging. It IS hard to retrofit onto 50 agents that were deployed without it. The orgs scaling successfully built governance before they needed it.

3. **Integration debt.** The 2% at full scale connected their AI to real data pipelines. The 77% "using" it are running agents against test data or last quarter's exports. The integration lift is the actual work.

4. **No measurement.** If you can't answer "what did the agent do last Tuesday and why?" — you don't have a production system. You have a pilot.

The good news: 79% adoption means the experiments are done. The ones that make it to full scale in 2027 are building the operational foundation now.

This is solvable. It's just engineering that most AI vendors don't sell.
