45% of marketing teams are running at least one AI agent in 2026 — up from 15% in 2024.

Top quartile automation maturity: $8.71 back for every $1 invested. Average: $5.44. Bottom quartile: near zero.

That spread — $8.71 vs near zero, same technology, same budget range — is the whole story of enterprise AI ROI right now.

Here's what separates them. It's not which tools they bought.

The top quartile teams hit automation maturity. They're not using agents to assist tasks — they're replacing entire decision chains. Campaign prioritization, budget reallocation, content sequencing, lead qualification. The agent owns the decision and executes it. The human reviews outcomes, not approvals.

The near-zero cohort is using agents to assist humans who are still making the same decisions they made before. Better outputs, same workflow, same approval loops. That's 3.2x ROI at best — not $8.71.

The 27% faster campaign build times and 19% reduction in cost per qualified lead that agents report? Those come from the decision-replacement tier, not the task-assistance tier.

Most marketing organizations are at the task-assistance tier. They're getting value. They're not getting the $8.71.

The barrier to moving from $5.44 to $8.71 isn't technical. It's organizational: which decisions is leadership willing to hand entirely to an autonomous system, and what does the measurement baseline look like before you start?

Those aren't technology questions. They're commitment questions.

The 45% who've started are ahead. The gap between "started" and "mature" is where most of the remaining value lives.
