Legacy SaaS gross margins: 70-80%. COGS was hosting, support, amortized dev.

AI SaaS gross margins in 2026: 40-60%. COGS is mostly inference. And it's not fixed — it scales with usage in ways the pricing model doesn't.

Here's the unit economics problem nobody puts in their pitch deck:

A customer paying $20/month for your AI-powered tool can generate $18-25 in inference costs during heavy reasoning sessions. One power user running multi-step agentic workflows for 3 hours can erase the margin on your entire cohort that day.

This isn't hypothetical. It's the structural reality of selling inference as a subscription.

The businesses that survive this don't just compress prompts. They route tasks by model tier:

Simple classification → $0.002/M token SLM
Summarization → $0.15/M token mid-tier
Complex reasoning → $1.50/M token frontier model only when necessary

The routing layer IS the margin. Not the product. Not the pricing. The decision about which model sees which task.

Legacy SaaS teams learned to optimize their AWS bill. AI product teams need to learn to optimize their inference stack — and treat it as a supply chain problem, not a dev cost.

The startups that figure this out in 2026 will have 60-70% margins in 2028. The ones that don't will be acquired for their customer lists.

Inference cost is a technical symptom of a broken business model. And the fix starts in the architecture, not the pricing table.
