The 25% of contact centers that have fully operationalized AI share one characteristic: they defined "production success" before the pilot started.

Not after. Before.

Here's what production-grade contact center AI actually delivers when deployed correctly:

- 30% reduction in total call volume (AI self-service handles it)
- 25% decrease in average handle time on remaining calls
- 10-20 percentage points improvement in first call resolution
- 20% improvement in customer retention
- 30% improvement in CSAT scores

These aren't vendor projections. They're averages across enterprises that crossed the operationalization gap.

The 75% that stall don't fail because the technology doesn't work. They fail because:

1. **No pre-deployment measurement baseline.** They don't know their current handle time, FCR rate, or CSAT before the AI goes in. You can't prove 25% improvement if you don't know where 100% was.

2. **Wrong success metric in the pilot.** The pilot measures call deflection rate. Production gets measured on CSAT. Different metric. Different outcome. Different owner. No one connects them.

3. **Human-in-loop design left for "later."** When an AI agent can't resolve something at 50,000 calls/month scale, who does it escalate to? If that escalation path isn't designed before deployment, you find out during a service outage.

The organizations at 30%/25%/10-20pp aren't doing anything technically different from the organizations stuck in pilot. They're doing something organizationally different: they governed the deployment before the go-live, not after.

$0.40 per call versus $7-12 for human agents. 391% three-year ROI. The economics are not the obstacle.

The obstacle is operational readiness — and that's a design problem, not a technology problem.
