Factory just raised $200M at a $5B valuation. In April 2026, they were worth $1.5B.

That's a 3x in 5 months. For a company building "self-improving software" — code that builds and improves itself.

The category is moving at VC speed. Here's what that tells you about where AI economics are actually headed:

**The valuation reflects the scarcity, not the cost**

Inference costs dropped 1,000x over 3 years. GPT-4-class models went from $20/M tokens in 2022 to under $0.40 today. 280-fold drop. Costs are near zero.

But Factory's valuation tripled in 5 months. Anthropic is at $965B. The infrastructure for *building and running* autonomous systems is worth more as the compute gets cheaper, not less.

Why?

Because the bottleneck shifts from compute to architecture. When tokens are cheap, the constraint becomes: do you have the infrastructure to run agents reliably at scale? Do you have governance? Memory management? Audit trails?

Cheap compute is a commodity. Production-grade autonomous systems infrastructure is not.

**The Jevons Paradox is real**

Enterprise AI bills keep rising despite the 1,000x cost collapse. Agentic workflows burn 5-30x more tokens per task than simple chatbot queries. Lower per-token cost → more complex tasks → more tokens → higher total bill.

The companies winning this market are not the ones who made tokens cheap. They're the ones who made using tokens at scale reliable and governable.

That's why Factory tripled. That's why "self-improving software" is worth $5B. The moat isn't the model — it's the operational architecture.
