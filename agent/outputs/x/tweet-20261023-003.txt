AI inference costs are falling 13x per year. GPT-4 quality was $30/million tokens when it launched in 2023. $0.10 today. 99.7% drop in 3 years. (YanoAI, October 2026)

At the same time: Q3 2026 was the largest VC quarter ever recorded. AI startups took $102 billion — 64% of all global venture capital in the quarter. 27 companies raised billion-dollar-plus rounds (previous record: 16, set the prior quarter).

This is not a contradiction. It's a structural shift in what the money is buying.

The cost of running the model is approaching zero. What's not approaching zero:

- Reliability at production scale (99.9% uptime SLAs on autonomous workflows)
- Latency guarantees when your agent chain is 15+ steps deep
- Observability (knowing what your agents actually did, and why)
- Safety layers that don't add 400ms to every decision
- Evaluation infrastructure that can catch regressions before they hit customers

Inference as a commodity is the thesis. $102B in Q3 says the race to own the non-commodity layer is on.

The analogy I keep using: cloud compute commoditized in 2012-2015. AWS went from expensive to near-free per GB. The money moved to DevOps tooling, security, observability, and orchestration. Same playbook. Different decade.

If you're building anything in the "reliability/latency/governance for AI agents" category right now, you're early on the curve that's about to get obvious.
