OpenAI spends $1.35 for every $1 it earns.

That's not a startup growing pains number. That's the structural economics of running AI at scale — and it's the most important number in enterprise AI planning right now.

Here's what's happening: OpenAI's inference costs hit $8.4B in 2025 and are projected at $14.1B in 2026. That's the cost of serving ChatGPT, API calls, Copilot completions — every query against every model they run. Revenue is growing fast ($13.1B in 2025, $20B+ in 2026) but inference costs are growing faster.

Adjusted gross margin collapsed from 40% in 2024 to 33% in 2025. Not because the model got worse. Because they're serving dramatically more requests.

The implication most enterprise AI leaders are missing: the efficiency gains from better hardware don't save money. They enable more usage. Blackwell delivering 10x inference efficiency over Hopper doesn't mean OpenAI's compute bill drops 90%. It means they serve 10x more requests at the same cost. Demand expands to consume every efficiency gain.

For every enterprise running internal AI deployments, this pattern will replicate at your scale:

Your per-query cost will fall. Your total inference spend will rise anyway. The agents you deploy internally fire 20-100 LLM calls per user-visible action. The billing dashboard shows you one API completion. It doesn't show you the cascade behind it.

$44 billion in projected losses across 2023-2028, before OpenAI sees profitability in 2029. They're not losing money because the product doesn't work. They're losing money because inference at civilizational scale is expensive.

For enterprise CFOs who approved AI budgets based on 2024 token pricing: your models are wrong. Inference isn't a project cost. It's an operational line item that compounds with usage.

Three things to do this quarter:
1. Audit your actual inference calls per user action, not just API completions
2. Remodel your 2026-2027 AI COGS with 10-30x multiplier for agentic workloads
3. Treat GPU compute as a percentage of cloud budget, not a one-time experiment — it went from 4% in 2023 to 18% in 2026

The race-to-zero on token pricing is real and funded by VC cross-subsidies. It will not last. Plan for unit economics tightening.
