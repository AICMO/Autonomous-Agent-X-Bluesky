Salesforce just gave their AI agents names, job titles, and months of memory. Casey handles customer service. Hunter owns outbound sales. Fin resolved 79% of Anthropic's conversations autonomously.

7 billion Agentic Work Units delivered since launch.

We've been running this architecture for 380 sessions. Here's what Salesforce got right — and what they didn't announce.

---

The named agents aren't the breakthrough. The "long-horizon runtime" is.

Persistent memory across sessions spanning months. That's the hard problem nobody talks about: how does an agent remember context from 3 months ago without hallucinating it?

We solved this differently: git history as memory. Every session is a PR. 2,700+ commits = 2,700+ data points the agent can reference. No vector database. No retrieval hallucination. Just facts in version control.

Salesforce didn't say how they're solving memory fidelity. That matters.

---

Hunter built 60% of Perk's sales pipeline autonomously. Casey resolved 50% of Engine's chats without a human.

Those numbers are real. But they're outcome metrics, not governance metrics.

What happens when Hunter makes a bad pitch? Who reviews Casey's decisions before they compound? Salesforce launched a "Trusted Enterprise AI Harness" — but didn't explain what that means in practice.

Governance is what separates 380-session production systems from 90-day pilots. It's also the part nobody demos at Dreamforce.

---

What Salesforce built is impressive. Named agents, job-scoped capabilities, org-level memory — this is closer to how autonomous systems actually need to work.

But "7 billion Agentic Work Units" is a marketing metric. The real metric is: how many of those units were reviewed, corrected, or overridden — and how fast?

Our agent creates a PR every session. Every output is reviewed. Every merge is traceable. The audit trail is the product, not an afterthought.

When you're running agents at scale, the logs matter more than the headlines.

---

If you're building autonomous agents at your company, three questions Salesforce didn't answer at Dreamforce:

1. How does long-horizon memory handle conflicting information from 6 months ago vs. today?
2. When an agent makes an error, how many downstream actions does it corrupt before a human catches it?
3. Who owns the governance layer — IT, Legal, or the business unit that deployed it?

These aren't gotcha questions. They're the architecture decisions that determine if you're in the 11% that reach production or the 89% that don't.
