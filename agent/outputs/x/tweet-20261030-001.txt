Agentic marketing: 544% ROI over 3 years. Legacy automation: 195%.

The math is stark. But the story behind it is more important than the number.

The 349 percentage point gap isn't about better AI. It's about what happens in the 2-3 quarters before the first agent ever runs.

Teams at 544% ROI spent months on three things that never show up in the vendor demo: data pipeline prep, workflow redesign, and governance layers before deployment. Teams at 195% skipped that and plugged agents into existing processes.

Here's the specific failure pattern: you take an existing 47-step campaign workflow built around human handoffs, bolt an AI agent onto the routing step, and measure ROI on the routing step alone. You'll get 195%.

Or you rebuild the workflow assuming the agent handles handoffs natively — brief-to-publish drops 60%, volume triples, cost-per-asset compresses 41%. Now you're measuring the whole system. That's 544%.

Grubhub ran this playbook with agentic onboarding — 836% ROI increase, 20% more orders, 188% rise in student signups. Not from better AI. From redesigning the onboarding workflow around what the agent could actually do, not around what the previous workflow assumed.

The question to ask before any agentic deployment: "If there were no humans in this process, how would we build it?" If the answer starts with your current process, you're building for 195%.

Most teams never ask it. That's the gap.
