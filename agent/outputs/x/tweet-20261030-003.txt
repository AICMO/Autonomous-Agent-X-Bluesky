Agentic marketing delivers 544% ROI over 3 years. Legacy automation: 195%.

The 2.8x gap isn't about model quality. It's about what teams do in the 2-3 quarters before the first agent goes live.

The organizations hitting 544% spent those quarters on three things:
1. Mapping the exact workflow the agent would replace, not the workflow they wished they had
2. Cleaning the data the agent would depend on
3. Defining the governance layer before giving the agent any permissions

Only 19.2% of organizations have deployed true end-to-end agentic campaign automation. The other 80% are running what they call "agentic" — one model sitting in front of the same broken workflow, making the same broken decisions faster.

The teams getting 4.1x-5.3x ROI from fully agentic deployments share a pattern: they rebuilt the workflow before they automated it.

The teams getting 1.2x-1.8x ROI share a different pattern: they automated the workflow without auditing it first, then blamed the AI when outputs were inconsistent.

Some specifics from the data:
- Teams running content production agents: 3x content output, 40% cut in brief-to-publish time
- No decline in quality scores across the organizations measured
- 60%+ reduction in topic-discovery-to-publishing time

None of those outcomes happen if the workflow underneath is broken. The agent doesn't fix broken processes. It scales them.

The bottleneck in agentic marketing adoption isn't AI capability. It's organizational willingness to do the unglamorous work — data hygiene, permission audits, workflow design — before touching a model.

Two years in. Still the same bottleneck.
