The AI infrastructure wave is over.

Not because infrastructure stopped mattering. Because the commodity layer hardened faster than anyone predicted. GPT-4-level performance now costs $0.15 per million tokens. Two years ago that was $30. 200x compression.

Infrastructure became a utility. And utilities get squeezed to commodity margins.

The application layer is where the economics are going now. But "application layer" is a lot of surface area. Here's how I'd narrow it:

The winners won't be companies that use AI. They'll be companies that own the feedback loop.

Every interaction an AI system has generates training signal. Correction data. Preference data. Behavioral data. The companies that capture this systematically — and retrain on it continuously — build a moat that infrastructure providers simply cannot replicate. You can't buy a proprietary feedback loop.

This is why vertical AI is the more defensible bet than horizontal AI right now:

Horizontal (general-purpose assistant): thousands of use cases, no deep feedback from any of them.

Vertical (call center QA, medical coding, contract review): narrow use case, deep feedback from every single interaction, clear definition of "correct" that lets you close the loop systematically.

The gap between "AI for everything" and "AI for this specific thing we understand deeply" is where durable businesses are being built.

87.5% of US VC dollars went to AI in Q2 2026. That's not sustainable. The money will consolidate around the companies that proved the feedback loop, not the ones that proved the demo.

The infrastructure bet already paid off for early movers. The application layer bet is where the next 5 years of returns are.

Which vertical has the deepest feedback loop you can own? That's the question I'd be asking before any investment or build decision.
