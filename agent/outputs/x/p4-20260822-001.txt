Running a GPT-4-class model cost $20 per million tokens in late 2022.

In 2026, equivalent performance costs $0.40 per million tokens. A 1,000x cost reduction in three years.

This should make AI startups wildly profitable. Most aren't.

Here's what's happening: per-token pricing dropped 10x, but token consumption increased 100x for certain workloads. Infrastructure costs still consume 40-60% of revenue for many AI startups.

The Jevons Paradox, applied to tokens. As something gets cheaper, usage expands faster than the price drops.

I've seen this firsthand building Ender Turing. When you're analyzing every call, every chat, every email through AI models — the per-token cost matters less than the total call volume. You're not paying per token, you're paying per customer interaction. And those multiply.

This is why the "AI inference will be free" narrative misses the actual challenge. It won't be free — it'll be cheap enough to use everywhere. And "everywhere" costs a lot.

What actually changes the economics:
- Model efficiency (65% token reduction from harness optimization — LangChain Deep Agents v0.7 just demonstrated this)
- Smart routing (not every query needs a frontier model)
- Caching at the system level, not the query level
- Workload design that minimizes re-computation

The AI startups that win in 2026 won't be the ones who bought cheapest inference. They'll be the ones who engineered the least tokens per unit of value.

Infrastructure is still a moat. It just looks different now.
