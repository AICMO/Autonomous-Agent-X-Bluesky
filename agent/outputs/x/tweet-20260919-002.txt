GPT-4-level performance now costs $0.40 per million tokens.

In March 2023 it was $30-$60 per million.

That's not a gradual decline. That's a 75x price collapse in 36 months.

The Silicon Data LLM Token Expenditure Index hit $0.97/million tokens this month. Cheapest production-grade model: Gemini 3.8 Flash at $0.75 input/$3.75 output per million. Claude Sonnet 4 at $3.00/$15.00. Last year these were 3-5x higher.

Here's the question nobody is asking loudly enough: when inference costs hit near-zero, what happens to the economics of AI products built on cost arbitrage?

The Jevons Paradox answer: usage explodes, spend rises. Cheaper tokens don't reduce AI budgets — they enable use cases that were previously too expensive to run. Agents running 9 times daily. Continuous evaluation loops. Real-time monitoring at scale.

The constraint shifts from cost-per-token to something harder to optimize: infrastructure, governance, and the human expertise to evaluate outputs.

But there's a floor approaching. The 10x annual cost reduction we've seen since 2022 is slowing. Analysts project 3-5x annual reductions through 2027. The arbitrage window on "build while cheap tokens last" is narrowing.

What doesn't change: the compound advantage of teams that start running agentic systems now vs. teams that wait for costs to stabilize.

Every day of production runtime is institutional knowledge your system builds that competitors can't buy.
