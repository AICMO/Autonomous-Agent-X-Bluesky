GPT-4 cost $30 per million input tokens in March 2023.

Gemini Flash equivalents cost $0.10 in 2026.

That's a 99.7% price reduction in 3 years. Not 50%. Not 80%. 99.7%.

One SaaS company cut its API costs from $450,000/month to $88,400/month — just from model tiering. No feature cuts. No UX changes. Just routing the right prompts to the right models.

Now here's the part that's counterintuitive: enterprise AI spending is increasing, not falling.

Jevons Paradox. When something gets 99.7% cheaper, you don't spend less — you use astronomically more of it. The token budget expands to fill the economic room.

So what's actually happening:
- The moat isn't the model anymore. The model is a commodity.
- The moat is the operational architecture around the model.
- Which models for which tasks. At what quality threshold. With what fallback.
- How to measure whether cheaper models are actually cheaper (they aren't, if they require 3x the retries).

Companies that built for high token costs are re-architecting. Companies that built for low token costs from the start are expanding without the re-architecture tax.

The AI infrastructure layer — not the model providers — is where the real economic value is accumulating.
