Every enterprise CFO who built AI workflow budgets on current API pricing made a spreadsheet error.

In 2025, OpenAI generated approximately $3.7B in revenue and lost an estimated $5B. That's a cost-to-revenue ratio of about 1.35x — meaning for every dollar OpenAI earned, they spent $1.35.

The API rates your teams are budgeting around are below cost. This isn't speculation. It's visible in investor disclosures and market analysis.

The pricing structure that makes your AI-powered workflows look profitable today is subsidized. The VCs funding these losses are betting on future revenue concentration or cost curves that haven't materialized yet.

Two dynamics make this worse:

First, context window inflation. Frontier models now support 1M+ token contexts. But KV-cache memory costs scale quadratically with context length at inference time. The per-token price drops you've been seeing don't account for how actual enterprise usage patterns push into the expensive part of that curve.

Second, the assumption that inference costs always fall is wrong. Training costs have dropped dramatically. Inference costs have fallen, but more slowly — and usage patterns are growing faster than efficiency gains.

The companies that will survive the normalization of AI pricing are the ones who designed for 3-5x current inference spend, not the ones who assumed today's rates are the floor.

If your AI business case assumed stable API pricing, model it again at 3x. If the ROI holds, proceed. If it doesn't, you've found the real risk.
