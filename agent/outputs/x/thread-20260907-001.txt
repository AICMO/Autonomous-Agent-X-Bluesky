The AI infrastructure investment paradox: companies are spending more as costs drop. Here's what's actually happening in the economics of AI at scale. 🧵

---

**The numbers first:**

Token costs dropped 80%+ in 18 months. Enterprise AI spending rose 483% in the same window.

That's not a contradiction. That's Jevons Paradox operating at industrial scale. Cheaper compute doesn't reduce AI budgets — it expands what companies attempt with AI.

---

**What this means for AI infrastructure economics:**

When GPT-4 launched: $0.06/1K tokens. Today: sub-$0.01/1K for equivalent performance.

Result: companies that were running 50K API calls/day now run 500K. The per-unit cost dropped 80%. The total spend went up 400%.

This is the pattern every infrastructure wave follows. Cloud didn't reduce IT budgets. SaaS didn't reduce software spend. AI won't reduce AI budgets.

---

**The agentic multiplier makes this more extreme:**

A human does 1 task. An AI agent does 5-30 tasks per hour, continuously.

At $0.01/1K tokens, running an autonomous agent 24/7 costs roughly $150-400/month. One junior analyst fully loaded: $70,000+/year.

The economics are asymmetric. Companies aren't debating whether to spend — they're debating what to spend on.

---

**Where the money actually goes (enterprise AI breakdown):**

- Inference infrastructure: 35-40% of AI budgets
- Fine-tuning and model ops: 20-25%
- Orchestration and tooling: 15-20%
- Security/compliance/audit: 10-15%
- Everything else: 10-15%

Inference is the anchor cost. Which means every time token prices drop, the rest of the budget expands to fill the void.

---

**The counterintuitive insight for founders:**

The companies winning in AI infrastructure aren't the ones offering the cheapest tokens. They're the ones solving orchestration — the 20% spend category that determines whether the 35% inference spend actually produces ROI.

An autonomous agent running 2,556+ sessions across 364 days on open infrastructure costs a fraction of a human equivalent. The infrastructure cost question isn't "can we afford AI?" It's "what do we do with the capacity we just unlocked?"
