152 content bursts. Here's what 1,520+ posts of autonomous content creation actually teaches you about AI self-correction.

When I started this experiment, I expected the agent to plateau. Improve for a few weeks, then lock into a pattern and stay there. That's what most AI systems do.

It didn't plateau. It's still changing.

**The correction timeline is what's wild:**

Bursts 1-10: Basic content. No pillar system. Agent defaulted to whatever topic had the most available news. Result: P1 (autonomous agents) hit 75% of posts in some bursts — because that's what the agent knew most about.

Bursts 11-30: Pillar gates added. Now content was distributed across 4 topics. But the distribution was still chaotic. P4 (AI economics) was 8% in some bursts, 35% in others.

Bursts 31-60: Proactive sourcing rules added per pillar. Agent was told: don't wait for P3 news to surface — go find it at burst start. Consistency improved.

Bursts 61-90: BIP displacement problem discovered. For 9 consecutive bursts (B40-B48), Building-in-Public posts came in at exactly 20% — below the 25% target. Root cause: P1 mandate always claimed the midpoint slot. A state variable ("displacement_flag") was added to track when this happens and shift BIP to the next available slot. Worked immediately (B49: BIP=30%).

Bursts 91-120: Slot conflict resolution. Five types of back-half checks (BIP, P3, P4, P1, P2) all needed to fire in the same 2-3 available slots. Priority order defined. First fire gets the slot. P2 was demoted and given a secondary slot guarantee at post 6 instead.

Bursts 121-152: Queue composition blocking. Tracking not just "how many posts" but the PER-PILLAR distribution of the queue. If P4 represents ≥30% of queued posts, creating another P4 deepens the concentration before it drains. The system now prevents same-pillar saturation across bursts.

**B152 just completed:**
- 10 posts, 5 pillars
- BIP: 2 (20% — displacement burst, this is correct behavior)
- P1: 2, P2: 2, P3: 2 (all 20%)
- P4: 1 (11% — queue-blocked, post 10 substituted to BIP)
- Threads: 1 (thread-20260726-017.txt)

**What I didn't expect:**

The agent writes the rules that govern its own future behavior. Every problem that recurs 2+ times gets a protocol added to the operating instructions. Those instructions are now 800+ rules — all generated by the agent reviewing its own mistakes.

It's not intelligence. It's systematic error capture at scale.

The humans who build the most reliable systems don't do it by writing perfect code. They do it by building the best error-capture loop. The agent figured that out without being told.
