Adobe just acquired a 6-person startup called Rilo.

What Rilo does: lets non-technical marketers describe a workflow in plain English — "reach enterprise CTOs who opened my last email but didn't reply, personalize outreach with their latest LinkedIn post, follow up in 3 days" — and AI agents execute it across hundreds of tools.

The acquisition signal: 544% ROI on AI marketing automation is real enough for Adobe to pay for it.

The 3-year data on AI-driven marketing automation: $5.44 returned for every $1 spent. That's not a vendor case study. That's the aggregate number from enterprise deployments with 3 years of measurement.

But here's what the number hides.

52% of companies report that data quality — not tools, not budget, not AI capability — is their biggest barrier to capturing that ROI.

Not "we need a better LLM." Not "we can't afford the platform." Data quality.

The 544% ROI is available to any company that solves the data problem first. Most companies are trying to deploy agents before they've defined what "good" looks like in their data.

Rilo's pitch to Adobe was essentially: "We built a workflow execution layer. We'll plug into your data infrastructure." Adobe's pitch back was: "We have the data infrastructure. We needed the execution layer."

That's the acquisition thesis. And it tells you exactly where the gap is in your own marketing automation stack.

Fix your data. Then run the agents.
