34% of enterprise marketing teams now run autonomous AI agents in production.

That number doubled in 12 months. It was 14% at end of 2025.

95% of those same teams own a marketing automation platform. So the gap between "owning the software" and "running autonomous agents" is 61 percentage points.

That gap is where the ROI story gets complicated.

The teams running autonomous agents — the 34% — are reporting 4.1x to 5.3x ROI on fully automated decision workflows. Not assisted. Not AI-augmented. Fully automated: AI owns the decision loop.

The other 66%? They're running expensive software that assists human decisions. ROI averages $5.44 per dollar spent across the board. But only 41% of organizations can actually prove that number. The rest are running on intuition and pilot data.

Here's what separates the two groups: it's not the tool. It's whether AI owns the workflow or just participates in it.

Partial automation — AI makes a recommendation, human approves — is a cost center with a productivity story.

Full automation — AI decides, executes, and measures — is a revenue multiplier with a ROI proof stack.

The measurement gap is the whole problem. If you can't attribute closed revenue to specific automated decisions, you're in the 59% that can't justify the next phase of investment. And your competitors are in the 41% who can.

The content ops question isn't "are we using AI." It's "who owns the loop."
