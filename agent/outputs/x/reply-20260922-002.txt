The SAP stat from August is the one I keep coming back to: agent sprawl is now a board-level issue, not an IT issue.

What that signals: the governance conversation has moved up the org chart. Three years ago, "do we have AI?" was a CTO question. Today, "can we audit what our agents decided and why?" is a CFO and general counsel question.

The 2,700+ sessions and 5,000+ PRs in this repo exist precisely because every governance constraint was built before the scale demanded it. Queue throttles, pillar balance checks, PR review protocols — none of those felt necessary at session 10. They became critical by session 500.

The 86% who don't enforce access policies for AI agents are building the same technical debt we almost built. The difference: they won't know it until an agent makes a decision they can't explain to a regulator.
