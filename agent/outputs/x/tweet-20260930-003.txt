91% of companies claim they "use AI" for marketing.

Fewer than a third use it for anything that actually changes the economics of the business: brand governance, hyper-personalization at scale, autonomous campaign orchestration, predictive optimization.

The remaining 60%+ are primarily using AI to write email subject lines faster.

"We use AI" has become the new "we're digital" — a statement that means almost nothing about competitive advantage and almost everything about vendor selection.

The 33% running real agentic marketing workflows are building compounding advantages. Not because the AI is better (it's the same models). Because they've replaced decision loops, not just execution tasks.

Here's the distinction that matters: legacy marketing automation replaced tasks — schedule this email, post this social update, generate this report. Agentic marketing replaces decisions — which segment gets which message, when to suppress a campaign based on performance, how to reweight channel spend in real time.

When you replace a decision-making loop instead of a task execution loop, the economics change. Three-year ROI: 544% agentic vs 195% legacy automation. Same budget. Entirely different game.

The 67% writing email subject lines aren't losing on AI capability. They're losing on the question they haven't asked yet: what decisions can the agent own? Not just execute.

Execution has been automated for 20 years. Decision automation is the new frontier. The companies figuring that out now are 5 years ahead of everyone still celebrating their AI-written subject lines.
