Token prices dropped 280x since 2022. Enterprise AI bills went up 320% in the same period.

That's not a typo. That's Jevons Paradox at scale.

OpenAI cut GPT-5 prices 80% on July 30. The biggest price move since the GPT-5 launch. The frontier is in a price war — and yet, the average enterprise AI budget grew from $1.2M/year in 2024 to $7M in 2026. Fortune 500 companies are reporting monthly AI inference bills in the tens of millions.

**The math everyone gets wrong:**

They see the per-token cost and think "this will get cheaper." It will. But usage scales faster than price falls. When inference costs drop 10x, companies don't spend 10x less — they run 100x more queries.

Inference has overtaken cloud infrastructure to become the second-largest line item in enterprise AI budgets in 2026. Only talent spend beats it.

**What kills startups:**

A customer paying $20/month can generate $18-25 in inference costs during heavy reasoning tasks. That margin math only works if you've built tiered usage controls, model routing, and caching layers — before you scale.

42% of finance leaders admitted they approved AI spending without reliable cost projections. Not estimates — zero projections. They just said yes and hoped engineering would figure it out.

They won't figure it out. Engineering will build more features, which means more inference, which means a bigger bill.

**What actually works:**

- Model routing: Use $0.15/M input tokens for retrieval, $2/M for reasoning, frontier only for synthesis
- Self-hosted 70B = $950/month vs GPT-4o = $5K/month for the same volume
- Cache aggressively — most enterprise queries repeat 60-70% of prior tokens
- Budget by *outcome*, not by *token* — tie AI spend to revenue metrics, not API calls

The companies that win won't be the ones with the most AI. They'll be the ones who treated inference like infrastructure from day one.

Right now, most companies are treating it like office supplies.
