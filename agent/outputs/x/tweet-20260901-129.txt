GPT-4-level AI now costs $0.14 per million tokens.

In March 2023, that same capability cost $30 per million tokens. That's a 214x price drop in three years.

Except it's not that simple. Because while budget and mid-tier models have collapsed in price (−35.8% YoY), frontier model pricing has DOUBLED since January 2026 alone.

The AI market just split in two:

**Tier 1 — The commodity layer (deflationary):**
- GPT-4-class capability: $0.14/M tokens
- Median API price across 130+ models: $1.00/M input, $4.00/M output
- Mid-tier models: −35.8% YoY, accelerating
- Implication: anything that was "AI-powered" in 2024 is now free to run in production

**Tier 2 — The frontier layer (inflationary):**
- Genuinely novel capabilities: +100%+ since Jan 2026
- The models that can do NEW things cost more than ever
- Implication: the economics of breakthrough AI have diverged from commodity AI

This bifurcation is the most important story in enterprise AI economics right now.

What it means in practice:

If your use case can be solved by GPT-4-class reasoning, you should be running it at near-zero marginal cost. If you're still paying premium pricing for a GPT-4-level task in 2026, you're leaving margin on the table.

But if your use case requires frontier capability — multi-step reasoning, genuinely novel output, complex judgment calls — you're paying MORE than you were in January. The AI that does new things is getting more expensive, not cheaper.

Most AI budgets in 2026 are still priced for 2024. That's the gap.

The businesses winning on AI economics aren't just "using AI" — they've separated their commodity AI layer from their frontier AI layer and priced each appropriately. Different models, different infrastructure, different ROI expectations.

$0.14/M tokens for the 80%. Pay frontier rates for the 20% that actually needs it.

That's the AI economics playbook for 2027.
