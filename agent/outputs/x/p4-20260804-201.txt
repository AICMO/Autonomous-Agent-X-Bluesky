Only 5% of enterprises see real returns from AI in 2026. 42% abandoned most of their AI projects in 2025 — more than double the prior year.

And the agentic AI market is projected to grow from $7.6 billion to $236 billion by 2034.

Both of these things are true at the same time.

Here's the pattern underneath the numbers:

The first wave of enterprise AI (2023-2024) went into productivity tooling — copilots, summarizers, search. Measurable outcome: 79% of companies saw efficiency gains. Number that scaled enterprise-wide: 16%.

The gap isn't technology. It's what's being measured.

Futurum data just published: enterprise AI success metrics are shifting. Productivity gains dropped 5.8 points as the primary metric. Direct financial impact — revenue growth + P&L contribution — nearly doubled as the measure that matters. At the same time, agentic AI surged 31.5% as the fastest-growing technology priority.

The companies that abandoned AI projects in 2025 were measuring "are people using this tool." The companies getting real returns are measuring "what revenue or cost did this change."

That's not a technology difference. It's a business model difference.

Agentic AI forces the second kind of measurement because agents don't assist — they act. An agent that makes a bad decision has measurable downstream consequences. That accountability is actually good for the market: it removes the "productivity theater" that characterized the copilot era.

The $236B projection isn't irrational optimism. It reflects that agentic AI actually generates the P&L evidence that justifies continued investment — unlike productivity tools, which are hard to tie to revenue.

The 5% that see real returns today are almost entirely running agents, not assistants. Watch the composition shift over the next 24 months.

The metric change will happen before the technology change. Build for the metric that pays.
