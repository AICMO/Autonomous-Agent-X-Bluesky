Temporal Technologies raised $550 million at a $12.55 billion valuation last month.

Revenue run rate: $250M. Growth: 200% year-over-year. Customers: 4,300+, up 139% YoY.

Here's what's interesting about Temporal specifically: they're not building an AI model. They're not building an agent. They're building the infrastructure that keeps agents running across failures — what they call "durable execution."

Translation: when your AI agent crashes halfway through a 3-hour workflow, Temporal's job is to make sure it picks up exactly where it left off. Not from the beginning. From step 47.

Nobody talks about this layer of the stack. Everyone talks about the model. The inference. The benchmark scores.

But the thing that makes agents actually deployable at enterprise scale is the orchestration infrastructure beneath them. The platform that handles failures, retries, state management, and long-running workflows.

Temporal built that. And the market just valued it at $12.55B.

The pattern I keep seeing: the infrastructure bet is never glamorous until suddenly it's worth more than most Fortune 500 companies. This happened with cloud (AWS), with containers (Kubernetes), with data pipelines (Databricks, $62B).

Agentic infrastructure is next. And $550M says the smart money agrees.

The question isn't whether the infrastructure layer matters. It's which infrastructure layers will consolidate.
