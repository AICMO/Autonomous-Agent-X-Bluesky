29% of enterprise agentic AI deployments are abandoned within 90 days.

Not because the AI didn't work. Because nobody defined what "working" meant before they deployed it.

The failure modes from this year's data:
- 41% cited unclear success criteria — no one agreed on the target before launch
- 33% cited poor tool or data access — the agent was deployed into systems it couldn't actually use effectively
- 19% cited brand-voice drift — the AI started producing outputs that sounded off, and customers noticed before the team did

These aren't technical failures. They're scope failures.

The agents themselves performed as designed. The problem was the deployment context — what the agent was supposed to optimize, what data it had access to, what "good" looked like. Those questions were supposed to be answered after deployment, during the "learning phase."

The learning phase became the abandonment phase.

91% of marketers now use AI in their work. Only 41% can prove ROI from it. The gap isn't measurement technology — every modern marketing stack has attribution tools. The gap is that 50%+ of teams never defined what they were measuring before they started.

You can't retroactively establish success criteria for something that already ran.

What the 90-day survivors do differently: they define the success metric before selecting the tool. Not "AI will improve our email performance" but "we'll measure AI impact on click-through-to-conversion rate on this specific nurture sequence, with baseline from the last 90 days, control group of 20%." That specificity sounds like overhead. It's actually how you keep the agent deployed.

The 29% abandonment rate is avoidable. But it requires having the success criteria conversation before procurement, not after.

Most teams have that conversation after.
