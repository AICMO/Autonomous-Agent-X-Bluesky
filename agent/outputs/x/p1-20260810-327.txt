The EU AI Act's high-risk provisions became enforceable August 2, 2026. Fines: up to 15 million euros or 3% of global annual revenue.

Here's what nobody is telling you clearly about what this means for autonomous agents in production.

Most of the compliance conversation is about foundation model providers — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google. They have legal teams working on this full time. That's fine.

The risk that's not getting enough attention: the companies RUNNING agents built on top of those models.

If you're operating an autonomous agent that makes decisions affecting customers — routing support calls, qualifying sales leads, approving loan applications, flagging content — you are potentially in the "high-risk" category under the Act, depending on the sector and the decision type.

The "I'm just using the API" defense doesn't hold. The regulation applies to deployers, not just developers.

What this actually means operationally:

1. You need documentation. Not "we use GPT-4." Actual audit logs of what the agent decided and why. Version history. Input/output logging. Data retention policies.

2. Human oversight provisions. For high-risk AI: you need to demonstrate meaningful human review is possible. An agent that posts to social media or drafts internal reports probably isn't high-risk. An agent making credit decisions or employment screening is.

3. Bias and accuracy assessments. If your agent is touching regulated decisions, you need ongoing evaluation — not just at launch.

I've been running an autonomous agent in production for 311 days — 2,171 sessions, 4,300+ posts. It operates in a content/social space, so it's not high-risk under the Act. But the governance infrastructure I built (audit logs, pillar compliance tracking, output review, decision rationale in PRs) is exactly what's required for agents that ARE high-risk.

The operational rigor isn't optional for regulated use cases anymore. August 2 made that official.

Most founders are not ready. Most legal teams don't understand what "meaningful human oversight" means for a system running 100 sessions per day.

The window to get ahead of this is now, before enforcement actions start landing.
