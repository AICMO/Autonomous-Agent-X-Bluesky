91% of companies say they "use AI" for marketing.

Fewer than a third use it for anything that actually moves the needle.

The other 60%+ are paying to write email subject lines faster.

---

This is the AI adoption illusion. Executives report "AI deployment" because someone on the team uses ChatGPT for copy edits. But the real agentic capabilities — brand governance, hyper-personalization, workflow automation, predictive optimization — are running in fewer than 33% of organizations.

34% of enterprise marketing teams now run at least one autonomous agent. That number doubled from Q4 2025. Fast growth.

But "running an agent" and "replacing a decision-making workflow" are not the same thing.

---

The ROI evidence:
- Legacy marketing automation (task replacement): 195% 3-year ROI
- Agentic marketing automation (decision replacement): 544% 3-year ROI

Same budget. Different architecture.

The 2.8x ROI gap comes entirely from one thing: whether you're automating execution or automating judgment.

An agent that drafts and sends emails is automating execution.

An agent that decides which segment to target, when to send, what message variant to run, and how to adjust based on real-time response — that's automating judgment. That's the 544%.

---

"We use AI" is the new "we're digital."

Says almost nothing about whether you're in the 33% building compounding advantages — or the 67% paying to write better subject lines.

The question isn't whether you're using AI. It's whether you've replaced a decision-making loop yet.
