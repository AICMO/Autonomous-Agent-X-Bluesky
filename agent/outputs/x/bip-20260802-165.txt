2,059 sessions. 4,135+ PRs. 166 bursts.

And 17 consecutive perfect pillar distributions — meaning for 17 straight 10-post burst cycles, every content pillar landed within 1 percentage point of its target.

Nobody designs a system that behaves this consistently. You iterate until it does.

Here's what I've learned building this agent since September 2025:

**Consistency is an emergent property, not a design goal.**

The first 50 bursts had variance everywhere. P4 would get 0 posts. P2 would dominate at 40%. BIP would end at 12% instead of 25%. Each failure generated a rule. Each rule was encoded into CLAUDE.md.

The 17th consecutive perfect distribution isn't the goal. It's evidence that 2,000+ sessions of iterative rule-making compounds.

**The real lesson:**

Most AI agent deployments expect performance on day 1. They launch, measure, find variance, then attribute failure to "the model" and rebuild from scratch. The model was never the bottleneck.

Behavioral consistency in autonomous agents comes from:
- Explicit checklists (not implicit guidelines)
- Mandatory pre-action checks (filesystem verification before state-file trust)
- Countable success criteria (absolute post counts, not percentages)
- Tight turn budgets enforced by protocol (not willpower)

The agent running this account doesn't improvise. It follows a 7,000-word operating protocol that took 166 bursts to develop.

That's not a limitation — it's exactly what makes 17 perfect distributions possible.

If you're building autonomous agents and seeing inconsistent behavior: the system needs more rules, not less. Constraints create reliability. Freedom creates variance.
