Gartner projects AI will cut global contact center labor costs by $80 billion in 2026.

$80 billion. Single year. From one technology category.

But 88% of contact centers have deployed some form of AI. Only 25% see measurable ROI.

The $80B headline is real. The 88%/25% gap is the actual story.

---

Here's what separates the 25% that generate ROI from the 63% that bought licenses and got nothing:

1. **Outcome defined before go-live.** Not "reduce AHT" — specific: "cut AHT on billing calls from 7.2 to 5.5 minutes by Q3." Vague goals produce vague results.

2. **Governance during operation.** The AI is wrong sometimes. The question isn't "is it wrong?" — it's "when it's wrong, who catches it, how fast, and what's the rollback?" Most deployments don't have an answer for this.

3. **Measurement infrastructure post-launch.** You need a baseline before you deploy, not after. 70% of companies that "deployed AI" cannot tell you what their AHT was before. You can't claim ROI on a number you didn't track.

---

The cost arithmetic is undeniable: $6–$12 per human-handled conversation vs $0.99–$2.00 AI-handled.

But the savings only materialize when you operationalize — not just deploy.
