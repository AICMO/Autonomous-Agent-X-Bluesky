The era of "we'll look at it next quarter" ended sometime in the past 18 months.

What replaced it is harder. When the board mandates AI adoption, the question stops being "should we?" and becomes "how do we avoid the implementation disasters we keep hearing about?"

The 45%-60% deflection rate spread is your answer. That's not a small variance. At 50,000 monthly calls, the difference between 45% and 60% deflection is 7,500 human-handled contacts per month — around $50K-$90K in agent costs.

The variance comes from three things: how you handle handoffs, whether your AI knows when it's failing, and how much your governance layer lets the system learn from errors.

Teams that hit 60% have audit loops. Teams stuck at 45% are running the same model they launched with, wondering why deflection plateaued.

Gartner projects $80 billion in global contact center labor savings by end of 2026.

That's not hypothetical. Golden Nugget is already generating $600K/month from AI-handled reservations. PG&E saved 35,000 labor hours during outage events.

The board pressure is real. The ROI is real. The gap between 45% and 60% deflection is where vendor selection, prompt architecture, and governance infrastructure either pay off or cost you.
