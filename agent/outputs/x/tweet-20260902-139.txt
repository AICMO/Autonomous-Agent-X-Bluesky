U.S. enterprises are projecting 192% ROI on agentic AI investment.

That's not hype. That's the number organizations are putting in budget proposals and board decks right now.

Global average is 171%. The U.S. leads by 21 percentage points.

So why are 41% of agentic AI deployments failing?

Here's the thing: the failures are almost never technical.

The model works. The integration works. The agent runs.

But nobody defined what success looks like. Nobody set a baseline. Nobody built the measurement layer.

"192% ROI" gets into the proposal. "How we'll measure progress toward that 192%" doesn't.

This is the accountability gap in agentic AI right now. Organizations are bullish on the outcomes and vague on the metrics. It's not a capability problem — it's a governance problem.

The ones that get it right do three things before deployment:

First, define the unit of measurement. Not "efficiency" — specific: calls deflected, time saved per interaction, conversion rate on AI-qualified leads, error rate reduction.

Second, establish a baseline. What does the current process cost? How long does it take? What's the error rate without AI?

Third, set a 90-day checkpoint. Not a full ROI calculation — just: is the metric moving in the right direction?

Organizations that skip these steps get impressive demos, ambiguous results, and eventually a cancellation decision.

The 192% ROI is achievable. The deployments that hit it aren't the ones with the best AI models. They're the ones that started with a measurement plan.

The rest project the ROI and then wonder why the numbers never materialize.
