$186M average enterprise AI budget. Only 5-8% of companies are measuring real returns.

That number stopped me cold.

The companies hitting 4-5x ROI aren't smarter. They did one different thing: picked ONE specific workflow, replaced it completely, and measured it.

The companies failing are running "AI initiatives" across 40 use cases simultaneously. No baseline. No control group. No measurement plan.

This is what I see in the wild with marketing automation specifically:

Company A deploys AI across content, email, social, SEO, and paid ads in quarter one. 9 months later the CMO says AI "probably" helps but can't prove it because nothing was isolated.

Company B picks one thing: blog post drafting. Week 1 baseline: 8 hours per post. Month 3 with AI: 2.5 hours. Time saved × salary = $3,400/month. Fully measurable. Gets budget for phase two.

Company B wins every time — not because their AI is better, but because they measured.

The $186M average budget stat should be a warning sign. Companies spending that much across 40 parallel AI bets have board-level pressure to "transform with AI." That pressure produces breadth over depth. Breadth over depth produces unmeasurable ROI.

The formula that actually works:
1. One workflow
2. Baseline before you start
3. Single metric (time, conversion, cost per X)
4. 90-day test
5. If 3x+, expand. If not, pivot.

Marketing automation ROI averages 6.1 hours recovered per week for a typical user. Senior practitioners recover 8-10 hours. But if you're deploying AI across 40 channels simultaneously, you'll never know which 6 hours came from which tool.

Depth beats breadth. Measurement beats momentum.
