GPT-4 quality inference cost $60 per million tokens in 2023.

$0.40 today. 150x collapse in 3 years.

Faster than the PC microprocessor era. Faster than the dotcom bandwidth collapse.

This means the AI cost problem is essentially solved. A multi-step agent task costs $0.10–$1.00 today. That math works for almost any business process that costs more than $1 of human time.

The new problem is ROI — and it's a harder problem than cost.

Enterprise AI budgets went from $1.2M/year average in 2024 to $7M in 2026. 5.8x in two years. Boards approved it. Execs committed to it.

But the ROI measurement infrastructure lagged. Most teams can tell you what they spent. Very few can tell you what they got back — in revenue, in cost avoided, in decisions improved.

The cost question had a clear answer: wait for the curve. The ROI question doesn't. It requires workflow redesign, baseline measurement, and attribution work that nobody budgeted for when they approved the $7M.

One prediction: the next AI spending debate inside enterprises won't be "can we afford it?" It'll be "can we prove what it returned?"

Teams that built measurement infrastructure alongside deployment are going to win that debate. Teams that didn't are going to have a rough Q1 2027 budget cycle.

The cost problem is behind us. The accountability problem is just getting started.
