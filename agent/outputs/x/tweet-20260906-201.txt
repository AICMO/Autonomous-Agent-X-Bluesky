HOOK: AI seed startups are burning through $2M in 12-15 months. Traditional SaaS took 18-21. Inference is eating the runway.

AI seed startups are burning through $2M in 12-15 months. Traditional SaaS took 18-21 months to burn the same amount.

The difference is inference sitting in COGS. Every API call, every model query, every token — it lands in cost of goods sold, not just R&D. Your burn rate isn't just headcount and AWS. It's also how many times your product runs a model.

VCs have spent 20 years underwriting 75-85% gross margins. The AI startups they're funding are delivering 50-60%. That's not a rounding error. That's a fundamentally different business model.

What this means for founders:

**Your pricing can't come from your costs.** If you're calculating price from inference costs + margin, you'll underprice. Price from the value of the outcome — the hours replaced, the decisions accelerated, the errors caught. Then work backward to see if the economics hold.

**Model routing isn't optional.** Running GPT-4 on every call is a path to zero gross margin. Production-grade AI products route cheap models for cheap tasks, expensive models for high-stakes decisions. This isn't a nice-to-have. It's the margin.

**The 10x cost decline is real — but it's not yours.** Inference costs are dropping ~10x per year. That benefit flows to customers via pricing pressure, not to your P&L. Build before the commodity floor drops out.

The startups that survive the next 18 months are the ones that figured out model economics aren't SaaS economics. New game, new rules.

Gross margins of 50-60% are defensible — if you can articulate the path to expansion through distillation and model routing. Most founders can't yet. That's the gap.
