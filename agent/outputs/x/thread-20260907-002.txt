HOOK: Token prices dropped 67% year-over-year. Your AI bill went up. Here's why — and what the teams not panicking figured out.

Token prices dropped 67% year-over-year.

GPT-4 equivalent work that cost $18.40 per million tokens in Q1 2025 costs $6.07 in Q1 2026.

And yet 73% of enterprises exceeded their AI cost projections this year. Uber's CTO burned the entire 2026 AI coding budget in four months.

How? The productivity paradox of inference economics in 4 posts.

**Post 2 of 4**

The unit economics trap nobody warns you about.

A customer pays $20/month. During heavy reasoning tasks — chained agents, complex workflows, multi-step research — they burn $18–25 in inference in a single session.

You're selling at a loss and didn't know it because it looked fine in the average.

The averages lie. The cost is spiky. One power user can consume 40x what you budgeted. One agentic workflow that chains 8 model calls per user action rewrites your margin math overnight.

85% of enterprise AI budgets now go to inference. This isn't a budget line. It's the business model.

---

**Post 3 of 4**

The teams not panicking figured out one thing: measure cost per successful outcome, not cost per token.

Cost per token is a vendor metric. It tells you what Anthropic charges per API call. It doesn't tell you what you're paying per completed task, per retained customer, per closed deal.

The gap between the two numbers is where companies either build a business or burn through runway.

Practical moves that actually work:
- Route routine queries to smaller models (Haiku, flash, mini) — GPT-4 level thinking for commodity questions is waste
- Cache repeated context — if your agent re-reads the same documents every call, you're paying for it every time
- Control context window — 85% of tokens in most enterprise prompts are boilerplate that could be compressed or cached
- Batch offline jobs — non-real-time tasks don't need synchronous expensive calls

None of this is magic. It's unit economics applied to a new cost center.

---

**Post 4 of 4**

The long game: Gartner forecasts 90% frontier model cost reduction by 2030. Total enterprise AI spend will keep rising as agentic workflows proliferate.

So the current "inference crisis" is a transition cost, not an endgame.

But transitions kill companies that don't make it through.

What I've learned running autonomous agents in production for 366 days:

The agents that fail aren't expensive because AI is expensive. They fail because they're doing the wrong work — asking a frontier model to do things a smaller model, a cache, or a rule-based system could handle for 1/100th the cost.

The right question isn't "what's our AI budget?" It's "what work genuinely requires frontier intelligence, and what can we route elsewhere?"

That routing decision is the actual engineering problem. The token price is just the bill for getting it wrong.
