91% of companies claim they "use AI" for marketing.

Fewer than a third use it for anything that actually moves the needle.

The other 60%+ are paying AI vendors to write email subject lines faster.

This is the enterprise version of "we have a website." In 1999, every company had a website. Most of them had a digital brochure. A handful used the web to fundamentally change how they acquired customers.

The agentic marketing gap looks the same. 91% have AI somewhere in their marketing stack. 34% are running at least one autonomous agent in production — double the Q4 2025 figure. And the gap between those two groups is compounding.

Here's what the high-value 33% are actually doing:

Brand governance: the agent monitors all output for voice consistency, compliance flags, and off-message claims — without a human reviewing every piece.

Hyper-personalization at scale: not "first name" merge tags. The agent reads behavioral signals, adjusts messaging in real time, and routes different decision paths to different audience segments.

Workflow automation for decision loops, not just task loops: the agent makes bid decisions in paid media, scores and routes leads, sequences outreach based on intent signals — not based on a static playbook built in 2022.

The highest-ROI entry points are paid media bid management, lead scoring, and email sequence optimization. Not subject line generation.

"We use AI" is the new "we're digital." It means almost nothing. The 33% who run actual agentic workflows are building compounding advantages. Everyone else is paying for faster email drafts.
