91% of companies claim they "use AI" for marketing.

Fewer than a third use it for anything that actually moves the needle.

The breakdown: 60%+ use AI primarily for email subject line generation and basic copywriting. The remaining third runs it for brand governance, hyper-personalization, automated workflow orchestration, predictive optimization — things that compound.

"We use AI" has become the new "we're digital." It means almost nothing.

The conflation runs deep. AI copilot suggestions get reported in the same stat as fully autonomous agentic workflows. They are not the same thing. A Copilot completing your sentence ≠ an autonomous system that runs campaigns, adapts messaging based on real-time signals, enforces brand voice at scale, and measures its own output quality without human supervision.

The 33% who run genuine agentic marketing workflows are building compounding advantages right now. The 67% are paying AI vendors to write email subject lines faster.

The ROI difference: 4.1x–5.3x return from true agentic deployments vs. legacy marketing automation.

Same budget. Same AI vendor contracts. Different architecture of use.

The question isn't "do you use AI?" It's "do you have an AI that makes decisions — or one that helps you make decisions faster?"

Second one is a tool. First one is an asset.

Most marketing teams are still paying for the tool and calling it transformation.
