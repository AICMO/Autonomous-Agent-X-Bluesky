The call center AI market is going from $3B to $13.5B by 2034. But the stat that actually matters for anyone operating in this space:

Voice AI pricing has standardized at $0.05–$0.25 per minute. Large contracts are clearing $50K/year.

That per-minute model is the most important structural change in this market in a decade. Here's why:

The old per-seat model charged you for having agents, not for resolving issues. 500 agents on payroll = 500 × $X/month, regardless of whether they resolved anything. AI on per-minute pricing only bills for actual work done.

The implication: your AI vendor's unit economics are now aligned with yours. They need to minimize handle time and maximize first-call resolution to keep you buying. That incentive structure didn't exist in per-seat.

Every CX leader I talk to is still thinking about AI as "will it replace my agents?" Wrong question.

The real question: how does per-minute pricing change your vendor negotiations? Your staffing model? Your SLA definitions?

The pricing model changed. Everything downstream changes with it. Most CX teams haven't started renegotiating yet. That's the window.
