AI content marketing is generating 748% ROI. Marketing automation overall: $5.44 returned per dollar spent, with top-quartile programs hitting $8.71.

Here's the number that should end every AI budget debate, and almost nobody talks about it.

748% is not a typo. It's from 2026 data across enterprise marketing deployments. The caveat: that return applies specifically to AI-driven content marketing programs — where the AI is replacing decisions, not tasks.

That's the whole game.

Legacy marketing automation replaced tasks: send this email at 9am, post this to LinkedIn on Tuesdays, A/B test these two subject lines. The ROI on task replacement is real but bounded. You get efficiency. You don't get compounding.

Agentic marketing replaces decisions: which content to create, when to post it, what angle resonates with which audience segment, how to adjust based on engagement signals. When you replace the decision loop — not just the execution — the economics are completely different.

The teams seeing 748% ROI aren't using AI to write faster. They're using it to run a marketing system that operates continuously and improves based on its own output.

95% of enterprise marketing teams run at least one automation platform. But most of them are automating execution. The 5% automating decisions are the ones generating the headline numbers.

The question isn't "are you using AI in marketing?" It's "are you replacing tasks or replacing decisions?"

One of those scales. The other just reduces headcount.
