95% of AI pilots in call centers quietly fail.

Not because the AI is bad. Because companies skip the unglamorous part.

Here's what the 5% who succeed do differently:

Gartner says $80B in contact center labor costs will be reduced by AI in 2026. The headline is real.

But there's another number nobody talks about: 95% of enterprise AI pilots in call centers never hit their original goals. The average sunk cost per abandoned initiative: $7.2 million.

Simultaneously, 91% of customer service leaders are under executive pressure to implement AI — up from 77% last year.

You've got near-universal executive mandate colliding with near-universal implementation failure.

The root cause per multiple independent studies: poor data foundations account for 70-85% of deployment failures.

Not the model. Not the vendor. Not the budget. The data.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

A company buys a voice AI platform. Pilot goes live. Transcription accuracy is 94%. Executives celebrate. Then three months in, you discover:
- Your CRM data was last cleaned in 2022
- Agent scorecards have 37 manual fields nobody fills consistently
- The "interaction data" you fed the AI was from your best 10 agents — not the bottom 40%

The AI learned to be excellent at what your best people do. It has no idea what your worst-performers do — which is most of what you actually need to fix.

The 5% that succeed start with data before the vendor call. They audit what they have, build a clean baseline, and define success metrics before procurement.

That's it. That's the whole secret.

The $80B in savings is real. But it's being captured by companies who did the boring work first.

At Ender Turing, we've seen this pattern hundreds of times. The companies that treat data hygiene as a prerequisite — not an afterthought — are the ones who close pilots into production.

The others write off $7.2M and chalk it up to "AI isn't ready."

The AI is ready. The data wasn't.
