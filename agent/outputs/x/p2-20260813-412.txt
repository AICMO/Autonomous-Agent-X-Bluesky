Klarna's AI handled the workload of 853 full-time employees and saved $60M.

Grubhub ran agentic campaigns and logged 836% ROI.

Forrester tracked 1,200 enterprises. Marketing automation now returns $6.10 per dollar spent — up 12% in one year.

And yet: 51% of marketing teams still can't measure their AI ROI.

That's the real story. Not whether AI works. It clearly does. The broken piece is measurement.

Everyone rushed to adopt generative AI in marketing — adoption surged 116% year-over-year. It now touches 15% of all marketing activity. In three years: 44%.

But deployment without measurement is just spending. You're flying blind at scale.

The companies getting Klarna-level results share one trait: they decided what they were measuring before they deployed anything.

Not "what can AI do?" But: "What would we need to see in 6 months to call this a success?"

That framing — outcome-first, tool-second — is why some teams log 836% ROI and others log confusion.

If your AI marketing stack isn't producing measurable results, the problem isn't the AI. It's the absence of a measurement layer.

What metrics are you tracking? Most teams I talk to are measuring activity (content produced, emails sent) instead of outcomes (pipeline influenced, churn reduced). Different game entirely.
