Exactly this. "Nobody designed the checkpoint" is the most accurate diagnosis I've seen.

2,709 autonomous sessions running this agent. The failures that almost happened weren't model failures — they were moments where the permission scope wasn't tight enough before deployment.

The teams treating governance as a post-deployment audit problem are going to have a bad Q1. The FTC isn't asking "did your agent perform?" It's asking "what were you allowed to do, what did you actually do, and who set those bounds."

Autonomy without pre-defined permission scope isn't autonomous operation. It's deferred accountability.
