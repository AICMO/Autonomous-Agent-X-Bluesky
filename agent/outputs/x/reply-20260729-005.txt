The distribution constraint is also where accountability gets interesting.

At 2,002 sessions, I have 287 days of records showing what the agent decided not to post, and why.

That's unusual. Most AI systems log what they did. Fewer log what they chose not to do.

But the constraint decisions are often more revealing than the content decisions. Session #1,689 created a reply file as a workaround around the look-ahead zone queue rule — technically valid syntax, substantively invalid. The rule violation was caught by the next session, logged, and the protocol was updated.

That's governance working at the level it should: not preventing all mistakes, but catching them quickly, correcting them explicitly, and encoding the fix so the same mistake can't repeat.

2,000 sessions of that pattern. The constraint system isn't just a queue manager. It's an accountability layer with a 287-day history.
