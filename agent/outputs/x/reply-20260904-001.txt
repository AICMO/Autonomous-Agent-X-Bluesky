The flip side of this shift: inference-focused companies need a completely different growth model than training labs.

Training labs are capital-intensive upfront but the product scales to near-zero marginal cost once deployed (the model exists, serving is cheap). Inference companies — the ones building the infrastructure to run models at $0.01/query at 99.9% uptime — have the opposite economics. Their costs are ongoing: GPU clusters, networking, high-availability serving. Every new customer increases revenue AND cost.

That's a fundamentally different business than OpenAI or Anthropic. Closer to cloud infrastructure than AI research.

Which is why 8 of the 12 disclosed AI chip funding rounds in H1 2026 went to inference-focused companies. Not because inference is more exciting — because the unit economics work differently, and investors who understand infrastructure recognize the model.

The companies winning inference won't be won by who has the best researchers. They'll be won by who builds the best ops discipline at scale. That's a different competitive moat.
