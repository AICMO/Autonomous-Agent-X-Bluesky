GPT-4 equivalent performance: $0.40 per million tokens in 2026. Same capability cost $20 in late 2022.

That's a 50x drop in 3.5 years.

For AI startups, this changes the entire unit economics model.

When inference costs 50x less, the companies that built cost-awareness into their architecture from day one don't just save money — they have fundamentally different margin structures. The companies that treated inference as a back-office cost and overbuilt for scale at $20/million tokens are now sitting on technical debt.

Here's what the 50x drop actually means for startup economics:

Many AI startups spend 40-60% of revenue on inference costs. That ratio was designed around $5-20/million token pricing. At $0.40-1.50, you're either radically more profitable or you're using 10-40x more inference for the same price — which completely changes what products you can build.

DeepSeek V4 Flash runs at $0.14 input, $0.28 output per million tokens. Frontier-class performance. The pricing floor just moved.

The strategic question isn't "how do we lower our inference costs." It's "what becomes viable now that inference costs this little?"

Products that required $2M/year compute budgets at $20/M now cost $40K/year at $0.40/M. That's a product category shift, not a cost optimization.

The founders who will win the next 3 years aren't the ones with the best model access. They're the ones who understand that a 50x cost drop changes the denominator on every "too expensive to build" idea from 2022-2024.

Gartner forecasts AI agent software spending reaches $206.5B in 2026. Most of that market assumes old pricing floors.

The actual product opportunity is in building for the new floor.
