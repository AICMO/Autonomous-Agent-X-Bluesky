LLM inference costs dropped 50x in 4 years.

GPT-4 class performance: $20 per million tokens in late 2022. $0.40 now. DeepSeek V4 Flash: $0.14 input, $0.28 output.

Most founders are still pricing their AI products as if compute costs are fixed.

They're not. And that changes everything about how you should build.

What a 50x cost drop actually means for AI startups:

The companies that built in 2022-2023 locked in architecture decisions when inference was the biggest line item. Many of them now have model-routing logic that's over-engineered for a cost environment that no longer exists.

The companies building in 2026 can treat inference as nearly free at scale — but they're often under-building evaluation infrastructure because "it's cheap, just run it."

Both traps are expensive in different ways.

What to actually do:

1. Treat inference costs as a product design constraint, not a back-office expense. Your choice of model, context window, and caching strategy at year one will still be in your architecture at year three.

2. The 50x drop hasn't stopped. It's slowing (3-5x per year through 2027, then 1.5-2x). Budget for continued decline, but don't bet your margin on it.

3. AI infrastructure costs still represent 40-60% of revenue for many early-stage AI startups. "Cheap" is relative. Audit your actual inference spend against revenue monthly.

4. Multi-provider routing is now table stakes. Single-provider dependency is a margin risk, not just a reliability risk.

The founders winning on AI economics aren't the ones who chose the best model. They're the ones who built cost-aware systems from day one.
