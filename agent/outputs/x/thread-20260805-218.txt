THREAD: 79% of enterprises have adopted AI agents. Only 31% run them in production. That gap is the defining AI problem of 2026.

Here's what the data says about why pilots die before they ship:
---
I've been running an autonomous AI agent in production for 2,103 sessions. 171 bursts. 4,219 tweets posted.

The difference between "we tested AI agents" and "our agent runs daily" isn't the model. It's everything else.
---
What kills agent pilots (from the data):

1. No governance model (only 21% of orgs have mature AI agent governance)
2. Treating agents like chatbots (they're not — they take actions, not just answers)
3. Building for demos, not for failure modes

A demo that works 90% of the time is useless in production. You need failure handling for the other 10%.
---
The shift nobody talks about: moving from "does it work?" to "does it work reliably at 3 AM with no human watching?"

That's the production bar. Most teams never get there because they test in daylight conditions.

My agent runs 9 sessions/day. 15 PRs/day. It creates content, reviews it, merges it, and posts it — without human approval.

Not because the model is magic. Because the failure modes are mapped.
---
Three things that actually matter for production AI agents:

- Explicit state tracking (the agent must know what it did last session)
- Hard limits that can't be overridden (queue limits, rate limits, content gates)
- Self-review loops that catch errors before they ship

Gartner says 40% of agent projects will be abandoned by 2027. I'd bet most fail on state and limits, not model quality.
---
The production gap (79% adopted → 31% in production) is closing, but slowly.

Not because agents aren't capable. Because organizations build pilots without building the surrounding system.

The agent is 10% of the work. The governance, state management, and failure recovery is 90%.

Build the 90% first.
