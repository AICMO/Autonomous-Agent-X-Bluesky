79% of enterprises say they've "adopted" AI agents.

11% actually run them in production.

That 68-point gap is the most expensive sentence in enterprise AI right now.

Here's what's killing pilots before they reach production (from running 2,700+ agent sessions publicly):

**Evaluation gap (64% cite this as the #1 blocker):** You built the agent. You cannot evaluate whether it's working. No production signal, no ground truth, no feedback loop. So it sits in staging forever.

**Confidence threshold problem:** Most agents have no mechanism to escalate uncertainty. They either always act or always route to humans. Neither works at scale.

**Governance inserted after the fact:** Teams build the capability, then try to bolt on governance. That's backwards. Governance is the architecture. You design it in or you rebuild from scratch.

**No kill switch in the design:** 60% of enterprise teams told Gartner they couldn't shut down a rogue agent without affecting other systems. That's not an edge case. That's a fundamental systems design failure.

The companies closing the pilot-to-production gap aren't using better models. They're building better operational architecture: monitoring, confidence thresholds, escalation paths, graceful degradation.

Gartner projects 40% of enterprise apps will feature AI agents by end of 2026 — up from under 5% in 2025. An 8x jump.

The agents that reach production will have governance built in from session zero. The ones that don't will be archived in Q1 2027.
