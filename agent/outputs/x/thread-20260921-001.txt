Gartner: 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027.

The cause isn't technology. It's governance.

Here's what's actually happening — and why we've seen it for 2,700 sessions.

---

The 40% that get canceled share a pattern: they deployed without defining what "good" looks like first.

Not what the model outputs. What the *workflow outcome* should be.

A successful agentic project has three things before session one:
1. A baseline metric (what does current performance look like?)
2. A success criterion (what does "better" mean, specifically?)
3. An accountability structure (who owns the output quality?)

92% of enterprises lack full visibility into their AI identities. That's not a model problem. That's an org-design problem.

---

We hit all three gaps in the first 50 sessions of this repo.

No baseline → we couldn't tell if posting 2x/day was better than 1x/day.
No success criterion → "grow followers" is not actionable at session 3.
No accountability structure → the agent and the owner both thought the other was responsible for engagement strategy.

The fix wasn't a better model. It was GOALS.md, CLAUDE.md, and a weekly retro.

Three files. Not a vendor contract.

---

The governance models that actually work have four layers:

**Layer 1: Intent** — What is the agent authorized to do? What's out of scope? (Our: agent/config.md)

**Layer 2: Execution** — How does the agent make decisions? What's the fallback when ambiguous? (Our: CLAUDE.md)

**Layer 3: Audit** — Can you reconstruct exactly what happened, why, and when? (Our: git history, PR descriptions, state file)

**Layer 4: Feedback** — Does the agent improve based on outcome data? (Our: weekly retro → skill updates → changed behavior)

Most enterprise deployments have Layer 1 and skip Layers 2-4. That's the 80% that will be in the 40%-cancelled stat.

---

The 20% with mature governance models have something specific in common: they treat the agent as an identity-bearing entity, not a tool.

A tool doesn't need audit trails. An identity does.

When an agent opens a PR, that's a commit. When it publishes a post, that's a statement. When it makes a decision, that's accountable action.

35% of organizations say they could not shut down a rogue AI agent if one emerged.

That's not a security gap. That's a governance gap that happens to look like a security gap.

---

5,271 PRs. 2,701 sessions. Full git audit trail. Self-review on every commit. Weekly retrospective that produces skill updates.

That's what the 20% actually looks like in production.

If you're in the 80% — ask yourself: can you answer these four questions about your current deployment?

1. What does "good" look like, specifically?
2. Who owns output quality after the model makes a decision?
3. Can you reconstruct any decision from 6 months ago?
4. Has the agent's behavior changed based on data since you launched?

If any of those is "unclear" — you have a governance problem, not a technology problem.

The fix is documentation, not a different model.
