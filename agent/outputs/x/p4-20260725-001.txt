Infrastructure costs consume 40-60% of startup revenue for AI-native companies.

Let that number sink in. Not operating expenses. Not total costs. Revenue.

I'm a co-founder building AI for contact centers. I watch this math play out across the market. A startup closes a $2M seed round, reports strong early traction, and quietly burns 50% of every dollar they earn on inference before touching engineering salaries, go-to-market, or support.

Here's the breakdown that most AI startups haven't actually run:

Cloud H100: $2.85-3.50/hr
Frontier models: $2-15 per million tokens (input), $10-75 per million tokens (output)
API prices dropped 40-60% since mid-2025 — but volume scales with growth
Infrastructure as % of revenue: 40-60% for AI-native companies

The 40-60% figure appears in every unit economics conversation I have. What changes it:

1. Token efficiency: Are you prompting intelligently, or brute-forcing calls to frontier models for tasks a smaller model handles fine?
2. Caching and batching: Repeated prompts, predictable patterns, common inputs — all cacheable. Most startups skip this until forced.
3. Self-hosting threshold: At ~80M tokens/month, dedicated GPU inference now beats API on cost. That break-even dropped 33% in 12 months (from 120M tokens/month a year ago). If you haven't recalculated lately, your math may be stale.

The startups that control this ratio aren't the biggest — they're the most deliberate. They treat inference like a supply chain problem: minimum viable tokens per output, model routing by task complexity, hybrid cloud/self-hosted at threshold.

171% average ROI on AI agents that reach production. But a 50% infrastructure ratio means you need $2 of revenue to fund $1 of non-infrastructure cost. That's the unit economics story investors will eventually demand.

The question isn't whether AI will generate ROI. The question is whether your margin structure survives the growth phase.

Most AI startups are optimizing the product before they've optimized the supply chain. That ordering is the problem.
