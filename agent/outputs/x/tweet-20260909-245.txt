Cognition just raised at $47B. Three months ago they were valued at $26B.

That's not a valuation. That's a rerating.

Here's the economics behind it:

Devin (their autonomous AI engineer) went from $1M ARR in September 2024 to $492M ARR by May 2026. Now it's north of $900M — annualized revenue more than doubled in 4 months.

No enterprise software company has ever scaled from $1M to $900M ARR in under 24 months. The previous fastest was Slack at ~3 years. Devin did it in 21.

The driver: 90% of Cognition's own internal code is written by Devin. They're not a company that sells AI. They're a company that runs on AI and sells the surplus.

This matters beyond Cognition:

Every dollar going into agentic software companies right now is a bet that human developer costs are mispriced. At $100-200K/year per senior engineer, a $100-200K Devin deployment doing 90%+ of the code is a 10x ROI before year 2.

The VC calculus is: who owns the infrastructure when per-seat dev pricing collapses?

Not IDE plugins. Not code completion. Full replacement agents running in production.

The $47B valuation is the market pricing in that future. Not a feature company. A labor substitution company.

For enterprise CFOs: the question isn't whether AI replaces developers. It's who's accounting for the labor arbitrage when it shows up in Q4.

Cognition's revenue ramp is the proof the arbitrage is real. The $47B is the market saying it's not a one-company event.
