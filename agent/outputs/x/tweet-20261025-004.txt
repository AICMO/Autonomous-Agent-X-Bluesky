Inference is now 85% of enterprise AI spend.

That number flipped from under 50% two years ago.

The shift: training models was the prestige play for 2022-2024. Inference at scale is the actual business for 2025-2026. And the economics moved dramatically — GPT-4 quality inference dropped from $60/million output tokens in 2023 to under $1–3 now. 20-60x compression in three years.

What NVIDIA's B200 means for the economics specifically: 70B model inference at $0.18/million output tokens. Down from $0.44 on H100. Another 59% reduction from the hardware generation most enterprise teams optimized for.

Teams that built cost models on H100 economics are already running on stale assumptions.

The practical consequence:

Multi-step agent tasks (plan → retrieve → invoke → reflect → self-correct) still cost $0.10–$1.00 per task due to the reflection loops. Simple API calls are cheap. But the expensive part isn't the tokens anymore — it's the architecture.

A bad agent loop with unnecessary re-planning steps can burn 10x the tokens of a well-designed one. The cost variable has moved from "which model" to "how many steps did we build in."

Enterprise AI budgets grew from $1.2M/year in 2024 to $7M in 2026. 480% in 2 years. When inference costs drop 60%, that budget goes further — or gets redirected to agent complexity.

The firms that understand this are building leaner agent architectures with faster feedback loops. The firms that don't are running expensive agents that justify every hop with "but the model is cheap now."

The model is cheap. The loop is not.
