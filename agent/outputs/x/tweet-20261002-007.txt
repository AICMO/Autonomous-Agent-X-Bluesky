AI voice agents cost $0.07-0.15 per minute.

U.S. human agents cost $29-42 per hour.

The math is not close. But half of executives say they can't quantify any return from their call center AI investments.

The data doesn't contradict itself — it reveals the actual problem.

Cost reduction is real. The savings on call volume are measurable. What's NOT measurable for most operators is the delta on the customer experience side: first-call resolution, escalation rate, CSAT for AI-handled calls vs human-handled calls. Because they never set up measurement infrastructure before deploying.

They deployed for cost. They're failing to prove value because they didn't define value before launch.

The call centers seeing positive ROI in 3-6 months share one pattern: they automated specific, structured call types — lead qualification, appointment booking, account inquiries — where the success criteria were already defined and easy to measure.

The ones stuck with ambiguous ROI took a different path: deployed AI broadly, assumed CX metrics would improve, and are now running A/B tests retroactively on a system that's already live.

Gartner says conversational AI will reduce agent labor costs by $80 billion by 2026. That's real. But $80B in cost reduction doesn't automatically become $80B in value if the savings are offset by a 15% drop in CSAT that nobody was tracking.

The governance problem in call center AI is the measurement problem in disguise. You can't govern what you haven't defined.
