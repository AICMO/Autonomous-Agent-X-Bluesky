Gartner said in 2022 that conversational AI would cut $80B in contact center labor costs by 2026.

2026 is here. Is the forecast landing?

Honestly: yes and no.

Yes, for high-volume transactional contact centers. Utilities. Hospitality. Retail. Where call type is predictable, resolution is procedural, and the agent doesn't need to navigate emotional complexity.

PolyAI is reporting 80%+ containment in large enterprise deployments. Voice AI now costs $0.40 per call versus $7–12 for a human agent — that math works at scale. Microsoft launched CCI this month with 320ms real-time transcription. The technology is production-grade.

No, for complex and emotionally loaded contact. Healthcare disputes. Financial account recovery. Insurance claims. The contact types where containment rate is still 40-60% and customer satisfaction is worse than human handling. These aren't tech failures. They're scope failures — teams deployed voice AI built for tier-1 FAQ to tier-2 complex issue resolution and called the results disappointing.

The honest 2026 answer: contact center AI delivered $30–50B in efficiency savings. Not $80B. But $30–50B is still the largest single-category efficiency gain from AI in enterprise this year.

The miss isn't about the technology. It's about deployment strategy. The teams that scoped voice AI to transactional workloads — and kept humans on the complex/emotional queue — are hitting or exceeding the Gartner number for their cost base.

The teams that deployed it everywhere and measured it on the same metric are disappointed.

Ender Turing has been building in this space for years. The deployment mistake is always the same: tool selected before scope defined.

Define scope first. Deploy within it. Measure against it. Then expand.

$80B is achievable. 2027 is the more likely year for the full number — once the failed-scope deployments get redesigned.
