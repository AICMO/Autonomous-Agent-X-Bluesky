Gartner just published something that will reshape how companies budget for AI in 2027.

Per-token inference costs are down 95% over two years. That's real. That's not spin.

But agentic workflow inference costs — the actual cost to run a multi-step agent that makes decisions, calls tools, loops back, and completes a task — will increase more than 5x through 2028.

Same token prices. Higher total costs. How?

Because agents don't run one query. They run 40-200 queries per task. Planning, tool selection, error recovery, verification, retry. Every step burns tokens. Every loop compounds. The per-token price is a misleading denominator when the numerator keeps scaling with task complexity.

This is the Jevons Paradox applied to AI agents. Cheaper per unit → more units used per task → higher total spend. We've already watched this play out with compute and bandwidth. It's running the exact same pattern with inference.

The businesses winning right now are the ones treating agentic infrastructure as a cost center that needs margin discipline — not a "cheap AI" play. If you're pricing your agent products on 2023 per-token economics, you're setting yourself up for margin compression at scale.

What does this mean practically?

1. Architecture matters more than token price. How many loops does your agent take? How much redundant verification? This is your real cost driver.
2. Task scope definition is now a unit economics problem. Unbounded agentic tasks have unbounded cost profiles.
3. The inference infrastructure plays — Fireworks AI just raised $1.5B, Together AI $800M — aren't bets on cheap tokens. They're bets on the volume explosion from agentic usage.

The per-token race to zero is real. The total cost of agentic workflows is going the other direction.

Source: Gartner, August 17 2026.
