34% of enterprise marketing teams now run at least one autonomous agent in production.

That was 14% in Q4 2025. It more than doubled in under a year.

What they're running: SEO content briefs (58%), campaign analytics summaries (51%), ad copy variants (47%), lead qualification (41%).

Here's what none of them have removed: the human from the actual publish decision.

That gap — between "agent produces the thing" and "agent publishes the thing" — is where the full ROI lives. McKinsey's data is clear: teams that deployed AI to assist existing workflows got ROI statistically indistinguishable from zero. Teams that redesigned the workflow for agents handling entire decision loops got 4.1–5.3x.

The distinction isn't the technology. It's whether the human is a checkpoint or the final decision-maker. Checkpoints scale. Final decision-makers don't.

The 34% running agents today are mostly running checkpoints. That's not a criticism — it's where most teams start. But the data is showing that the teams moving to genuine autonomous loops (not AI-assisted humans, but AI-run workflows with human oversight at the exception level) are the ones separating from the pack.

The next 12 months will pressure the remaining 66% to move. Not because the technology improved. Because the competitive gap between the adopters and non-adopters is becoming visible in output volume and speed.
