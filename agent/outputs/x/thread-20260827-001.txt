Top contact center AI deployments automate 70%+ of support volume in the first quarter.

Median deployments: 20-30%.

Same vendors. Same models. 8x difference in ROI.

Here's what separates them. [Thread]

---

The difference isn't AI capability. The models available to both groups are nearly identical.

The difference is what happened in the 8 weeks before the contract was signed.

Top performers had three things in place before deployment:

1. Clean contact taxonomy — every contact reason categorized, with "other" under 5% of volume
2. Accurate routing architecture — intent mapped to resolution path, not to department
3. Measurable resolution standards — FCR tracked per-intent, not blended

Median performers had a vendor pitch deck and an SLA.

---

The wall at 20-30% automation isn't a technology ceiling. AI can handle far more.

The wall is data quality. Specifically:

You can't automate what you can't categorize. If "billing issue" contains 14 different problem types, the agent can't route it — it can only escalate it.

You can't route what you can't identify. If your intent taxonomy has 40% in catch-all buckets, you've told your AI that 40% of customers are unknowns.

You can't improve what you don't measure. If FCR is a blended metric, you have no signal on which intents need work.

---

This is why top performers spend 3-6 weeks on taxonomy cleanup before vendor evaluation.

Not because it's exciting work. Because it's the only work that actually moves the automation ceiling.

The economics:
- 70% automation rate → 8x ROI
- 20-30% automation rate → 1-2x ROI
- Gap: 6-7x return on identical technology investment

The variable isn't the AI. It's whether your operations team did the foundational work that made the AI useful.

---

One number from this that nobody talks about:

The taxonomy cleanup — the 3-6 weeks of categorizing contacts, cleaning routing logic, setting FCR standards — costs roughly 2-3% of what the AI contract costs.

The teams that skip it lose 80-85% of the ROI they paid for.

Most contact center AI implementations aren't failing because the AI is bad. They're failing because the team that deployed it never gave it clean data to work with.

Fix the taxonomy first. Evaluate vendors second. In that order, every time.
