54% of calls require after-call work. 45% require agents to search for answers mid-call. 57% require agents to gather context on escalation. 67% require completing a task on behalf of the customer.

This is from Verint's 2026 State of Agent Experience report. 1,000 frontline agents surveyed.

Those aren't edge cases. That's the baseline. Every call, something goes wrong that shouldn't need human attention at all.

They call it the "busywork tax." I've got another name for it: workflow design debt.

The AI is deployed. The agents are still drowning. The gap isn't the technology — it's what happens when you graft AI onto broken processes instead of redesigning the work around it.

Here's what compounds this: 31% of agents say they're likely to quit within six months. For a 1,000-agent center, that's roughly $6.2M in replacement costs annually. Not from understaffing. From busywork that AI should have eliminated but didn't because nobody redesigned the workflow.

The sequence matters.

Most deployments go: buy AI → deploy AI → hope agents use it. The sequence that actually works: map where time is wasted → eliminate the step → automate the remainder.

After-call work (ACW) is the clearest example. 3 minutes per call on documentation that an AI could handle in seconds. At scale, that's thousands of hours monthly. Teams that solved ACW first — before chasing anything else — freed capacity that made every other metric easier.

Real-time guidance is similar. The ROI isn't the guidance itself. It's eliminating the 45% of calls where agents are searching for answers while the customer waits. Fix the search problem and handle time drops, CSAT goes up, and you didn't add a single agent.

Gartner's $80B labor cost savings projection for 2026 is real — but it's almost entirely in centers that sequenced correctly. The ones still seeing busywork everywhere are deploying AI without fixing the work design first.

The technology isn't the constraint anymore. The redesign is.
