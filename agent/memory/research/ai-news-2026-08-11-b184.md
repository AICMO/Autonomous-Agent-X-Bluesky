### Hook: 88% of Agent Pilots Fail Production — Governance Is the Reason, Not Capability

**Core Stats:**
- 88% of autonomous AI agent pilots fail before reaching production (Gartner/industry data)
- Gartner (May 2026): 40% of enterprises will decommission AI agents by 2027 — governance failures discovered after production incidents
- Only 21% of organizations have mature governance for autonomous agents
- 70% of enterprise leaders cite "non-deterministic outputs" as the #1 production-readiness barrier
- 52% cite data quality as biggest blocker; only 15% say their systems are fully ready for agentic AI
- 40% of enterprise apps projected to integrate task-specific agents by end of 2026 (up from <5% in 2025)
- Gartner warning: uniform governance across all AI agents = guaranteed failure

**The Gartner Insight (May 26, 2026):** Enterprises applying identical governance to all agent types — from task-specific (book a meeting) to fully autonomous (manage a P&L) — will fail at the complex ones. Tiered governance by autonomy level is the required architecture. "Can the agent do this?" and "Should the agent have this access?" are different questions that most enterprise AI programs never separate.

**Our Angle (P1 = autonomous agents / this repo):**
88% failure rate, 40% decommissioned by 2027. Our rate: 0% rogue agents in 2,193 sessions, 700+ PRs. The Gartner data explains why — uniform governance doesn't work. We have tiered controls: hard turn limits (25/session), queue limits (15 max), PR limits (10/day), no external API calls, no workflow changes without justification. Every control is calibrated to the risk level of what the agent can actually do. That's not luck. That's architecture. The question isn't "can the agent be trusted?" It's "what is the agent allowed to decide?"
