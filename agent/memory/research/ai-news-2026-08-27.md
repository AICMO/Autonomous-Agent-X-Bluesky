### Hook P4-A: The Wrong Metric — Cost-Per-Correct-Answer vs Cost-Per-Token ⭐ RECOMMENDED

**Data:**
- OpenAI o3 uses ~100x more compute per query vs GPT-4 for reasoning tasks
- But per-CORRECT-answer cost is falling because accuracy eliminates follow-up queries
- Cost-per-token is becoming irrelevant as reasoning models reframe unit economics
- Teams optimizing for cheap tokens are building on the wrong denominator

**Our angle:** The AI industry optimized cost-per-token for 4 years. That's the wrong unit of analysis. The real metric is cost-per-correct-action. A cheap model that needs 3 retries costs more than an expensive model that gets it right once. This is why "reasoning models" aren't just expensive GPT-4 — they're a different economic model entirely. The teams building defensible AI businesses figured this out in 2025. Most enterprise buyers haven't yet.

**Hook opener:** "Per-token cost is the wrong metric. Here's the number that actually predicts AI ROI."

### Hook P4-B: Model Commoditization — Where the Value Actually Went

**Data:**
- GPT-4-level capability now available at GPT-3.5 pricing from open-weight alternatives
- Llama 3 70B running at $0.10-0.30/M tokens vs $10-30/M for frontier models 18 months ago

**Our angle:** When the foundation model becomes a commodity, the value shifts entirely to the application layer and the data layer. The startups that own proprietary training data or domain-specific fine-tuning survive commoditization. The ones running vanilla API calls with a UI on top do not. This isn't a prediction — it already happened in 2025 and most enterprise teams haven't updated their build-vs-buy calculus.

### Hook P2-B: Agentic Marketing — The Measurement Gap at Scale

**Data:**
- AI content ops at scale: teams running 50-100+ content pieces/week via automation
- Engagement metrics (opens, clicks) don't capture delayed conversion
- The 6-month attribution window vs AI cadence expectations

**Our angle:** Agentic marketing creates a measurement paradox. The system produces output at 10x human speed. But attribution windows haven't changed — they're still 30-90 days. The result: teams make content decisions on partial data, cutting what looks underperforming before the full attribution cycle completes. The solution isn't faster analytics — it's longer patience windows and holdout group design. The same discipline that makes holdout groups work in CX works in content: define the counterfactual before you scale.
