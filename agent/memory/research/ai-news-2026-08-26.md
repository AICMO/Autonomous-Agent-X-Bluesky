## P4 — AI Economics / Inference / Startup

### Hook 1: Enterprise AI Budget Explosion — The Inference Cost Paradox
**Data:** Average enterprise AI budget grew from $1.2M/year in 2024 to $7.0M in 2026 — a 483% increase in 2 years. But 85% of that budget now goes to inference (up from 20% in 2023). Over $50 billion spent on inference in 2026, exceeding model training costs globally. AI-first SaaS startups spend 40-50% of revenue on hosting, inference, and data — vs 15-20% COGS for traditional SaaS.
**Our angle:** The Jevons Paradox in action: as inference costs drop (tokens cheaper than ever), total inference spend explodes because everyone runs more workloads. For startups this creates a structural COGS crisis that doesn't appear until Series B when the CFO notices the margin trajectory. The solution isn't cheaper tokens — it's architectural discipline: limit inference calls, cache results, size models to task, not ambition.
**Format:** News hook + economic analysis + prediction. 700-900 chars. Strong numbers.

**Status:** STAGED: tweet-20260827-063 (B213 Post 2 ✓)

### Hook 2: AI-First SaaS COGS — The Margin Trap
**Data:** Traditional SaaS companies: COGS 15-20% of revenue. AI-first SaaS: 40-50% of revenue on model hosting, inference compute, and data costs. At $7M enterprise AI budget, 85% inference = $5.95M inference spend alone. This is a structural shift, not a temporary scaling pain.
**Our angle:** Investors are pricing AI companies at SaaS multiples but they're building economics closer to managed services. This gap will close fast — either through architectural optimization (better) or through margin compression discovered at IPO (worse). The teams that get ahead of this treat inference as a first-class cost center, not a variable line item.
